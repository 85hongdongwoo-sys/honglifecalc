Stamp Duty explained: how much you’ll pay on a £300k, £400k or £500k home – and when you pay nothing

Short answer: on a £300,000 home a home mover pays £5,000 in Stamp Duty, while a first-time buyer pays nothing. On £400,000 it’s £10,000 for a mover and £5,000 for a first-time buyer. If the home will be a second property, add 5% of the whole price – £20,000 more on £400,000.

If you’re buying a home in England or Northern Ireland, Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is usually the biggest cost after your deposit – and it catches a lot of people out because it isn’t one flat percentage. Like income tax, it works in slices: each part of the price is taxed at its own rate. The good news is that once you see the slices, it’s easy to check your own figure.

First-time buyers: do you really pay nothing?

Often, yes. If you’ve never owned a home anywhere in the world – including inherited shares of a property – and the price is £500,000 or less, you pay 0% on the first £300,000 and 5% only on the part between £300,000 and £500,000. Buying with a partner? You both need to be first-time buyers to get the relief; if one of you has owned before, the normal mover rates apply.

One thing that surprises people: there’s a cliff edge at £500,000. At £500,000 a first-time buyer pays £10,000; at £500,001 the relief disappears completely and the bill jumps to £15,000. If you’re negotiating near that price, that £1 matters.

Saving for your first home? The Lifetime ISA bonus can go towards the deposit on a home up to £450,000. From 29 October 2026 the withdrawal charge falls to 20% if you buy above that cap, and the government’s “Your First Home” equity loan opens for applications from February 2027. Second homes, buy-to-let and moving before you’ve sold

If buying the new place means you’ll own more than one home on completion day, the higher rates apply: an extra 5% on the whole price. On a £300,000 buy-to-let that’s £20,000 instead of £5,000.

The fairest twist is for movers who buy before their old home sells. You pay the higher rates at first, but if you sell your previous main home within three years, you can claim the extra back from HMRC. Keep the paperwork – the claim is made online and needs the completion dates of both sales.

When and how Stamp Duty is paid

  1. Your solicitor or conveyancer works out the figure and includes it in the completion statement.
  2. You send them the money before completion – it isn’t taken from the mortgage automatically, although some lenders let you borrow a little more to cover it.
  3. They file the SDLT return and pay HMRC within 14 days of completion. Late returns and late payments attract penalties and interest.

Buying in Scotland or Wales? Stamp Duty doesn’t apply there – Scotland has LBTT and Wales has Land Transaction Tax, with their own bands. Use the LBTT calculator for Scotland.

Questions people ask about Stamp Duty

How much Stamp Duty will I pay on a £300k house?
If you’re a first-time buyer, nothing at all. If you’re moving home, it’s £5,000: 2% on £125,000 to £250,000 (£2,500) plus 5% on the last £50,000 (£2,500). As a second home or buy-to-let it would be £20,000.
Do I pay Stamp Duty when I sell a house?
No – Stamp Duty is only paid by the buyer. When you sell, your costs are things like estate agent and solicitor fees, and possibly Capital Gains Tax if it isn’t your main home.
Can I add Stamp Duty to my mortgage?
Not directly – it’s due in cash within 14 days of completion. Some people borrow a bit more (a bigger loan-to-value) to free up savings for it, but that means paying interest on it for years, so it’s worth comparing the total cost first with the mortgage calculator.
Do I pay Stamp Duty on an inherited property?
No. Inheriting a home isn’t a purchase, so there’s no Stamp Duty – though Inheritance Tax may apply to the estate. Bear in mind that owning an inherited share of a home can stop you counting as a first-time buyer later, and can trigger the 5% higher rates if you then buy another home.
Do you pay Stamp Duty on a new build or shared ownership?
Yes on a new build – the same rates apply. With shared ownership you can choose to pay Stamp Duty on the full market value up front, or only on the share you buy (and again later if you staircase above 80%); first-time buyer relief can apply in either case.
Is Stamp Duty changing or being scrapped?
Not for now. The bands changed on 1 April 2025, and the Budget on 28 October 2026 left them as they are. The one recent tweak is that from 1 May 2026, rent paid under assured tenancies in England no longer counts for SDLT. We update this page whenever rates change.
Is Stamp Duty refundable?
Only in specific cases – the most common is when you paid the 5% higher rates and then sell your previous main home within three years. You can also amend the return within 12 months if it was calculated wrongly.
