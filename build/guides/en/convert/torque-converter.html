N·m, lb·ft and kgf·m

Torque units: converting N·m, lb·ft and kgf·m for torque wrenches and engine specs

Short answer: 1 lb·ft = 1.3558 N·m, 1 N·m = 0.7376 lb·ft, 1 kgf·m = 9.807 N·m and 1 lb·in = 0.113 N·m. So 100 N·m is 73.8 lb·ft, 10.2 kgf·m or 885 lb·in.

Torque is force times the length of the lever. You meet it in two places: tightening specifications (wheel nuts, spark plugs, bike parts) and engine data sheets. A US manual gives lb·ft, a European one N·m, and older Korean or Japanese brochures kgf·m – the converter lines them up so you set the wrench right.

Note: this page converts units of torque. A car’s “torque converter” is a transmission part, which is a different thing.

Torque, rpm and horsepower

Engine power comes from torque and speed: hp = lb·ft × rpm ÷ 5,252, or kW = N·m × rpm ÷ 9,549. That’s why torque and horsepower curves always cross at 5,252 rpm on a lb·ft/hp chart.

Questions people ask

How do I convert N·m to ft·lbs?
Multiply by 0.7376. A 120 N·m wheel-nut spec is about 88.5 lb·ft.
Is lb·ft the same as ft·lb?
For torque, people use both to mean the same thing. Strictly, ft·lb is often used for energy (work) and lb·ft for torque, but the number is identical.
My torque wrench is in lb·in – how do I set it?
Multiply lb·ft by 12, or N·m by 8.851. A 10 N·m spec is about 88.5 lb·in.
What does “27 kgf·m” in an old brochure mean?
It’s the engine’s peak torque in kilogram-force metres: 27 × 9.807 ≈ 265 N·m, or about 195 lb·ft.
