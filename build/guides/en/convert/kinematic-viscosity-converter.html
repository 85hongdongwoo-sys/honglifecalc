Stokes and centistokes

Kinematic viscosity: cSt, St and m²/s, oil grades, and converting to dynamic viscosity

Short answer: 1 stoke (St) = 100 centistokes (cSt) = 0.0001 m²/s, and 1 cSt = 1 mm²/s. Water at 20 °C is about 1.0 cSt. To get dynamic viscosity in mPa·s (centipoise), multiply cSt by the fluid’s density in g/cm³.

Kinematic viscosity tells you how quickly a fluid flows under its own weight – it’s what a lab measures by timing oil through a glass capillary. Oil grades, hydraulic fluid specs and fuel standards all quote it, almost always in centistokes at a stated temperature.

What typical numbers look like

FluidKinematic viscosity
Water at 20 °C1.00 cSt
Water at 40 °C0.66 cSt
ISO VG 46 hydraulic oil at 40 °C41.4–50.6 cSt
SAE 30-grade engine oil at 100 °C (e.g. 5W-30)9.3 to under 12.5 cSt
SAE 40-grade engine oil at 100 °C (e.g. 10W-40)12.5 to under 16.3 cSt

Oil ranges are the grade limits in ISO 3448 and SAE J300.

Kinematic vs dynamic viscosity

Dynamic (absolute) viscosity is the fluid’s internal resistance to shear, measured in pascal-seconds or centipoise. Kinematic viscosity divides that by density. So dynamic (mPa·s) = kinematic (cSt) × density (g/cm³). An oil of 46 cSt with a density of 0.87 g/cm³ has a dynamic viscosity of about 40 mPa·s.

Questions people ask

What is the kinematic viscosity of water?
About 1.004 cSt (1.004 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s) at 20 °C, falling to about 0.66 cSt at 40 °C and 0.29 cSt at 100 °C.
Can I convert viscosity to a different temperature with this page?
No – temperature changes the fluid itself, not the unit. Oils thin out sharply as they warm. If you know the viscosity at two temperatures (usually 40 °C and 100 °C), the ASTM D341 chart method estimates values in between.
Why do oil specs use both 40 °C and 100 °C?
40 °C stands for a machine at normal running temperature and 100 °C for a hot engine. The ratio between the two gives the viscosity index – a higher index means the oil changes less with temperature.
How many cSt are in 1 ft²/s?
About 92,903 cSt, because one square foot is 92,903 mm².
