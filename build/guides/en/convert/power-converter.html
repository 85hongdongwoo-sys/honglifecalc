Watts, kilowatts and BTU/h

Power units: watts to BTU per hour, horsepower, air-conditioner tons, and travel voltage

Short answer: 1 kW = 1,000 W = 3,412 BTU/h = 860 kcal/h. A 12,000 BTU/h air conditioner is about 3.5 kW of cooling. For horsepower, 1 mechanical hp = 745.7 W and 1 metric hp (PS) = 735.5 W.

Power is energy per second. Appliance plates show watts, heating and cooling equipment in many countries shows BTU per hour or kcal per hour, and engines show horsepower. Converting them puts heaters, air conditioners and generators side by side.

“Power converter” for travel: check the voltage, not just the plug

If you came here because of a trip: a travel power converter changes voltage, which is different from converting units. US and Canada use 120 V, Mexico 127 V, Japan 100 V, Korea 220 V, and most of Europe, the UK, Australia, India and Thailand 220–240 V. Look at your device’s label: if it says INPUT 100–240 V (most phone and laptop chargers), you only need a plug adapter. Single-voltage items like some hair dryers need a real voltage converter rated above their wattage.

Questions people ask

How many watts is 1 horsepower?
745.7 W for mechanical (imperial) horsepower and 735.5 W for metric horsepower (PS, CV). This converter covers W, kW, MW, GW, BTU/h and kcal/h.
How do I convert BTU to kW for an air conditioner?
Divide BTU per hour by 3,412. A 9,000 BTU/h unit is about 2.6 kW of cooling.
What’s the difference between kW and kWh?
kW is a rate; kWh is an amount. A 2 kW heater running for 3 hours uses 6 kWh. Use the energy converter for kWh, joules and calories.
Do I need a power converter in Japan or Mexico?
For dual-voltage chargers, no – both countries use the same flat two-pin plug as the US, and the chargers accept 100 V or 127 V. Single-voltage 230 V appliances from Europe or Asia would need a step-up converter there.
