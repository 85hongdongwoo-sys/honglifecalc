Newtons, kgf and pound-force

Force units: newtons vs kilogram-force vs pound-force, with load-rating examples

Short answer: 1 kgf = 9.80665 N, 1 lbf = 4.44822 N, 1 kN ≈ 102 kgf ≈ 225 lbf, and 1 dyne = 0.00001 N. A climbing carabiner rated 24 kN can hold a static load of about 2,450 kgf.

The newton is the SI unit of force: the force that speeds up 1 kg by 1 m/s every second. Kilogram-force and pound-force are “gravity” units – the weight of 1 kg or 1 lb under standard gravity – so they feel familiar but aren’t masses. Equipment ratings, spring specs and older manuals mix all of them, which is where the converter helps.

Questions people ask

Is kilogram-force the same as kilogram?
No. A kilogram is mass; kilogram-force is the weight of that mass under standard gravity (9.80665 m/s²). On Earth the numbers match, which is why scales show kg, but on the Moon 1 kg weighs only about 0.17 kgf.
How do I convert newtons to kg?
Divide by 9.81 to get the mass whose weight equals that force: 1,000 N ≈ 102 kg. This only makes sense for weight under Earth gravity.
What is a tonne-force?
The weight of 1,000 kg: 9,806.65 N, or about 9.8 kN. Press and jack capacities are often given in tonnes meaning tonne-force.
How is force related to pressure and torque?
Pressure is force divided by area (1 N per m² is 1 pascal); torque is force times the lever length. See the torque converter for N·m and lb·ft.
