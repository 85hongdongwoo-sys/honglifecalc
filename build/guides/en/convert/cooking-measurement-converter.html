Cups, spoons and grams

Cooking measurements: why a “cup” isn’t the same everywhere, and how to turn cups into grams

Short answer: a US cup is 236.6 mL, a metric cup (UK, Australia, Canada, NZ) is 250 mL and a Korean or Japanese cup is 200 mL. 1 US tablespoon = 3 teaspoons = 14.8 mL, and 16 tablespoons make a US cup. To get grams you need the ingredient: a US cup of water weighs about 237 g, but a cup of flour only about 120 g.

Most recipe mistakes come from two things: a cup from one country used with a recipe from another, and dry ingredients measured by volume when the recipe writer weighed them. The converter above handles the first problem exactly. For the second, the table below gives typical weights you can trust for everyday baking.

Spoon sizes that trip people up

  • Australian tablespoon = 20 mL (4 teaspoons), not 15 mL. In Australian recipes, a “tablespoon” of baking powder is a third more than elsewhere.
  • US and metric teaspoons are almost the same (4.93 vs 5 mL) – the difference never matters at home.
  • “Heaped” and “level” spoons can differ by half again, so level off with a knife unless the recipe says otherwise.

Questions people ask

How do I convert cups to grams?
There’s no single factor, because grams measure weight and cups measure volume. Multiply the volume by the ingredient’s density – or use the table above: about 120 g for flour, 200 g for sugar and 227 g for butter per US cup. For water-like liquids, millilitres and grams are practically the same, so the converter’s mL result is also the weight in grams.
Is a UK cup the same as a US cup?
Modern UK recipes that use cups mean the 250 mL metric cup, which is about 13 mL bigger than a US cup. Older British recipes sometimes used a 284 mL “half pint” cup, so if a vintage recipe turns out dry, that’s a likely reason.
How many tablespoons are in a cup?
16 US tablespoons make a US cup, and 1/4 cup is 4 tablespoons. With metric spoons, a 250 mL cup is about 16.7 tablespoons of 15 mL.
How much is a fluid ounce in millilitres?
A US fluid ounce is 29.57 mL, so a US cup is 8 fl oz. The UK imperial fluid ounce is slightly smaller, 28.41 mL.
Unit sizes: US customary (NIST Handbook 44), metric cup 250 mL, Australian tablespoon 20 mL (Standards Australia). Ingredient weights are typical averages from published baking weight charts; checked 28 Nov 2026.
