Litres, gallons and cubic feet

Volume conversion: litres to gallons and cubic feet, tank sizes, and turning volume into weight

Short answer: 1 US gallon = 3.785 L, 1 cubic foot = 28.32 L, 1 m³ = 1,000 L = 264.2 US gallons, and 1 L = 1,000 mL = 1,000 cc. A 60 × 30 × 36 cm tank holds 64.8 L.

Volume shows up everywhere: fuel and drinks in litres or gallons, engines in cc, fridges and moving trucks in cubic feet, concrete and water bills in cubic metres. The converter covers all of these. Below are the checks people usually need next.

Volume of a box-shaped tank

  1. Measure inside length, width and height in centimetres.
  2. Multiply them: 60 × 30 × 36 = 64,800 cm³.
  3. Divide by 1,000 for litres: 64.8 L (17.1 US gallons). In practice, gravel, decor and the gap at the top cut usable water by about 10–15%.

Questions people ask

How many litres are in a gallon?
3.785 L in a US gallon and 4.546 L in a UK (imperial) gallon. This converter uses US gallons; for imperial gallons multiply the litre result by 0.22.
How do I convert volume to kg?
Multiply the volume by the density. For water, litres equal kilograms almost exactly; for other liquids use the table above or the product’s data sheet.
What does a 2.0-litre engine mean?
The total volume swept by its pistons, usually just under 2,000 cc (often 1,998 cc). 1 cc is exactly 1 mL.
How many cubic feet is a cubic metre?
35.31 ft³. Moving and storage companies often quote in cubic feet; a 10 m³ load is about 353 ft³.
