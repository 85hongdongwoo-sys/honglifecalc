Macros · protein, carbs and fat

How to split your calories into protein, carbs and fat – example macro plans for 1,800, 2,200 and 2,600 calories

Short answer: a balanced, easy-to-follow split is protein 1.6 g per kg of body weight, fat about 30% of calories, and carbs for the rest. For 2,200 kcal at 75 kg that’s about 120 g protein, 73 g fat and 266 g carbs a day. Each gram of protein or carbs is 4 kcal; each gram of fat is 9 kcal.

Counting macros sounds technical, but it’s just a way of making your calories work for your goal. Protein protects and builds muscle, fat supports hormones and keeps meals satisfying, and carbs fuel training and daily life. Once protein and fat are set, carbs simply fill the gap.

Adjusting for your goal

  • Fat loss: keep protein at 1.6–2.2 g/kg, fat at 25–30%, and let carbs come down as calories come down.
  • Muscle gain: protein about 1.6 g/kg is plenty; add the extra calories mainly from carbs to fuel training.
  • Endurance sport: carbs often need to be higher – 5–7 g per kg on heavy training days.
  • Low-carb preference: fine if it suits you; keep fat from mostly unsaturated sources and fibre from vegetables, nuts and seeds.

Questions people ask about macros

What is the best macro ratio for weight loss?
There isn’t one magic ratio – studies comparing diets find similar fat loss when calories and protein are matched. Pick a split you can stick to, with protein around 1.6 g/kg, and keep calories below your TDEE.
How do I count macros without weighing everything?
Use your hand as a guide: a palm of protein, a cupped hand of carbs, a thumb of fat and a fist of vegetables per meal is a rough but workable start. Weigh food for a week or two to calibrate, then estimate.
Do I have to hit my macros exactly every day?
No. Getting within about 10 g of protein and staying near your calorie target is plenty. Weekly averages matter more than any single day.
What is a 40/30/30 macro split?
40% of calories from carbs, 30% from protein and 30% from fat. At 2,000 kcal that’s 200 g carbs, 150 g protein and about 67 g fat – a higher-protein plan popular for fat loss.
Does alcohol count as a macro?
It’s sometimes called the fourth macro: 7 kcal per gram with no useful nutrients. If you drink, count it in your calories, usually by taking it from your carb or fat allowance.
