Protein · how much is enough

How much protein do I need a day? Grams by body weight, goal and age – and what that looks like on a plate

Short answer: the basic recommendation for adults is 0.8 g per kg of body weight – about 56 g a day for a 70 kg person. If you lift weights or want to keep muscle while losing fat, 1.6 g per kg (around 110 g at 70 kg) is a well-supported target. Adults over 65 generally do better with at least 1.0–1.2 g per kg.

Protein advice swings from “you’re eating too much” to “you need a shake after every meal”. The truth is in between, and it depends mostly on two things: your body weight and what you’re asking your body to do. Here’s a simple guide, plus everyday foods so you can picture the grams.

If you carry a lot of body fat, base the numbers on a healthy target weight rather than your current weight.

What 30 g of protein looks like

  • A cooked chicken breast (about 100 g) – roughly 30 g
  • A 100 g can of tuna – about 25 g
  • Four large eggs – about 25 g
  • A 170 g pot of plain Greek yoghurt plus a glass (250 ml) of milk – about 25 g
  • A large bowl of cooked lentils (about 300 g) – about 27 g
  • 200 g of firm tofu – about 25–30 g, depending on the brand

Spreading protein across the day – roughly 20–40 g at each meal – seems to work better for muscle than eating most of it at dinner.

Questions people ask about protein

How much protein do I need to build muscle?
Around 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day captures most of the benefit for people doing regular strength training; going up to about 2.2 g/kg may help a little more for some. Training consistently matters far more than the exact number.
How much protein should I eat to lose weight?
Aim for the higher end – about 1.2–1.6 g/kg. Protein keeps you fuller and helps you hold on to muscle while eating less, so more of the weight you lose comes from fat.
Can you eat too much protein?
For healthy people, intakes up to about 2 g/kg appear safe. People with kidney disease are usually advised to limit protein, so check with your doctor if that applies to you.
Do I need protein shakes?
No – they’re just a convenient food. If you reach your target with meals, a shake adds nothing extra. They can help on busy days or if you don’t eat much meat, fish, eggs or dairy.
Can vegetarians and vegans get enough protein?
Yes. Combine beans, lentils, tofu, tempeh, seitan, soya milk, nuts and whole grains through the day. Plant proteins are slightly less concentrated, so aiming about 10% higher than the table is a sensible margin.
