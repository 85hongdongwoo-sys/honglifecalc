Ideal weight · what the numbers mean

What should I weigh for my height? Ideal weight charts for men and women, and why a range beats a single number

Short answer: for a man of 175 cm (5′9″), the classic formulas give about 69–72 kg (152–159 lb), and a healthy BMI range is 57–76 kg. For a woman of 165 cm (5′5″), the formulas give about 56–60 kg (123–132 lb), with a healthy BMI range of 50–68 kg. The range is a better target than any single “ideal” figure.

“Ideal body weight” formulas were created in the 1960s–80s mainly to help doctors dose medicines, not to tell people what they should weigh. They’re still a handy reference point, but they don’t know whether you’re muscular, broad-shouldered or small-framed. That’s why this guide shows both the formulas and the healthy BMI range side by side.

“Formulas” is the spread of the Devine, Robinson, Miller and Hamwi equations. Healthy BMI range is 18.5–24.9 for adults; some guidelines use lower cut-offs for people of Asian heritage (around 23).

Why the formulas disagree

Each formula was fitted to different data, so they drift apart at the extremes – at 190 cm the male estimates range from 77 to 88 kg. None of them accounts for muscle, so a fit, muscular person can sit well above their “ideal” weight and be perfectly healthy. Waist size is often a better second check: for most adults, a waist under half your height is a good sign.

Questions people ask about ideal weight

What is the ideal weight for a 5′6″ woman?
The formulas give about 59–61 kg (129–135 lb), and a healthy BMI range is roughly 52–70 kg (115–154 lb). Anywhere in that wider range can be healthy, depending on your build and fitness.
What is the ideal weight for a 6 ft man?
At 183 cm, the formulas give about 73–80 kg (161–176 lb), and the healthy BMI range is about 62–83 kg (137–184 lb).
Is ideal body weight the same as a healthy weight?
Not exactly. Ideal body weight is a single estimate from a formula; a healthy weight is a range, usually defined by BMI 18.5–24.9 plus other signs such as waist size, blood pressure and fitness.
Does ideal weight change with age?
The formulas don’t change, but for adults over about 65, many doctors consider a slightly higher weight (BMI up to around 27) to be protective. Losing muscle with age matters more than a few kilos on the scale.
I’m above my ideal weight but I lift weights – should I worry?
Probably not. Muscle is denser than fat, so strength-trained people often weigh more than formulas suggest. Waist-to-height ratio or a body fat estimate will tell you more – try our body fat calculator.
