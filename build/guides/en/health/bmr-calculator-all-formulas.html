BMR · the calories you burn at rest

What is a normal BMR? Typical values for men and women, which formula to trust, and how to use BMR without overthinking it

Short answer: a 35-year-old man of 80 kg and 178 cm has a BMR of about 1,740–1,820 kcal a day depending on the formula; a woman of 65 kg and 165 cm is about 1,350–1,410 kcal. BMR is only the energy for staying alive at rest – your real daily needs (TDEE) are usually 1.2 to 1.9 times higher.

Your basal metabolic rate is the energy your body uses for breathing, circulation, keeping warm and repairing cells while you lie completely still. It’s the biggest slice of the calories you burn each day – usually 60–70% – which is why it’s the starting point for every diet plan. The calculator above shows several published formulas side by side; this guide explains how they differ and what to do with the number.

What changes your BMR

  • Size and muscle: bigger bodies and more muscle burn more at rest. This is the main reason men’s BMR is usually higher.
  • Age: BMR drifts down slowly with age, largely because many people lose muscle.
  • Dieting: long, steep calorie cuts make the body economise slightly, beyond what the weight loss alone explains.
  • Thyroid and some medicines can push BMR up or down – one reason to see a doctor if your weight changes unexpectedly.

Questions people ask about BMR

Should I eat my BMR to lose weight?
Usually not. Eating at your BMR means eating well below what you actually burn, which can feel hard and cost muscle. Most people do better eating about 300–500 kcal below their TDEE, which is often still above their BMR.
What’s the difference between BMR and RMR?
BMR is measured under strict conditions (after a night’s sleep, fasting, lying still); resting metabolic rate (RMR) is measured more casually and is usually a few percent higher. For everyday use, they’re interchangeable.
How can I increase my BMR?
Building muscle through strength training is the most reliable way, though each kilogram of muscle adds only a modest amount at rest. Staying active, eating enough protein and avoiding crash diets all help keep your metabolism from slowing.
Is a low BMR bad?
A low BMR usually just reflects a smaller or older body – it isn’t a disease. If your BMR estimate is normal but you’re gaining weight unexpectedly, feel cold or tired all the time, a doctor can check things like thyroid function.
How accurate are BMR calculators?
For most people, within about 10%. Only a lab test (indirect calorimetry) measures it directly. The practical approach: use the estimate, track your weight for a few weeks, and adjust.
