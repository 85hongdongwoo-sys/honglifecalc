Hydration, without the myths

How much water should I drink a day? Real guidelines for men and women, by body weight, and on hot or active days

Short answer: European guidelines suggest about 2.0 litres a day for women and 2.5 litres for men from all drinks and food – roughly 1.6 and 2.0 litres from drinks alone, or about 6–8 glasses. You need more in hot weather, when you exercise, and while pregnant or breastfeeding.

“Eight glasses a day” is easy to remember, but it isn’t based on a specific study, and your needs depend on your size, the weather and how much you move. Food also counts: fruit, vegetables, soup and yoghurt provide about a fifth of the water most people take in. Here’s what the official guidelines actually say, and how to tell whether you’re drinking enough.

“Total water” includes water from food. The US figures are higher because they’re based on typical intakes of healthy people rather than a minimum need.

A simple body-weight rule

Many dietitians use 30–35 ml per kg of body weight as a daily starting point for total fluids:

When to drink more

  • Exercise: add roughly 0.4–0.8 L for each hour of sweaty activity, more in heat.
  • Hot weather or a heated office: sweat losses rise even when you don’t notice them.
  • Pregnancy (about +0.3 L a day) and breastfeeding (about +0.7 L a day), according to EFSA.
  • Illness with fever, vomiting or diarrhoea – oral rehydration solutions replace salts as well as water.
The easiest check is your urine: pale straw-coloured means you’re well hydrated; dark yellow means drink more. Clear all day can mean you’re drinking more than you need.

Questions people ask about drinking water

Do tea and coffee count towards my water intake?
Yes. The mild diuretic effect of caffeine is small for people who drink it regularly, so tea, coffee, milk and other drinks all count. Water is still the best everyday choice because it has no sugar or calories.
Is it possible to drink too much water?
Yes, though it’s rare. Drinking several litres in a short time – for example during a long race – can dilute the salt in your blood (hyponatraemia). Drink to thirst during endurance events rather than forcing water down.
Does drinking water help with weight loss?
It can help a little: a glass before meals may make you feel fuller, and swapping sugary drinks for water cuts calories. It doesn’t burn fat by itself.
How much water should I drink when I exercise?
Drink normally during the day, have a glass or two in the couple of hours before training, and sip during long or hot sessions. Weighing yourself before and after a workout is a good guide: each kilogram lost is about a litre of fluid to replace.
Is 3 litres of water a day too much?
For most healthy adults, no – especially if you’re active, larger or in a hot climate. People with heart or kidney conditions may be told to limit fluids, so follow your doctor’s advice if that applies to you.
