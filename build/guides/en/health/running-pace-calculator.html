What pace do I need? Finish times for 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon – plus how to pace race day

Short answer: running at 6:00 per km (9:39 per mile) gets you a 30-minute 5K, a 1-hour 10K, a 2:06 half marathon and a 4:13 marathon. To break 2 hours in the half you need about 5:41 per km; for a sub-4 marathon, about 5:41 per km too – but holding it for twice as long.

Pace is the number runners live by: minutes per kilometre or per mile. It tells you whether a goal time is realistic, how fast your easy runs should be, and how to avoid the classic mistake of starting a race too quickly. How to pace a race (and enjoy it)

The most common mistake is running the first kilometres too fast because you feel fresh and the crowd is buzzing. Even or slightly negative splits – the second half a touch faster than the first – almost always give a better finish time. A simple plan: run the first quarter 5–10 seconds per km slower than goal pace, settle into goal pace through the middle, and use whatever you have left for the final stretch.

Most of your training should feel easy: many coaches suggest running easy days 60–90 seconds per km slower than your 10K race pace. It feels too slow at first, but it’s what builds the endurance that makes race pace possible.

Questions people ask about running pace

What is a good running pace for a beginner?
Anything that lets you finish comfortably! Many new runners start around 6:30–8:00 per km (10:30–13:00 per mile), often with walking breaks. If you can talk in full sentences while running, you’re at a good easy pace.
How do I convert pace per km to pace per mile?
Multiply the pace by 1.609. A 5:00/km pace is 5 × 1.609 = 8.05 minutes, or 8:03 per mile. To go the other way, divide by 1.609.
What pace do I need to run a 10K in under an hour?
Faster than 6:00 per km, or 9:39 per mile. Aim for about 5:55 per km to leave a small buffer for the start and water stations.
Why does my watch show a different distance from the race?
GPS watches often read a little long in races because you weave around people and don’t run the exact shortest line that courses are measured on. Expect your watch to show 0.5–2% more – so set your target pace slightly faster than the chart.
How much faster is race pace than training pace?
Easy runs are usually 60–90 seconds per km slower than 10K race pace, and long runs a little slower again. Only a small part of training – intervals and tempo runs – should be at or faster than race pace.
