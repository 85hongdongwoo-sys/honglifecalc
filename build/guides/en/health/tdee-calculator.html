TDEE guide · calories in real life

What your TDEE really means – typical numbers, how to use them for fat loss or muscle gain, and why the scale doesn’t always agree

Short answer: a 35-year-old man of 80 kg and 178 cm who trains 3–5 times a week burns about 2,700 kcal a day; a woman of 65 kg and 165 cm with the same routine burns about 2,090 kcal. To lose roughly half a kilo a week, eat about 500 kcal less than your TDEE; to gain muscle slowly, eat 200–300 kcal more.

Most people come to a TDEE calculator with one real question: how much should I eat? Your TDEE is the honest starting point – the number of calories that would keep your weight exactly where it is. Everything else (cutting, bulking, maintaining) is just a step up or down from there. Below are typical figures so you can sanity-check your own result, and the practical questions people ask once they have their number.

Turning your TDEE into a daily target

  • Fat loss: TDEE minus 300–500 kcal. Keep protein high (around 1.6 g per kg of body weight) so most of what you lose is fat.
  • Maintenance: eat at your TDEE and watch the weekly average weight – that tells you more than any formula.
  • Muscle gain: TDEE plus 200–300 kcal, together with progressive strength training. Gaining faster mostly adds fat.

Why the scale might not match the maths

It’s completely normal for the first week to look “wrong”. Water and stored carbohydrate can move your weight 1–2 kg in either direction, which hides the fat change underneath. Give it two or three weeks, compare weekly averages, and only then nudge your intake by 100–200 kcal. If you’re eating at the target and nothing has changed after a month, your real TDEE is simply a bit lower (or higher) than the estimate – that’s useful information, not failure.

Questions people ask about TDEE

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?
Start around 500 kcal below your TDEE – that’s roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) a week for most people. If your TDEE is 2,000 kcal, aim for about 1,500. If that feels very hard, 300 kcal below still works; it just takes a little longer.
Should I eat less on rest days?
You can, but you don’t have to. Your TDEE is a daily average across the week, so eating the same amount every day works just as well and is easier to plan. What matters is the weekly total.
Do I add the calories my watch says I burned?
Not if you already chose an activity level that includes your workouts – that would count them twice. Fitness trackers also tend to overestimate exercise calories, so if you do eat some back, eat about half.
Why is my TDEE lower than my friend’s?
Body size, muscle, age and everyday movement all play a part. A taller, heavier or more active person simply burns more. Steps matter more than people think: someone who walks 10,000 steps a day can burn several hundred calories more than a desk worker of the same size.
Does TDEE go down with age?
A little – the formula takes off about 5 kcal a day for each extra year, and many people also move less and lose muscle as they get older. Strength training and staying active slow that down more than any diet trick.
