Tirzepatide guide · Mounjaro & Zepbound

Mounjaro dosing explained – the step-up schedule, missed doses, how much weight people lose, and the safety points worth knowing

Short answer: tirzepatide is started at 2.5 mg once a week for 4 weeks, then usually raised to 5 mg. After that, the dose can go up by 2.5 mg at a time, no sooner than every 4 weeks, to a maximum of 15 mg. In the SURMOUNT-1 trial, people without diabetes lost an average of 15% (5 mg), 19.5% (10 mg) and 20.9% (15 mg) of their body weight over 72 weeks.

Starting a weekly injection comes with a lot of small questions: am I on the right step, what if I’m late with a dose, is my weight loss “normal”? This page answers them using the official prescribing information and the main clinical trial. It’s meant to help you understand your treatment and have better conversations with your doctor – not to replace them.

“Earliest” assumes every step-up happens at the minimum 4 weeks. Many people stay longer on a step – or stay on a lower dose for good – if it works well or side effects need time to settle. For weight management (Zepbound), the label lists 5, 10 and 15 mg as maintenance doses.

Missed a dose or need to change your injection day?

  • Within 4 days (96 hours) of the missed dose: take it as soon as you remember, then go back to your usual day.
  • More than 4 days late: skip it and take the next dose on your usual day. Don’t take two doses to catch up.
  • Changing your injection day is fine as long as there are at least 3 days (72 hours) between two doses.
  • If you’ve missed several doses in a row, talk to your prescriber before restarting – they may want you to go back to a lower dose to limit stomach side effects.

These are averages over a year and a half. Weight usually comes off fastest in the first months and slows later, and individual results vary widely – some people lose much more, some less. Losing less than the average early on doesn’t mean the medicine isn’t working; your prescriber looks at the trend over months, along with blood sugar, blood pressure and how you feel.

Measuring correctly: mg, mL and syringe units

Branded single-dose pens and the KwikPen deliver a fixed dose – there’s nothing to measure. Measuring only applies to vials drawn up with a syringe. The dose is in milligrams (mg), the liquid in millilitres (mL), and an insulin syringe is marked in units (on a U-100 syringe, 100 units = 1 mL). Mixing these up is the most common cause of dosing errors, and the US FDA has warned about overdoses with compounded GLP-1 injections (semaglutide) for exactly this reason. Always check the volume against your pharmacy label, and ask the pharmacist to show you the mark on your syringe.

Side effects and safety points

  • The most common side effects are nausea, diarrhoea, reduced appetite, vomiting, constipation, indigestion and stomach pain, mostly when starting or increasing the dose. Eating smaller meals and stopping when full often helps.
  • Seek medical help for severe stomach pain that won’t go away (possible pancreatitis), signs of gallbladder problems, a lump or swelling in the neck, trouble swallowing, or signs of a serious allergic reaction.
  • Tirzepatide must not be used by people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2.
  • It can make birth control pills less reliable for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase – the label advises adding a barrier method or switching to a non-oral method during those weeks.
  • Tell your surgeon or anaesthetist you’re taking it before any procedure that needs sedation.

Questions people ask about Mounjaro dosing

How long should I stay on 2.5 mg?
The label says 4 weeks. 2.5 mg is a starting dose to help your stomach adjust, not a long-term treatment dose, so most people move to 5 mg after the first month unless their prescriber decides otherwise.
When should I increase my Mounjaro dose?
Only when your prescriber says so, and no sooner than 4 weeks on your current dose. Many people stay on a dose longer if they’re still losing weight or reaching their blood sugar goals, or if side effects need time to settle.
What’s the difference between Mounjaro and Zepbound?
They contain the same medicine, tirzepatide, at the same doses. In the US, Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes, and Zepbound for weight reduction (alongside diet and activity) and for obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity. In many other countries, including the UK, the EU and Korea, Mounjaro is approved for both diabetes and weight management.
How much weight can I lose in a month on Mounjaro?
It varies a lot. Over the whole SURMOUNT-1 trial, average loss on 10–15 mg was about 20% over 72 weeks – roughly 1% of body weight a month on average, often faster at the start and slower later. Talk to your prescriber if you’re losing weight much faster than that or feel unwell.
Where should I inject it?
Under the skin of the stomach area (at least 5 cm from the belly button), the front of the thigh, or the back of the upper arm (usually with help). Rotate the spot each week.
What happens if I stop taking it?
In a follow-up study (SURMOUNT-4), people who switched to placebo after 36 weeks regained a good part of the weight over the following year, while those who continued kept losing. Weight management is usually treated as long-term care – discuss any plan to stop with your prescriber.
