Due date guide · weeks, trimesters & what to expect

How your due date is worked out – and why only a few babies arrive on the day

Short answer: your estimated due date is 280 days (40 weeks) after the first day of your last period. If your last period started on 1 March, your due date is around 6 December. Only about 4–5% of babies are born on that exact day – most arrive somewhere between 37 and 42 weeks.

Seeing a due date for the first time is a big moment. It helps to know that it’s an estimate, a midpoint rather than a deadline. Pregnancy is counted from the first day of your last period, about two weeks before conception actually happens, which is why you’re already “4 weeks pregnant” when a test first turns positive.

Key weeks at a glance

WeekWhat it means
4–5A home pregnancy test is usually positive by the time a period is missed.
8–14First scan; measuring the baby now gives the most accurate due date.
13 + 6End of the first trimester.
18–21Mid-pregnancy (anomaly) scan in many countries.
28Third trimester begins.
37 + 0Early term – babies born from now are no longer premature.
39 + 0 to 40 + 6Full term – the due date sits in the middle of this window.
41 + 0Late term; your midwife or doctor will usually talk about options.
42 + 0Post-term.

Term definitions follow the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. Local scan schedules vary by country and provider.

When the calculator’s date might change

  • After your dating scan. If the scan measurement differs from your period date by more than about a week in the first trimester, your team usually goes by the scan.
  • Longer or shorter cycles. The 280-day rule assumes a 28-day cycle. With a 35-day cycle, ovulation is likely a week later, so the due date moves about a week later too.
  • IVF pregnancies are dated from the embryo transfer: about 266 days after egg collection, which is 261 days after a day-5 transfer or 263 days after a day-3 transfer.

Questions people ask about due dates

How accurate is my due date?
It’s a good estimate but not a prediction of the day. Most babies are born within a week or two either side, and first babies are slightly more likely to come after the date than before it.
How many weeks pregnant am I?
Count from the first day of your last period. If that was 10 weeks and 3 days ago, you’re 10 + 3 weeks pregnant – even though conception happened about two weeks after that date.
When did I conceive?
Usually around two weeks after the start of your last period, so roughly 266 days before your due date. Sperm can live for several days, so the act of sex may have been a few days earlier.
Can my due date change at the scan?
Yes, and it’s common. An early scan measurement is the most reliable way to date a pregnancy, so it may move your date by a few days or more. Once set from an early scan, it isn’t normally changed again by later scans.
What if I don’t remember the date of my last period?
That’s fine – lots of people don’t. Your doctor or midwife will arrange a dating scan, which works out the due date from the baby’s size.
