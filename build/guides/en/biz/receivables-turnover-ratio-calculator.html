Receivables turnover & DSO

Accounts receivable turnover: the formula, how to turn it into days sales outstanding, and how to collect cash faster

Short answer: receivables turnover = net credit sales ÷ average accounts receivable. A business with $1.2 million of credit sales and receivables of $90,000 at the start of the year and $110,000 at the end (average $100,000) turns its receivables 12 times a year – customers take about 30 days to pay (365 ÷ 12).

Profit on paper doesn’t pay salaries – cash does. The receivables turnover ratio shows how quickly customers settle their invoices, which is why lenders and finance teams watch it closely.

DSO = 365 ÷ turnover. Compare it with your payment terms and your industry.

Ways to improve it

  • Invoice immediately and make payment easy (bank transfer details, card links).
  • Send polite reminders before and on the due date, not weeks after.
  • Check new customers’ credit before giving long terms.
  • Consider a small early-payment discount if your margin allows it.

Questions people ask

Is a higher receivables turnover better?
Usually – it means faster collection. But an extremely high ratio can mean your credit terms are so strict that you’re turning away good customers.
Should I use total sales or credit sales?
Credit sales are more accurate, because cash sales never create a receivable. If you only know total sales, the ratio will look better than it really is.
What’s a good DSO?
It depends on your terms and industry. As a rule of thumb, a DSO within about a third of your standard terms (around 40 days on net-30) is healthy.
