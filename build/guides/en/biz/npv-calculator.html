Net present value, in plain English

How to calculate NPV – a worked project example, what a positive or negative result means, and the Excel trap to avoid

Short answer: NPV adds up a project’s future cash flows in today’s money and subtracts what you invest. Spend $10,000 today to earn $3,000, $4,000, $4,000 and $3,000 over four years: at an 8% discount rate the NPV is about +$1,588, so the project adds value. At 15% it’s about −$21 – just below break-even.

A dollar next year is worth less than a dollar today, because today’s dollar could be invested in the meantime. Net present value turns every future cash flow into its value today using a discount rate – usually your cost of capital or the return you could get elsewhere – and then compares the total with the upfront cost.

Reading the result

  • NPV above zero: the project earns more than your required return – it creates value.
  • NPV of zero: it earns exactly your required return.
  • NPV below zero: you’d do better putting the money elsewhere at your discount rate.
  • When comparing projects of different sizes, the profitability index (PV of inflows ÷ investment) shows value per dollar invested.

Questions people ask about NPV

How do I calculate NPV in Excel?
Excel’s =NPV(rate, values) assumes the first value arrives one period from now. So enter the future cash flows in NPV and add the year-0 investment separately: =NPV(8%, B2:B5) + B1, where B1 is −10,000. Forgetting this is the most common NPV error in spreadsheets.
Does NPV include the initial investment?
Yes. Net present value is the present value of all inflows minus the initial outlay. Without subtracting the investment you only have “present value”.
Can NPV be negative?
Yes – it means the project’s returns don’t cover your required rate of return. A negative NPV doesn’t always mean the project loses money in plain cash terms; in the example above, it still returns $4,000 more than it costs at 15%, but not enough to beat a 15% hurdle.
What discount rate should I use?
Businesses usually use their weighted average cost of capital (WACC) or a hurdle rate set by management, sometimes higher for riskier projects. For personal decisions, use the return you could reasonably earn elsewhere at similar risk.
Does NPV include depreciation?
Not directly – NPV uses cash flows, and depreciation isn’t a cash payment. It matters only through its effect on taxes, which are a real cash flow.
