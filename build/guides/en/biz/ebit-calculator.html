EBIT and EBITDA in plain English

How to calculate EBIT – two methods, the difference between EBIT, EBITDA and net income, and what a good EBIT margin looks like

Short answer: EBIT is earnings before interest and taxes – roughly a company’s operating profit. Two ways to get it: revenue − cost of goods − operating expenses, or net income + interest + tax. A company with $1,000,000 in revenue, $600,000 cost of goods and $250,000 of operating costs has an EBIT of $150,000 (a 15% EBIT margin).

EBIT strips out how a company is financed (interest) and where it pays tax, so you can compare how well different businesses run their core operations. It’s a staple of company valuation, lending decisions and investor reports.

EBIT vs EBITDA vs net income

  • EBITDA adds back depreciation and amortisation as well. It’s popular for capital-heavy businesses, but it ignores the real cost of replacing equipment.
  • EBIT keeps depreciation in, so it’s a stricter measure of operating profit.
  • Net income is what’s left after interest and tax – the true bottom line for shareholders.

Questions people ask about EBIT

Is EBIT the same as operating income?
Often, yes. Operating income usually excludes non-operating items like investment gains or one-off asset sales, while some EBIT calculations include them – so the two can differ slightly in practice.
What is a good EBIT margin?
It depends on the industry. Grocery and retail may run at a few percent, while software and branded consumer goods can exceed 20%. Compare with similar companies and with past years.
Is EBITDA the same as gross profit?
No. Gross profit only subtracts the cost of goods sold. EBITDA also subtracts operating expenses such as salaries, rent and marketing.
What’s the difference between EBIT and EBT?
EBT (earnings before tax) has already subtracted interest; EBIT hasn’t. EBT = EBIT − interest.
