Profitability index (PI)

Profitability index: how to calculate it, how to rank projects when money is tight, and how it relates to NPV

Short answer: PI = present value of future cash flows ÷ initial investment. A $10,000 project whose future cash flows are worth $11,588 today (at 8%) has a PI of about 1.16 – every dollar invested returns $1.16 in today’s money. A PI above 1 means a positive NPV.

When a business has more good projects than money, it needs a way to choose. The profitability index measures value created per dollar invested, which makes it handy for ranking projects of different sizes under a fixed budget.

Ranking under a budget: a quick example

With a $100,000 budget: Project A costs $100,000 and has NPV $20,000 (PI 1.20). Projects B and C cost $50,000 each with NPVs of $15,000 and $12,000 (PIs 1.30 and 1.24). Ranking by PI picks B and C, for a combined NPV of $27,000 – more than A alone. That’s the main job of the profitability index.

Questions people ask

Is the profitability index the same as the benefit–cost ratio?
Essentially yes – both divide the present value of benefits by the cost. Some public-sector analyses define costs more broadly, but the idea is the same.
When can PI give the wrong answer?
When projects can’t be split and budgets don’t fit neatly, or when projects are mutually exclusive. In those cases, choose the combination with the highest total NPV.
How do I calculate PI in Excel?
=NPV(rate, future cash flows) ÷ initial investment. Remember Excel’s NPV doesn’t include the year-0 outlay, so don’t add it before dividing.
