Fixed asset turnover

Fixed asset turnover ratio: formula, how to read it for capital-heavy businesses, and common pitfalls

Short answer: fixed asset turnover = net sales ÷ average net fixed assets (property, plant and equipment after depreciation). Sales of $2.4 million on $800,000 of net fixed assets give a ratio of 3.0 – each dollar invested in long-term assets brought in $3 of sales.

For factories, airlines, hospitals or delivery companies, the big question is whether all that expensive equipment is earning its keep. The fixed asset turnover ratio answers it, and lenders often check it before financing new investment.

How to calculate it

  1. Take net sales for the year (after returns and discounts).
  2. Find net fixed assets – gross property, plant and equipment minus accumulated depreciation – at the start and end of the year, and average them.
  3. Divide sales by that average.

Pitfalls to watch

  • Old assets flatter the ratio: heavily depreciated equipment has a low book value, so turnover looks high even if the plant is ageing.
  • Big new investments depress it temporarily – sales usually lag behind new capacity.
  • Leasing vs owning: companies that lease rather than buy may show higher turnover; include right-of-use assets for a fairer comparison.
  • Seasonal or lumpy sales can distort a single year – look at a few years’ trend.

Questions people ask

What is a good fixed asset turnover ratio?
It depends heavily on the industry. Capital-light businesses such as retailers can exceed 5, while utilities and telecoms often sit below 1. The trend over time and comparison with close competitors are what matter.
Should I use gross or net fixed assets?
Net (after depreciation) is the usual convention. Some analysts use gross figures to avoid the old-asset effect – just stay consistent.
How does it relate to total asset turnover?
Total asset turnover includes cash, receivables and inventory too, so it’s always lower than fixed asset turnover for the same company. See the total asset turnover calculator.
