Asset turnover ratios

Total asset turnover: how efficiently a business turns its assets into sales – formula, examples and how to improve it

Short answer: total asset turnover = net sales ÷ average total assets. A business with $2.4 million of sales and average assets of $1.6 million has a turnover of 1.5 – each dollar of assets produced $1.50 of sales in the year.

Two businesses can earn the same profit margin and still produce very different returns, because one squeezes far more sales out of its buildings, machines and stock. Asset turnover captures that efficiency – and together with profit margin, it drives return on assets (ROA = margin × turnover).

Why the “normal” number varies so much

Business typeTypical pattern
Supermarkets, discount retailHigh turnover (often 2 or more), thin margins
ManufacturingModerate turnover, around 1
Utilities, telecoms, propertyLow turnover (well below 1), large asset bases

Ways to improve it

  • Sell or put to work idle assets – unused space, equipment or excess cash.
  • Reduce inventory with better forecasting and faster stock turns.
  • Collect receivables faster (see receivables turnover).
  • Grow sales without adding proportional assets, for example through online channels or longer opening hours.

Questions people ask

Is a higher asset turnover always better?
Generally it signals efficiency, but a very high figure can also mean old, fully depreciated assets that will soon need replacing – which will push turnover down later.
What’s the difference between total and fixed asset turnover?
Fixed asset turnover uses only property, plant and equipment, so it focuses on how well long-term assets generate sales. In this example, with $800,000 of net fixed assets, fixed asset turnover would be 3.0. Try the fixed asset turnover calculator.
Why use average assets?
Sales happen over the whole year, while the balance sheet is a snapshot. Averaging the opening and closing figures gives a fairer match.
