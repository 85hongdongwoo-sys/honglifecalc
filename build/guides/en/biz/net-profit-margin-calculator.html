Net profit margin, explained

What is a good net profit margin? How to calculate it, how it differs from gross margin, and practical ways to improve it

Short answer: net profit margin = net profit ÷ revenue × 100. A business with $500,000 in sales and $45,000 of profit after all costs, interest and tax has a 9% net margin – it keeps 9 cents of every dollar it sells. What’s “good” depends heavily on the industry.

Revenue shows how much a business sells; net profit margin shows how much of that it actually keeps. It’s one of the most useful numbers for comparing a business with itself over time, or with competitors of a similar type.

Gross, operating and net margin

MarginWhat’s subtracted from revenueExample ($500k sales)
Gross marginCost of goods sold only$200k ÷ $500k = 40%
Operating marginPlus rent, wages, marketing and other running costs$75k ÷ $500k = 15%
Net marginPlus interest and tax$45k ÷ $500k = 9%

Why “good” depends on the industry

Supermarkets and wholesalers often run on low single-digit net margins and make up for it with high volume, while software and some professional services can keep 20% or more. Compare your margin with businesses of the same type and size – and with your own figures from previous years – rather than with a universal target.

Ways to improve net margin

  • Raise prices where customers value what you offer – even a small increase drops straight to profit.
  • Cut the cost of goods by negotiating with suppliers or reducing waste and returns.
  • Trim overheads that don’t bring in revenue, such as unused subscriptions or space.
  • Focus on your most profitable products and customers instead of chasing all revenue.
  • Refinance expensive debt to lower interest costs.

Questions people ask about net profit margin

Is net profit margin before or after tax?
After tax. It uses the bottom line of the income statement – the profit left after every expense, interest and tax.
Is net profit margin the same as return on sales?
They’re closely related and sometimes used interchangeably, but return on sales often uses operating profit instead of net profit. Check which definition a report is using.
Can net profit margin be negative?
Yes – a negative margin means the business made a loss in that period. It’s common for start-ups and in one-off bad years.
How do I calculate net profit margin in Excel?
With revenue in A2 and net profit in B2, use =B2/A2 and format the cell as a percentage.
