APR · the real cost of borrowing

What APR really means – APR vs interest rate, what counts as a good APR, and how fees change the number

Short answer: APR (annual percentage rate) is the yearly cost of a loan including most fees, so it’s usually higher than the headline interest rate. A $10,000, 5-year loan at 7% with a $300 fee has an APR of about 8.3%. On a credit card, 24% APR works out to 2% a month – about 26.8% a year if the interest compounds monthly.

Two loans can advertise the same interest rate and still cost very different amounts, because one adds a hefty arrangement fee. APR exists to fix that: lenders in the US, UK and EU must show it, so you can compare offers on a like-for-like basis. Here’s how to read it.

APR vs interest rate vs APY

TermWhat it includesUse it for
Interest rateOnly the cost of borrowing the moneyWorking out the monthly interest charge
APRInterest plus most upfront fees, spread over the termComparing loans and mortgages
APY / AERInterest including compoundingComparing savings accounts

What is a good APR?

It depends on the type of credit and your credit history. As a rough guide in the US in 2026: well-qualified borrowers might see single-digit APRs on car loans and personal loans, while credit cards commonly charge 20% or more. Rather than chasing an “average”, compare at least three quotes for the same amount and term – a two-point difference on a $20,000 five-year loan is worth over $1,000.

How fees push the APR up

  • A fee paid upfront has a bigger effect on a short loan than a long one, because it’s spread over fewer payments.
  • Fees added to the balance still count – you pay interest on them too.
  • Some costs aren’t in APR, such as optional insurance or late fees. Read the cost table, not just the APR.

Questions people ask about APR

Is APR the same as the interest rate?
Only when there are no fees. Once a lender charges an origination or arrangement fee, the APR is higher than the interest rate. For credit cards without annual fees, the two are usually the same number.
Is APR monthly or yearly?
Yearly. To get the monthly rate, divide by 12: a 24% APR card charges about 2% a month on the balance you carry.
How is APR calculated on a credit card?
Most cards divide the APR by 365 to get a daily rate and charge it on your average daily balance. Pay the full statement balance by the due date and you normally pay no interest at all on purchases.
How much APR is too much for a car loan?
There’s no legal cut-off in most places, but if a dealer’s APR is several points above what your bank or credit union offers, it’s worth walking away or arranging finance first. Check the total amount payable, not just the monthly payment.
Can I lower my APR?
Often, yes: improve your credit score, ask your card issuer for a lower rate, move a balance to a 0% transfer card, or refinance a loan when rates fall. Paying on time every month is the single biggest factor.
