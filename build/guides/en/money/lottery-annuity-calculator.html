Lump sum or annuity?

Lottery annuity vs lump sum – how the 30 payments work, what a $100 million jackpot is really worth, and how winners decide

Short answer: for US Powerball and Mega Millions, the advertised jackpot is the total of 30 yearly payments that rise 5% a year. On a $100 million jackpot, the first payment is about $1.5 million and the last about $6.2 million (before tax). Valued at a 4% interest rate, that stream is worth about $52 million today – close to the usual cash option.

Winning a jackpot comes with an unusual decision: take a smaller amount now, or a larger total spread over three decades. Neither is automatically better. The right choice depends on taxes, discipline, what you could earn on the money and how you feel about risk.

Lump sum or annuity – how to think about it

  • Take the lump sum if you’re confident you (and good advisers) can invest it to beat the annuity’s built-in growth, or you want the money available now for family or big plans.
  • Take the annuity if you want protection from spending it all, a steady income for 30 years, and a guaranteed rising payment. If you die early, remaining payments typically go to your estate.
  • Taxes: US federal withholding of 24% applies to large prizes, and the top federal rate is 37%. Spreading income over 30 years can keep more of it out of the top bracket in some years; state taxes vary.
Most experts advise a jackpot winner to stay anonymous where the law allows, pause before announcing anything, and assemble a team – a fee-only financial planner, a tax adviser and an attorney – before choosing.

Questions people ask about lottery annuities

Why is the cash option so much smaller than the jackpot?
The jackpot is the sum of 30 future payments. The cash option is roughly what it would cost today to buy that stream of payments, so it reflects current interest rates. When rates are higher, the cash option is a smaller share of the jackpot.
How much is a $1 million lottery prize after tax?
In the US, 24% federal withholding leaves $760,000 up front, but the final federal tax can be higher (up to 37%) when you file. State tax ranges from none (for example Florida and Texas) to over 10%.
Can I change my mind after choosing the annuity?
Generally no – the choice is made when you claim. Some winners later sell future payments to a company for a lump sum, usually at a steep discount.
What discount rate should I use in the calculator?
Use the return you realistically expect on safe investments – for example, the yield on long-term government bonds. A higher rate makes the lump sum look better; a lower rate favours the annuity.
