Biweekly mortgage payments

Do biweekly mortgage payments really save money? A $300,000 example, how it works, and the cheaper way to get the same result

Short answer: yes. Paying half your monthly payment every two weeks means 26 half-payments – 13 full payments a year instead of 12. On a $300,000, 30-year loan at 6.5%, that pays it off about 6 years early and saves roughly $87,000 in interest.

The trick isn’t magic: it’s simply one extra monthly payment a year, slipped in so gradually you hardly notice. Every extra dollar goes straight to principal, so less interest builds up in every month that follows.

Watch out for fees

Some third-party “biweekly programs” charge a setup fee or a per-payment fee, and some just hold your money and pay the lender once a month – which removes most of the benefit. Before signing up, ask your lender whether it accepts biweekly payments directly and applies them as they arrive. If not, simply adding a twelfth to each monthly payment (and marking it “principal only”) achieves the same thing for free.

Questions people ask about biweekly payments

How many years does a biweekly mortgage take off?
On a 30-year loan, typically about 4 to 7 years depending on the rate (roughly 4 years at 4%, 7 years at 8%) – higher rates save more. On a 15-year loan, around 1½ to 2 years.
Is biweekly better than making one extra payment a year?
The effect is nearly identical. Biweekly spreads the extra over the year and lines up nicely with fortnightly paychecks; a single extra payment works if you get an annual bonus or tax refund.
Should I pay off my mortgage early or invest?
It’s a trade-off. Overpaying gives a guaranteed return equal to your mortgage rate; investing may earn more but with risk. Many people do both, and it usually makes sense to clear higher-interest debts and build an emergency fund first.
Does a biweekly schedule lower my monthly payment?
No – each payment is half your normal monthly amount, so it feels the same per paycheck. The saving comes from the one extra payment each year.
