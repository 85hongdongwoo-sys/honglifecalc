Before you buy the paint

How much paint do I need? Room-by-room estimates, coverage per litre and gallon, and how not to run out halfway

Short answer: a typical bedroom of 12 × 14 ft with 8 ft walls needs about 2 gallons for two coats (roughly 380 sq ft of wall). In metric, a 4 × 3.5 m room with 2.4 m walls has about 33 m² of wall and needs around 6.5 litres for two coats. One gallon covers about 350–400 sq ft per coat; one litre covers about 10–12 m².

Running out of paint with one wall to go is annoying; ending up with three half-used tins in the garage isn’t much better. The calculator above measures the walls for you, but these rules of thumb help you check the result in the shop.

Measure it yourself in four steps

  1. Add up the wall lengths (the room’s perimeter) and multiply by the wall height.
  2. Take off doors and windows: about 21 sq ft (1.9 m²) for a standard door and 15 sq ft (1.5 m²) for an average window.
  3. Multiply by the number of coats – two is normal; three for a big colour change or bare plaster.
  4. Divide by the coverage printed on the tin, then round up to the next tin size.

Things that change how much you need

  • New plaster or bare drywall soaks up paint – seal it with a mist coat or primer first, or allow 20–30% more.
  • Dark to light colours usually need a primer or an extra coat.
  • Textured walls have more surface area; add about 10–20%.
  • Rollers vs sprayers: sprayers waste more paint in the air, often 20–30% more than rolling.
Buy all the paint for one room at the same time, and write the batch number down. Colours can vary slightly between batches, and the difference shows on a single wall.

Questions people ask about paint

How much area does a gallon of paint cover?
Most interior wall paints cover 350–400 sq ft per gallon per coat on smooth, primed walls. Check the tin – thicker one-coat paints and textured walls can bring that down to 250–300 sq ft.
How much paint for a 12x12 room?
With 8 ft walls, a 12 × 12 ft room has about 384 sq ft of wall, or roughly 350 sq ft after a door and window. Two coats need about 2 gallons.
Do I really need two coats?
Almost always. One coat tends to look patchy, especially with darker colours or when the old colour shows through. Two thin coats look better and last longer than one thick coat.
Should I paint the ceiling first?
Yes – ceilings, then walls, then trim and doors. Drips from the ceiling land on walls you haven’t finished yet.
How long should I wait between coats?
For most water-based (latex/acrylic) paints, 2–4 hours; oil-based paints often need 24 hours. Cold or damp rooms slow drying down, so follow the time on the tin.
