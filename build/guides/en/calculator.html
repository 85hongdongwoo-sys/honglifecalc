Everyday calculator tips

Getting the right answer from a basic calculator – memory keys, percentages, chained sums and the mistakes almost everyone makes

Quick tips: this calculator follows the usual order of operations, so 2 + 3 × 4 = 14. Use brackets when you mean something else: (2 + 3) × 4 = 20. For percentages, 200 × 15 % = 30, and 200 + 15 % = 230 – handy for adding tax or a tip.

A simple calculator is still the quickest tool for splitting a bill, checking a receipt, totting up a budget or helping with homework. It works with your keyboard too: type numbers and + − * /, press Enter for the result and Backspace to correct a digit. Here are the tricks that make it faster and the traps that catch people out.

Everyday examples

  • Split a bill: $186.40 ÷ 4 = $46.60 each. Add a 15% tip first: 186.40 × 1.15 ÷ 4 = $53.59.
  • Price per unit: 750 g for $4.20 → 4.20 ÷ 750 × 100 = $0.56 per 100 g. Compare with the bigger pack the same way.
  • Add sales tax: $64 × 1.0825 = $69.28 at 8.25% tax.
  • Monthly to yearly: $1,350 rent × 12 = $16,200 a year.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting brackets in averages: (70 + 80 + 90) ÷ 3 = 80, but 70 + 80 + 90 ÷ 3 = 180.
  • Taking a percentage off twice: 20% off and then 10% off is 28% off in total, not 30%.
  • Mixing units – minutes and hours, pence and pounds, cm and m. Convert first, then calculate.
  • Rounding too early. Keep full numbers until the final answer, then round.

Questions people ask about calculators

Can I type on my keyboard instead of clicking?
Yes. Type numbers and + − * /, press Enter (or =) for the result, Backspace to delete a digit and Esc to clear. The % key works from the keyboard too.
How does a calculator work?
Inside, numbers are stored in binary and processed by a small chip that adds, subtracts, multiplies and divides very quickly; more complex functions are built from those basic steps. This online calculator does the same job in your browser.
Why do two calculators give different answers for 6 ÷ 2(1 + 2)?
Because the expression is ambiguous. Some calculators treat 2(1 + 2) as one block (answer 1); others go strictly left to right (answer 9). Writing it with explicit × and brackets – (6 ÷ 2) × 3 or 6 ÷ (2 × 3) – removes the doubt.
Can I use a calculator on the SAT or ACT?
Yes, on the maths sections of both tests, with an approved calculator (the digital SAT also has a built-in one). Online calculators on other websites aren’t allowed during the exam itself, so practise with the one you’ll use.
How do I calculate a percentage on a calculator?
Multiply by the percentage and divide by 100: 18% of 250 = 250 × 18 ÷ 100 = 45. For more percentage types – increase, decrease, reverse – try the percentage calculator.
Is there a scientific version?
Yes – for powers, roots, trigonometry and logs, use the scientific calculator.
