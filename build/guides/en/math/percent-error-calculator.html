Percent error for lab reports

How to calculate percent error – the formula, worked chemistry and physics examples, and what counts as “acceptable”

The formula: percent error = |experimental − theoretical| ÷ |theoretical| × 100. If you measured g as 9.60 m/s² and the accepted value is 9.81 m/s², the error is 0.21 ÷ 9.81 × 100 = 2.1%. It’s usually reported as a positive number.

Percent error is how lab reports answer the question “how close did we get?”. It compares your measured (experimental) value with the accepted (theoretical) one, as a percentage of the accepted value. Here’s how to calculate it, how to interpret it, and how to explain it in your discussion section.

How much percent error is acceptable?

There’s no single rule – it depends on the equipment and the experiment. As a rough guide in school and undergraduate labs:

  • Under about 5% – usually considered good for simple equipment (rulers, stopwatches, basic glassware).
  • 5–10% – acceptable if you can explain the main sources of error.
  • Above 10% – a sign of a systematic problem worth investigating: calibration, a leak, heat loss, reaction time, a calculation slip.

Precision instruments and professional labs work to much tighter tolerances. Your teacher or lab manual may set its own target – go by that.

Questions people ask about percent error

Can percent error be negative?
The standard formula uses absolute values, so it’s always positive. Some teachers drop the absolute value so the sign shows direction: negative means your value was too low, positive too high. Say which convention you used.
Is it experimental minus theoretical, or the other way round?
Either, when you take the absolute value – the result is the same. What matters is that you divide by the theoretical (accepted) value, not your measured one.
What’s the difference between percent error and percent difference?
Percent error compares a measurement with a known true value. Percent difference compares two measurements when neither is “correct”, dividing by their average: |A − B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2) × 100.
Does percent error measure accuracy or precision?
Accuracy – how close you are to the true value. Precision is about how close repeated measurements are to each other, which you’d show with a range or standard deviation instead.
How do I calculate percent error in Excel?
If the measured value is in A2 and the accepted value in B2, use =ABS(A2-B2)/ABS(B2)*100, or format the cell as a percentage and leave out the *100.
