Sample size for surveys & research

How big does my sample need to be? Sample sizes for common margins of error, small populations, and what to write in your methods section

Short answer: for a survey with a 95% confidence level and ±5% margin of error, you need about 385 responses from a large population. For ±3% you need about 1,068, and for ±10% about 97. If your whole population is small – say 1,000 people – 278 responses are enough for ±5%.

“Is my sample big enough?” is one of the first questions in any survey, dissertation or market research project. The answer depends on three things: how sure you want to be (confidence level), how precise you need the result to be (margin of error), and how varied the answers are likely to be. When your population is small

Notice how quickly it levels off: beyond a few thousand people, population size barely matters. That’s why national polls can use about 1,000 people.

Plan for non-responses

The numbers above are completed responses. If you expect only 20% of people to reply, invite five times as many – about 1,925 invitations to end up with 385 answers. And remember: a big sample can’t fix a biased one. 385 replies from people who all follow your brand on social media won’t represent the general public.

Questions people ask about sample size

How much sample size is enough for research?
For a descriptive survey, 385 completed responses (95% confidence, ±5%) is the common benchmark. Experiments comparing groups need a power analysis instead – the size depends on how big an effect you expect to detect.
How is sample size determined in qualitative research?
Not by a formula. Interview studies usually keep going until new interviews stop producing new themes (“saturation”), often somewhere around 12–30 interviews for a focused question.
Is sample size the same as the number of respondents?
In everyday use, yes: sample size (n) is the number of people whose answers you analyse. It’s different from the number you invited and from the population size (N).
What is a small sample size a limitation of?
A small sample gives a wider margin of error and less power to detect real differences, so results are less certain and harder to generalise. It’s worth stating your margin of error in the limitations section.
How do I write the sample size in my methods section?
State the population, the confidence level, margin of error and assumed proportion, and the formula: for example, “A minimum sample of 385 was calculated for a 95% confidence level, ±5% margin of error and p = 0.5 (Cochran, 1977).”
