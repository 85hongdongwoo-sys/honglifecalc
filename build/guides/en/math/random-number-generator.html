Random numbers · fair picks and draws

Using a random number generator fairly – picking winners, rolling dice, choosing lottery-style numbers, and how “random” it really is

Short answer: to pick one number between 1 and 100, set the minimum to 1 and the maximum to 100 – every number has exactly a 1 in 100 chance. For a giveaway, number your entries, generate one number, and the matching entry wins. Turn on “no repeats” when you need several different winners.

People use random numbers for all sorts of everyday decisions: drawing a raffle, choosing who goes first, assigning teams, picking a restaurant or testing a game idea. The generator above uses your browser’s built-in random function, which is more than good enough for draws, games and classroom activities.

Running a fair giveaway

  1. Close entries and make a numbered list – a spreadsheet row number is perfect.
  2. Remove duplicate or invalid entries before the draw, and say publicly how many valid entries there are.
  3. Generate the number(s) live or record your screen, so people can see the draw wasn’t chosen.
  4. Announce the matching entry. If a winner doesn’t respond, draw again from the remaining entries.

Questions people ask about random numbers

Is this generator truly random?
It’s “pseudo-random”: numbers come from an algorithm seeded unpredictably by your device. They’re statistically even and unpredictable enough for games, draws and sampling. For cryptography or regulated lotteries, special hardware or certified systems are used instead.
What are the odds of picking the same number twice?
With repeats allowed, each draw is independent. Drawing two numbers from 1–10, there’s a 1 in 10 chance the second matches the first. Over many draws, repeats happen far more often than people expect – that’s normal randomness, not a fault.
Can I generate random numbers in Excel?
Yes: =RANDBETWEEN(1,100) gives a whole number from 1 to 100, and =RAND() gives a decimal between 0 and 1. Both change every time the sheet recalculates.
What are the odds of winning a 6-from-49 lottery?
1 in 13,983,816 for the jackpot. Generated numbers have exactly the same chance as numbers you pick yourself – but random picks are less likely to share a jackpot, because many players choose birthdays.
Does a random number generator remember previous results?
No. Each new number is independent, so after five 6s in a row the next roll is still a 1 in 6 chance of another 6.
