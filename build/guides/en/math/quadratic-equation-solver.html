Quadratic equations, step by step

How to solve a quadratic equation – factoring, the quadratic formula and the discriminant, with fully worked examples

The formula: for ax² + bx + c = 0, the solutions are x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a. For x² − 5x + 6 = 0: x = (5 ± √(25 − 24)) ÷ 2 = (5 ± 1) ÷ 2, so x = 3 or x = 2.

Quadratics turn up in maths class, physics (anything thrown in the air), business (profit curves) and engineering. There are three main ways to solve them, and knowing when to use each saves a lot of time. The solver above shows the steps; here’s the reasoning behind them.

Method 1: factoring (fastest when it works)

Look for two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. For x² + 7x + 12 = 0: 3 × 4 = 12 and 3 + 4 = 7, so (x + 3)(x + 4) = 0 and x = −3 or x = −4. If you can’t spot the pair within a few seconds, move on to the formula.

Method 2: the quadratic formula (always works)

  1. Write the equation as ax² + bx + c = 0 and read off a, b and c. For 2x² + 3x − 2 = 0: a = 2, b = 3, c = −2.
  2. Work out the discriminant: b² − 4ac = 9 − 4(2)(−2) = 9 + 16 = 25.
  3. Take the square root: √25 = 5.
  4. Plug in: x = (−3 ± 5) ÷ 4, so x = 0.5 or x = −2.

Method 3: completing the square

Useful for finding the vertex of a parabola. x² + 6x + 5 = 0 becomes (x + 3)² − 4 = 0, so x + 3 = ±2 and x = −1 or −5. The vertex is at (−3, −4).

Questions people ask about quadratic equations

How do I solve a quadratic equation on a calculator?
Many scientific calculators have an equation mode (often under MODE → EQN). Otherwise, calculate the discriminant first, take its square root, then work out (−b + root) ÷ 2a and (−b − root) ÷ 2a separately – brackets matter.
What does a quadratic equation solve for?
The values of x that make the expression equal zero – the “roots”. On a graph, they’re where the parabola crosses the x-axis.
What if a is zero?
Then it isn’t a quadratic – it’s a linear equation (bx + c = 0) with the single solution x = −c ÷ b.
Can Excel solve quadratic equations?
Yes. With a, b and c in A1, B1 and C1: =(-B1+SQRT(B1^2-4*A1*C1))/(2*A1) and =(-B1-SQRT(B1^2-4*A1*C1))/(2*A1). Goal Seek also works for finding a root numerically.
How do I check my answer?
Substitute each solution back into the original equation. For x = 3 in x² − 5x + 6: 9 − 15 + 6 = 0. ✓ The sum of the roots should also equal −b ÷ a, and their product c ÷ a.
