Standard deviation, explained plainly

What standard deviation tells you, sample vs population, and how to work it out by hand

In one line: standard deviation is the typical distance of your numbers from their average. For the data 4, 8, 6, 5, 3, 7 the mean is 5.5 and the sample standard deviation is about 1.87 (the population version is about 1.71). A small value means the numbers are bunched together; a large one means they’re spread out.

Two classes can have the same average score of 75 and still feel completely different: in one, everyone scored between 70 and 80; in the other, scores ran from 40 to 100. The average can’t tell them apart – the standard deviation can. That’s why it shows up in exam results, quality control, investment risk and almost every science report.

Working it out by hand

  1. Find the mean. 4 + 8 + 6 + 5 + 3 + 7 = 33, and 33 ÷ 6 = 5.5.
  2. Subtract the mean from each value and square it: 2.25, 6.25, 0.25, 0.25, 6.25, 2.25.
  3. Add the squares: 17.5.
  4. Divide by n − 1 = 5 for a sample (3.5), or by n = 6 for a whole population (2.92). This is the variance.
  5. Take the square root: √3.5 ≈ 1.87 (sample) or √2.92 ≈ 1.71 (population).

Sample or population – which one?

UseWhen your data is…Divide by
Sample (s)a part of a bigger group – a survey, a batch you tested, last month’s pricesn − 1

If you’re not sure, use the sample version. Dividing by n − 1 (Bessel’s correction) makes up for the fact that a sample tends to look a little less spread out than the full group. With large data sets the two numbers become almost identical.

Questions people ask about standard deviation

What is a “good” standard deviation?
There’s no universal good or bad – it depends on the scale. A standard deviation of 5 is huge for shoe sizes and tiny for house prices. Compare it with the mean: the coefficient of variation (SD ÷ mean) puts different data sets on the same footing.
What’s the difference between variance and standard deviation?
Variance is the average squared distance from the mean; standard deviation is its square root. Standard deviation is easier to interpret because it’s in the same units as your data – points, kilograms, dollars.
Can standard deviation be negative or zero?
It can never be negative, because it comes from squared distances. It’s zero only when every value is identical.
How do I calculate it in Excel or Google Sheets?
Use =STDEV.S(range) for a sample and =STDEV.P(range) for a population. The older =STDEV() is the sample version.
What does one standard deviation above the mean mean for a test score?
If the mean is 80 and the SD is 11, a score of 91 is one SD above average – roughly better than 84% of test-takers if scores are bell-shaped.
