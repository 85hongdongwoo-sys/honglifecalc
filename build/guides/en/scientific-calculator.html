How to use a scientific calculator

Scientific calculator basics – order of operations, degrees vs radians, powers, roots and logs, with worked examples

Quick tip: most “wrong answers” on a scientific calculator come from two things – a missing bracket or the wrong angle mode. Type 2 + 3 × 4 and you get 14, not 20, because multiplication comes first. And sin(30) is 0.5 only in degree mode; in radians it’s about −0.988.

A scientific calculator does everything a basic one does, plus the functions you meet in school maths, science and engineering: powers and roots, trigonometry, logarithms, factorials and constants like π and e. Here’s how to get reliable answers from it, with examples you can type into the calculator above to check.

The order it calculates in

  1. Brackets first – anything inside ( ) is worked out before the rest.
  2. Powers and roots – x², xʸ, √.
  3. Multiplication and division, left to right.
  4. Addition and subtraction, left to right.

So 8 ÷ 2 × (2 + 2) is 8 ÷ 2 × 4 = 16. When in doubt, add brackets – they never hurt.

Degrees or radians?

Use degrees for everyday geometry and most school trigonometry (a right angle is 90°). Use radians in calculus, physics and programming, where a full turn is 2π. To convert, multiply degrees by π ÷ 180: 60° = π ÷ 3 ≈ 1.047 radians. If a trig answer looks strange, check the mode first.

Questions people ask

Why does my calculator give a different answer from my friend’s?
Usually one of you is in radians and the other in degrees, or one calculator treats implied multiplication like 2(3+1) differently. Writing the expression with an explicit × and brackets removes the ambiguity.
What does “E” mean in an answer like 3.2E-5?
It’s scientific notation: 3.2E-5 means 3.2 × 10⁻⁵, or 0.000032. Very large and very small results are shown this way so they fit on the screen.
What’s the difference between log and ln?
log is base 10 (log 100 = 2) and ln is the natural log, base e ≈ 2.718 (ln e = 1). Science formulas such as pH use log; growth and decay formulas usually use ln.
How do I do fractions or percentages?
Type fractions as a division in brackets – (3 ÷ 4) – and percentages as ÷ 100: 15% of 80 is 80 × 15 ÷ 100 = 12. Our percentage calculator handles increases and reverse percentages for you.
Can I use this calculator in an exam?
Online calculators aren’t allowed in most exams – check your exam board’s approved list. It’s great for homework and checking your working, though.
