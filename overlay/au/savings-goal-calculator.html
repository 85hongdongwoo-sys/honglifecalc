Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): Monthly saving = what you still need ÷ months, a little less if your account pays interest. Saving $10,000 in a year takes about $833 a month ($814 at 5% interest), and a $100,000 house deposit in 5 years about $1,667 a month ($1,470 at 5%). Interest is taxable income, and deposits are protected up to $250,000 per bank.

How the calculator works

Example first. You want $30,000 in 3 years. You already have $3,000, and your account pays 4.5% a year. The $3,000 grows on its own to about $3,431. You still need about $26,569. Saving $691 a month gets you there, because interest on your deposits adds about $2,136 along the way.

The steps are:

  • Monthly rate = yearly rate ÷ 12. So 4.5% a year is 0.375% a month.
  • Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest too. The calculator adds interest every month.
  • Gap = goal − what your current savings will grow to.
  • Monthly deposit = gap × i ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1), where i is the monthly rate and n is the number of months.

With no interest it is simply: (goal − already saved) ÷ months. If your current savings already grow past the goal, you need $0 a month.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Pay yourself first. Set up an automatic transfer on payday, so the money moves before you can spend it.
  • Split weekly or fortnightly. The calculator shows about how much that is if you are paid weekly or fortnightly.
  • Check bonus-interest conditions. Many accounts only pay the top rate if you deposit a set amount or make no withdrawals that month. Enter the rate you will really get.
  • Don't forget tax. Interest is taxable, so your real return is lower than the rate shown.
  • Keep a buffer. Saving for a goal works best when you also have money set aside for emergencies.
  • Rates change. If the RBA cash rate moves, savings rates often follow. Re-run the numbers every few months.

Savings goal FAQ

How much do I need to save each month?

Divide what is still needed by the number of months, then take off a little for interest. For $20,000 in 2 years from zero: $20,000 ÷ 24 = $833 a month with no interest, or $794 a month if your account pays 5% a year.

How long does it take to save $10,000?

At $500 a month it takes 20 months – with no interest you hit exactly $10,000, and at 4.5% a year you have about $10,364. At $1,000 a month it takes 10 months; at $250 a month, about 3 years and 4 months without interest.

How much should I save each month for a house deposit?

For a $100,000 deposit in 5 years from zero, about $1,667 a month with no interest, or $1,470 a month at 5% a year. If you already have $20,000 and earn 4.5%, you need about $1,116 a month.

Does interest make a big difference?

Over a short time, not much: saving $10,000 in a year at 5% only saves you about $19 a month. Over 5 years it adds up: $1,000 a month at 5% grows to about $68,006 instead of $60,000.

Is the interest on my savings taxed?

Yes. The ATO says Australian residents must declare interest as income. It is added to your taxable income for the year, so at a 30% tax rate $1,000 of interest leaves you about $700 (less the 2% Medicare levy).

Is my money safe in a savings account?

Deposits in Australian banks, building societies and credit unions are protected by the government's Financial Claims Scheme up to $250,000 per account holder per institution.
