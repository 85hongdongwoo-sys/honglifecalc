How the fuel cost is calculated

Fuel needed (L) = distance (km) × consumption (L/100 km) ÷ 100. Cost = litres × price per litre. The calculator then divides the cost between the people sharing the trip.

Ways to cut fuel costs

  • Buy at the low point of your city's price cycle — in the capital cities, unleaded prices rise and fall in regular cycles.
  • Keep tyres at the placard pressure and remove roof racks when not needed.
  • Drive smoothly and use cruise control on highways.
  • Compare stations with your state's fuel app (e.g. FuelCheck in NSW, FuelWatch in WA).

FAQ

How do I work out the fuel cost of a trip?

Litres = distance (km) × consumption (L/100 km) ÷ 100, then multiply by the price per litre. A 500 km trip in a car using 7 L/100 km needs 35 litres; at $2.34 a litre that is $81.90.

What is the average petrol price in Australia now?

News reports on 9 November 2026 put the national average for unleaded 91 at about 234.3 cents a litre and diesel at about 278 cents (prices as at 7 November). Prices move every week and vary by city cycle — check your state's fuel price app before you fill up.

How much fuel does a car use per 100 km?

Small cars typically use about 5–7 L/100 km, medium SUVs 7–9 and large 4WDs and utes 9–12 or more. Use the combined figure on your car's fuel consumption label or the trip computer for a better estimate.

How much does Sydney to Melbourne cost in fuel?

It is about 880 km via the Hume. At 7 L/100 km you need about 62 litres — roughly $144 at $2.34 a litre.

How do I split fuel costs between passengers?

Divide the total by the number of people sharing. If you claim car expenses for work, the ATO's cents-per-kilometre method uses a set rate instead of actual fuel.
