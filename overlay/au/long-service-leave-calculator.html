Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): In NSW, Queensland, WA and Tasmania you get 8.67 weeks of paid long service leave after 10 years with one employer; SA and the NT give 13 weeks after 10 years, and Victoria and the ACT give 6.07 weeks after 7 years. Leave keeps building at about 0.87 weeks a year (1.3 in SA and the NT), and a pro-rata payout can start after 5 or 7 years if you leave.

Long service leave rules by state (2026)

Long service leave is extra paid leave for staying a long time with the same employer. Each state and territory has its own law, so the rules depend on where you work.

How long service leave is worked out

First, an example. Sam works in NSW for 12 years and earns $1,800 a week. NSW gives 8.67 weeks for 10 years, which is 0.8667 weeks for each year. 12 × 0.8667 = 10.40 weeks. 10.40 × $1,800 = $18,720.

The same steps work everywhere:

  • Weeks of leave = years of service × your state's rate per year.
  • Rate per year = 0.8667 weeks in NSW, Victoria, Queensland, WA, Tasmania and the ACT, or 1.3 weeks in SA and the NT.
  • Value = weeks of leave × ordinary weekly pay.

Qualifying period means the years you must work before you can take leave: 7 years in Victoria and the ACT, 10 years everywhere else. In NSW, WA, Tasmania and the NT later leave comes in 5-year blocks. That is why the calculator shows both "accrued" (built up) and "can take now".

Ordinary weekly pay is your normal pay for your usual hours. Some states average pay over a period if your hours changed, so check your state's rules if you went part-time or casual.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check which law applies. Some awards and enterprise agreements, and portable schemes in industries like construction, cleaning and community services, have their own rules.
  • Don't resign a few weeks too early. In Queensland, NSW, Tasmania, the ACT and the NT, leaving before 10 years (7 in the ACT) only pays out for certain reasons. In NSW and Tasmania that does not include resigning just to change jobs.
  • Part years count in some states, not others. WA, SA and the NT count completed years only.
  • Casual work counts if it is regular and the gaps are not too long.
  • Payouts are taxed. Long service leave paid when you leave is added to your income, so the amount you get is less than the figure here.
  • Keep records. Your payslips and start date are your proof of continuous service.

Long service leave FAQ

How much long service leave do you get after 10 years?

In NSW, Queensland, WA and Tasmania it is 8.67 weeks (about 2 months). In South Australia and the Northern Territory it is 13 weeks. In Victoria and the ACT you have already qualified at 7 years, so by 10 years you have about 8.67 weeks.

How much long service leave after 7 years?

In Victoria and the ACT you can take it after 7 years: 6.07 weeks. In Victoria that is 1 week for every 60 weeks worked (364 ÷ 60 = 6.07). In most other states 7 years is when pro-rata payouts start if you leave, not when you can take leave.

What is pro-rata long service leave?

Pro-rata means a part payment for the years you have worked when your job ends before the full entitlement. Example: in NSW after 8 years, if your employer ends your job (not for serious and wilful misconduct), you are paid 8 × 0.8667 = 6.93 weeks. At $1,800 a week that is $12,480.

How is long service leave calculated?

Multiply your years of service by your state's rate, then by your ordinary weekly pay. In NSW the rate is 0.8667 weeks a year, so 12 years × 0.8667 = 10.4 weeks; at $1,800 a week that is $18,720 before tax. In SA and the NT the rate is 1.3 weeks a year.

Do casual workers get long service leave?

Yes, in every state and territory, if their work with one employer is continuous. Gaps can break it: in Victoria a gap of more than 12 weeks usually does, and in Queensland a break of more than 3 months may. In Tasmania casuals need regular work of at least 32 hours in each 4 weeks.

Can I cash out long service leave?

It depends on the state. NSW and the NT say no while you are still employed, and Victoria says generally no. South Australia and Tasmania allow it by agreement with your employer. Queensland allows it only if your award or agreement says so, or the Industrial Relations Commission orders it for hardship or compassionate reasons.
