Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): Under the First Home Super Saver scheme you can put up to $15,000 from any one financial year ($50,000 in total) of voluntary contributions into super and later have 85% of before-tax contributions plus associated earnings released for a first home. Earnings are worked out at the ATO shortfall interest charge rate, 7.51% a year for October to December 2026, and the ATO withholds your expected marginal rate minus a 30% offset (or 17%). On a $90,000 salary, $15,000 a year for 3 years pays out about $43,400 against about $32,300 saved in a bank at 4%.

How the FHSS scheme works

Example first. You earn $90,000 and salary sacrifice $15,000 into super each year. Your fund takes 15% contributions tax, so $12,750 stays in. If you saved that $15,000 of pay yourself, you would lose 32% to tax and keep only $10,200. That 15% versus 32% gap is the whole idea.

  • Salary sacrifice means part of your pay goes straight into super before income tax.
  • Concessional contributions are these before-tax contributions. They are taxed 15% in the fund.
  • Non-concessional contributions are made from money you have already paid tax on. All of them can be released.
  • Released amount = 85% of concessional contributions + 100% of non-concessional contributions + associated earnings.
  • Withheld at release = (concessional part + earnings) × (your expected marginal rate including Medicare − 30%), or 17% if the ATO cannot estimate your rate. It can't go below zero, and it is not your final tax.

The steps: 1) make voluntary contributions; 2) ask the ATO for an FHSS determination, which tells you how much you can get; 3) request the release before ownership of any property transfers to you; 4) wait 15 to 20 business days for the money; 5) buy your home, or recontribute the money to super.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Stay under the cap. Employer super plus salary sacrifice must stay under $32,500. At $150,000 your employer already pays $18,000, so about $14,500 is your room.
  • Low income? Below $45,000 your rate is 15% plus the Medicare levy, close to the fund's 15%. The tax saving is small. After-tax contributions still work, but give no tax saving.
  • Couples can both use it. Each person has their own $50,000 limit.
  • Request the release in time. It has to be before ownership of the property transfers to you, and the money takes 15 to 20 business days.
  • Check your fund. Ask your fund if it will release FHSS amounts. Most do.

FHSS FAQ

How much can I save with the First Home Super Saver scheme?

Up to $15,000 of voluntary contributions from any one financial year and $50,000 in total, plus associated earnings. If all $50,000 is salary sacrifice, 85% ($42,500) plus earnings can be released. On a $90,000 salary, $15,000 a year for 3 years pays out about $43,400 in our example.

How much better is FHSS than saving in a bank?

It depends on your tax rate. On a $90,000 salary (32% marginal rate with the Medicare levy), $15,000 of pay a year for 3 years pays out about $43,400 through FHSS and about $32,300 in a bank at 4% – roughly $11,100 more in our example. Part of that is the tax saving (15% in the fund instead of 32% on your pay). The rest is the 7.51% associated earnings rate being higher than the bank rate.

How is the FHSS release taxed?

The ATO does not take a final tax at release. It nominates a withholding rate for your release: your expected marginal rate including the 2% Medicare levy, minus a 30% tax offset, or 17% if it cannot estimate your rate. At a 32% expected rate the withholding is about 2%. The before-tax part and the earnings then go in your tax return, and what was withheld counts towards your end-of-year tax.

What are associated earnings for FHSS?

They are a notional amount of earnings the ATO works out for you, not what your super fund really earned. The ATO uses its shortfall interest charge rate, which is 7.51% a year for October to December 2026 and changes every quarter. Enter a different rate in the calculator to see the effect.

When do I need to apply for FHSS?

Ask the ATO for an FHSS determination and request the release before ownership of any property transfers to you. You must be 18 or older when you request the determination. A release takes between 15 and 20 business days, so plan ahead.

What happens if I do not buy a home after the release?

In the ATO's example you have 12 months to sign a contract and can ask for a further 12 months. If you still do not buy, you can recontribute the money to super as a non-concessional contribution, or keep it and pay FHSS tax of 20% of your assessable FHSS released amount.

Who is eligible for FHSS?

You must be 18 or older when you request a determination, be a first home buyer who has never owned property in Australia, have your name going on the title, and not have asked for an FHSS release before.

Does my employer super count towards FHSS?

No. Only voluntary contributions count: salary sacrifice, personal contributions you claim a deduction for, and after-tax contributions. Your employer's 12% super guarantee cannot be released.
