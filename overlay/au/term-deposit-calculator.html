Your numbers

Quick answer (2026-27): At an example rate of 4.5% a year, $50,000 for 12 months earns $2,250 and $100,000 earns $4,500. Interest is taxed at your marginal rate plus the 2% Medicare levy in the year you receive it – on $85,000 income you keep $1,530 and $3,060. Deposits are protected up to $250,000 per account holder per bank by the Financial Claims Scheme.

How term deposit interest works

A term deposit locks your money with a bank for a set time (the term) at a fixed interest rate. Here is a real example first: $50,000 at 4.5% a year for 6 months earns $50,000 × 4.5% × 6 ÷ 12 = $1,125.

  • Interest at maturity: deposit × rate × months ÷ 12. You get it all at the end.
  • Monthly, paid to you: the same total, paid in 12 parts. $50,000 at 4.5% pays $187.50 a month.
  • Reinvested (compounding): each payment is added to the balance and also earns interest. Monthly compounding turns 4.5% into $2,296.99 on $50,000 in a year.

Tax: interest is added to your other income. The calculator works out your tax with and without the interest, so the “about %” figure is the real extra tax, including the Medicare levy and any change to the low income tax offset.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Watch the rollover. Many term deposits renew automatically at maturity, sometimes at a lower rate. Set a reminder a week before.
  • Breaking early costs you. Banks usually cut the interest if you withdraw before the end and may ask for notice. Only lock away money you won’t need.
  • Give your TFN. Otherwise 47% may be withheld from interest of $120 or more a year.
  • Stay under $250,000 per bank. The Financial Claims Scheme limit is per account holder per ADI, not per account. Some brands share one banking licence, so check APRA’s list.
  • Compare dollars, not just rates. A higher rate for 6 months can earn less than a slightly lower rate for 12 months.
  • Don’t forget the tax year. A deposit maturing on 1 July instead of 30 June moves the interest into the next income year.

Term deposit questions

How much interest will I earn on $50,000 in a term deposit?

At an example rate of 4.5% a year, $50,000 for 12 months earns $2,250 paid at maturity. For 6 months it earns $1,125. If your taxable income is $85,000, tax and Medicare levy take about 32%, leaving $1,530 from the 12-month deposit.

How much interest on a $100,000 term deposit?

At an example rate of 4.5% a year, $100,000 earns $4,500 over 12 months. After tax at 32% (30% plus the 2% Medicare levy) you keep $3,060. Over 3 years with interest added each year, it earns $14,116.61 before tax.

Is term deposit interest taxed?

Yes. Interest is part of your taxable income and is taxed at your marginal rate (the rate on your last dollar of income) plus the 2% Medicare levy. For 2026-27 that is 32% on income between $45,001 and $135,000. Declare it in the year it is credited or paid to you – for a term deposit, usually the year it matures.

Is it better to get interest monthly or at maturity?

If you take monthly interest out, you earn the same as interest at maturity: $50,000 at 4.5% pays $187.50 a month, or $2,250 a year. If the monthly interest stays in and earns interest too, you get a bit more: $2,296.99. Banks often pay a lower rate for monthly interest, so compare the dollar amounts.

What happens if I don’t give my bank my tax file number?

The bank must withhold tax from your interest at the top rate plus the Medicare levy – 45% + 2% = 47% – once the interest is $120 or more a year. You get it back as a credit when you lodge your tax return, but your money is tied up until then.

Is my term deposit safe if the bank fails?

Deposits with an Australian bank, building society or credit union (an ADI – authorised deposit-taking institution) are protected by the government’s Financial Claims Scheme up to $250,000 per account holder per ADI. Term deposits are covered. Above $250,000, you can split your money across different ADIs.
