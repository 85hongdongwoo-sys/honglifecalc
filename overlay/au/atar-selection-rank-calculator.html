Your ATAR

Short answer: your selection rank for a course is your ATAR plus any adjustment points that course gives you, such as subject bonuses, regional points or Educational Access Schemes. The ATAR itself (0.00–99.95, in steps of 0.05) never changes, and each university caps how many points it adds – many eligible students get only 1 or 2.

How it works

Example first. Your ATAR is 85.40. You did well in a subject the course likes, so the university gives you 2 bonus points. Your selection rank for that course is 85.40 + 2 = 87.40. For another course with no bonus, your selection rank is still 85.40.

  • ATAR (Australian Tertiary Admission Rank) = your rank compared with your age group, from 0.00 to 99.95.
  • Adjustment factors = extra points a university adds for certain reasons, such as subject results, where you live or hardship.
  • Selection rank = ATAR + adjustment points, but no more than the university allows.

The calculator adds your points, cuts them to the university's maximum and adds them to your ATAR.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Check each course. The same scheme can give different points at different universities.
  • Apply for schemes on time. Educational Access Schemes usually need a separate application through UAC, VTAC or your state's admission centre.
  • Don't add points twice. Universities cap the number of points they add, so the total from all schemes may be cut.
  • Lowest selection ranks are a guide. They change every year, so list your dream course first and safer options after.

ATAR and selection rank FAQ

What is the difference between ATAR and selection rank?

Your ATAR is one number for everything. Your selection rank is your ATAR plus any adjustment points for one course. Example: ATAR 85.40 + 2 subject bonus points = selection rank 87.40 for courses that give that bonus.

Do bonus points change my ATAR?

No. Your ATAR stays the same. Bonus points (adjustment factors) only raise your selection rank for the courses and universities that offer them.

How many bonus points can I get?

It depends on the university and the scheme. Each university sets its own rules and its own maximum. If you qualify for 10 points but the university's limit is 5, only 5 count. Check the course page on UAC, VTAC or the university website.

Is there a limit on adjustment points?

Yes, but there is no single number. UAC says each institution caps the points it will add to your selection rank, and many eligible students only get 1 or 2 points. Put the limit for your course in the calculator. If your ATAR is close to 99.95, ask the university how it treats ranks above that.

What does an ATAR of 80 mean?

It puts you in the top 20% of your age group – all young people of your age, not only those who finished Year 12. It is a rank, not a percentage mark.

What is a lowest selection rank?

It is the lowest selection rank (ATAR plus adjustments) among students admitted to the course the previous year. It includes adjustment points; the lowest ATAR does not. If yours is a little lower, you may still get in, because the number changes every year.
