Your numbers

Quick answer (2026-27): the government adds 50 cents for every $1 you put into super after tax, up to $500, if your total income is $49,293 or less; it falls to $0 at $64,293. At $55,000 income the most you can get is about $310. Earn $37,000 or less and LISTO can also refund up to $500 of the tax on your before-tax super.

How it works

Example first. You earn $55,000 and put $1,000 of your own after-tax money into super. Your income is $5,707 over $49,293. The maximum falls by 3.333 cents for each of those dollars: $5,707 × 0.03333 = $190. So the maximum is $500 − $190 = $310. Half of your $1,000 is $500, which is more than $310, so you get $310.

  • Total income = assessable income + reportable fringe benefits + reportable employer super contributions, minus business deductions.
  • Maximum = $500 − (total income − $49,293) × 0.03333, never below $0.
  • Co-contribution = the smaller of half your after-tax contribution and the maximum.
  • LISTO = a refund of the tax on before-tax contributions, up to $500, if adjusted taxable income is $37,000 or less.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Contribute before 30 June. The money must reach your fund by the end of the financial year.
  • Don't claim a deduction for it. If you claim a tax deduction, the contribution no longer counts for the co-contribution.
  • Lodge your tax return. Nothing is paid until you do.
  • Give your fund your TFN. Without your tax file number the fund can't accept the payment.
  • Only put in what you need. Above the amount that earns the maximum, extra money gets no match (it still helps your super).

Super co-contribution FAQ

How much is the super co-contribution in 2026-27?

Up to $500. You get 50 cents for each $1 you put into super after tax. If your total income is $49,293 or less, $1,000 earns the full $500. Above that the maximum shrinks, and at $64,293 it reaches $0.

How much should I put in to get the full $500?

$1,000 of personal after-tax contributions, if your total income is $49,293 or less. Putting in more doesn't increase the co-contribution. At $55,000 income the maximum is about $310, so $620 is enough.

Do I need to apply for the co-contribution?

No. Lodge your tax return and make sure your fund has your tax file number – the fund needs it before it can accept the payment. The ATO works out the amount and pays it into your super fund.

What is LISTO?

LISTO (low income super tax offset) gives back the tax paid on your before-tax super contributions, up to $500 a year. You can get it if your adjusted taxable income is $37,000 or less. The ATO pays it into your fund.

Can I get both the co-contribution and LISTO?

Yes. Someone earning $35,000 who puts in $1,000 after tax can get the full $500 co-contribution, and LISTO of up to $500 on top.

Does salary sacrifice count for the co-contribution?

No. Salary sacrifice is a before-tax (concessional) contribution. Only personal after-tax contributions you don't claim a deduction for earn the co-contribution. Salary sacrifice is added to your total income for the income test.

Why did I get less than I expected?

Common reasons: your total income was above $49,293, you claimed a tax deduction for the contribution, less than 10% of your income came from work or a business, your total super balance was too high, or you haven't lodged your tax return yet.
