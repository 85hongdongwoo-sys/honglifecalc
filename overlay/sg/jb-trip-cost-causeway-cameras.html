Short answer: a car crossing at Woodlands pays S$0.80 in Singapore tolls (out only); at Tuas it pays S$2.10 each way. Malaysia adds a RM20 road charge per entry plus its own tolls. This page adds those to the petrol you burn, compares it with the cheaper petrol you can buy in JB, and shows live LTA checkpoint cameras.

How it works

  • Trip costs = Singapore tolls + RM20 road charge + Malaysian tolls (converted to S$) + petrol for the drive at the Singapore price
  • Petrol saving = litres bought in JB × (Singapore price − JB price in S$)
  • Result = petrol saving − trip costs

The Malaysian price is read live from data.gov.my and converted with the latest European Central Bank reference rates (MYR and SGD cross rate).

Rules to remember

  • ¾ tank rule: your tank must be at least three-quarters full when you leave Singapore.
  • Malaysia VEP: foreign cars need an activated VEP RFID tag; driving in without one can bring an immediate RM300 fine. A RM20 road charge applies to each entry.
  • Pay your tolls: insert your stored-value card at the booth even if the barrier is lifted – not paying can lead to fines of up to S$1,000.

Worked example

Woodlands, 60 km return at 7 L/100 km uses 4.2 litres. At S$2.80 that is S$11.76. Add S$0.80 Singapore toll and RM20 road charge (about S$6.27 at RM3.19 per S$1) and the trip costs about S$18.83 before Malaysian tolls. Buying 30 litres of RON97 at RM4.00 costs RM120 (about S$37.60) instead of S$84.00 in Singapore – a saving of about S$46.40, so you come out about S$27.58 ahead.

The example uses RM3.19 per S$1 and RON97 at RM4.00 (week of 19 November 2026). The live calculator uses this week’s figures.

FAQ

How much is the toll to drive from Singapore to JB?

On the Singapore side a car pays S$0.80 when leaving through Woodlands Checkpoint and nothing when coming back in. Through Tuas Checkpoint a car pays S$2.10 to leave and S$2.10 to come back. You pay with a NETS CashCard, Motoring Card or CEPAS card at the immigration booth, even if the barrier is already up. Malaysia charges its own tolls on top.

What is the RM20 road charge?

Foreign-registered cars entering Malaysia through the Johor Causeway or the Second Link must have an activated VEP (Vehicle Entry Permit) RFID tag and pay a RM20 road charge for each entry. The RFID sticker itself costs RM10 once.

Do I really need ¾ of a tank to leave Singapore?

Yes. LTA requires the fuel tank of every vehicle leaving Singapore by road to be at least three-quarters full, including hybrids. So you cannot drive across on an empty tank – the petrol you can save is limited to what you top up in JB.

Which Malaysian fuel price does the calculator use?

It reads the weekly prices published by Malaysia’s Ministry of Finance on data.gov.my. RON97 is the default. The RON95 option uses the unsubsidised RON95 price, not the BUDI95 subsidised price (RM1.99), which is only for eligible Malaysians.

Is driving to JB just for petrol worth it?

Only if you buy enough. With the default numbers (Woodlands, 60 km return, 7 L/100 km, S$2.80 in Singapore) the drive itself burns about 4.2 litres and the tolls and road charge add a few dollars, so you need to top up a decent amount in JB for the cheaper petrol to cover the trip.
