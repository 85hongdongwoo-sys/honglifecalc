Lottery tax in 2027: the short answer

A US lottery prize over $5,000 has 24% federal tax withheld at payout, but the prize is ordinary income, so a large win is taxed mostly at 37% — the 2027 top rate starts above $661,375 (single) or $793,650 (married filing jointly). The gap is due when you file.

  1. Subtract the ticket price from the prize.
  2. Add the winnings to your other taxable income for the year.
  3. Federal tax on the prize = tax on the total − tax on your other income alone (2027 brackets).
  4. Add your state rate (0% in states such as Florida or Texas, which have no state income tax).

Understanding the lottery tax calculator — concept and principle

This calculator estimates the tax on a lottery prize and what you keep. It covers the United States (federal income tax at 2027 or 2026 rates plus your state rate), South Korea (fixed withholding rates) and other countries with a flat rate you enter. It also shows how much is withheld when you collect and, in the US, what may still be due at filing.

How it is calculated

  • Taxable winnings = prize − ticket price.
  • US federal: tax on (other income + winnings) − tax on other income alone, using the 2026 brackets for single filers (10% to 12,400; 12% to 50,400; 22% to 105,700; 24% to 201,775; 32% to 256,225; 35% to 640,600; 37% above) or married filing jointly. State tax = your rate × winnings. Withholding = 24% of winnings over 5,000 USD.
  • South Korea: prizes up to 2 million won are not taxed; above that, 20% income tax on the first 300 million won and 30% above, plus local income tax of 10% of the income tax.
  • Other: flat rate × winnings.

The principle

In the US, a lottery prize is ordinary income. It sits on top of your other income, so most of it falls in the highest brackets. The 24% withheld is only a prepayment; the real tax is settled on your return. Korea taxes lottery prizes separately at fixed rates and withholds the full amount.

Worked example

Default inputs: United States, single, prize 1,000,000 USD, ticket 2 USD, other income 60,000 USD, state 4%.

  1. Winnings = 999,998 USD; total taxable income = 1,059,998 USD, top bracket 37%.
  2. Federal tax on the winnings = tax(1,059,998) − tax(60,000) ≈ 340,244.51 USD.
  3. State tax = 4% × 999,998 = 39,999.92 USD. Total tax = 380,244.43 USD (effective 38%).
  4. Prize after tax = 1,000,000 − 380,244.43 = 619,755.57 USD.
  5. Withheld at collection = 24% × 999,998 = 239,999.52 USD, so about 100,244.99 USD more is due at filing.

How to read the result

For a large US prize, the gap between 24% withholding and a 37% top rate is a common surprise; set the difference aside. State rates vary widely, and some states do not tax lottery winnings. In Korea, the amount you receive is final after withholding.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the 24% withholding is the final tax.
  • Forgetting state or local tax.
  • Ignoring that other income moves the winnings into a higher bracket.
  • Treating gifts of winnings to family as tax-free; gift tax rules may apply.
Keep in mind: Simplified estimate; deductions for gambling losses, credits and gift tax are not included. Tax law changes. This is not tax advice; consult a tax professional.

Frequently asked questions

How much federal tax is taken from lottery winnings in 2027?

The payer withholds 24% of winnings over $5,000. Your final federal tax uses the 2027 brackets, so for large prizes most of the money is taxed at 37% and you owe the difference when you file.

At what income does the 37% bracket start in 2027?

Above $661,375 of taxable income for single filers and above $793,650 for married couples filing jointly (Rev. Proc. 2026-35).

How much would I owe on a $1 million lottery win in 2027?

With $60,000 of other income as a single filer, federal tax on the prize is about $339,000 (33.9%), of which $240,000 is withheld at payout; state tax comes on top.

How much tax do you pay on a 1 million dollar lottery win?

With the defaults, about 38% in total (federal plus 4% state), leaving roughly 620,000 USD.

Are lottery winnings taxed in Korea?

Yes. Above 2 million won, 22% (including local tax) applies up to 300 million won and 33% on the part above.

Is the ticket price deductible?

The cost of the winning ticket reduces the taxable amount, as used here.
