Understanding the AMT calculator — concept and principle

This calculator estimates the US alternative minimum tax (AMT) for 2027 or 2026 (choose the tax year). From your regular taxable income, AMT adjustments and regular tax, it finds alternative minimum taxable income (AMTI), the exemption after phase-out, the tentative minimum tax and the AMT you owe. Use it to check whether large preference items, such as incentive stock options or certain deductions, might trigger AMT.

How it is calculated

  • AMTI = regular taxable income + AMT adjustments and preferences.
  • 2026 exemption: $90,100 (single, head of household), $140,200 (joint), $70,100 (married separately), reduced by 50 cents per dollar of AMTI above $500,000 ($1,000,000 joint).
  • Tentative minimum tax = 26% of (AMTI − exemption) up to $244,500 ($122,250 married separately), 28% above.
  • AMT = tentative minimum tax − regular tax, if positive.

Simplified: all income is treated as ordinary, and credits are left out. If regular tax is entered as 0, the page estimates it from the 2026 brackets.

The principle

The AMT is a parallel tax system meant to ensure that taxpayers with many tax preferences still pay a minimum amount. You compute tax twice: once under the regular rules and once under AMT rules, which add back certain preferences and use a flat 26%/28% structure with a large exemption. You pay whichever is higher; the difference is the AMT. The exemption phases out at higher incomes, which is why AMT mainly affects upper-middle and high incomes with large adjustments.

Worked example

Default inputs: single, taxable income $250,000, adjustments $200,000, regular tax estimated.

  1. AMTI = 250,000 + 200,000 = $450,000. This is below $500,000, so the full $90,100 exemption applies.
  2. AMT base = 450,000 − 90,100 = $359,900.
  3. Tentative tax = 26% × 244,500 + 28% × (359,900 − 244,500) = 63,570 + 32,312 = $95,882.
  4. Regular tax (2026 brackets, estimated) = $56,456.
  5. AMT = 95,882 − 56,456 = $39,426; total federal tax $95,882. The exemption would be fully phased out at AMTI $680,200.

How to read the result

If AMT is 0, the regular tax is higher and you pay no AMT. A positive figure is the extra tax on top of regular tax. Large one-time adjustments, such as the bargain element of exercised incentive stock options, are a common cause. Part of AMT paid may later be recovered as a credit in some cases, which this tool does not model.

Common mistakes

  • Entering adjusted gross income instead of taxable income.
  • Ignoring that qualified dividends and long-term gains get lower rates under the real AMT rules.
  • Assuming the exemption never phases out.
Keep in mind: This is a simplified estimate. The real calculation on Form 6251 has many special rules. Consult the IRS instructions or a qualified tax professional. This is not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the AMT exemption for 2027?

$93,000 for single and head-of-household filers, $144,700 for married filing jointly and $72,350 for married filing separately (Rev. Proc. 2026-35).

When does the AMT exemption phase out in 2027?

It is reduced by 25% of AMTI above $515,950 for single filers (gone at $701,950) and above $1,031,900 for joint filers (gone at $1,321,300).

At what amount does the 28% AMT rate apply in 2027?

On AMT base (AMTI minus exemption) above $252,500, or above $126,250 if married filing separately. Below that the rate is 26%.

Who has to pay AMT?

Anyone whose tentative minimum tax exceeds their regular tax, typically those with large preference items.

What triggers AMT most often?

Exercising incentive stock options and other large adjustments added back under AMT rules.

What is the 2026 AMT exemption for single filers?

$90,100, phased out by 50 cents per dollar of AMTI above $500,000.
