Illinois state tax in 2026, in one line

Illinois taxes income at a flat 4.95% after an exemption of $2,925 per person claimed (up from $2,850 in 2025). One exemption on $60,000 leaves $57,075 taxed, so the state tax is about $2,825 a year.

  1. Start from Illinois base income (federal AGI with Illinois additions and subtractions).
  2. Subtract $2,925 for yourself, your spouse if filing jointly, and each dependent.
  3. Multiply what is left by 4.95%.

Understanding the Illinois income tax calculator — concept and principle

This calculator estimates Illinois state income tax for 2026 from your Illinois base income and number of exemptions. It shows the tax, the effective rate, the exemption allowance, net income taxed, tax per paycheck and income after Illinois tax. Use it for budgeting, checking withholding or comparing take-home pay across states.

How it is calculated

  • Exemption allowance = $2,925 × number of exemptions (2026).
  • Net income = Illinois base income − exemption allowance.
  • Tax = 4.95% × net income.
  • Effective rate = tax ÷ base income.
  • Tax per paycheck = yearly tax ÷ number of paychecks.

Base income starts from federal AGI with Illinois additions and subtractions. Credits such as the property tax credit are not included.

The principle

Illinois uses a flat income tax: every dollar of net income is taxed at the same 4.95% rate. The exemption allowance shields a fixed amount per person, so the effective rate is slightly below 4.95% and rises toward it as income grows. The allowance is adjusted for inflation; for 2026 it rose from $2,850 to $2,925. Your employer's withholding depends on the allowances you claim on Form IL-W-4, so actual paycheck amounts may differ.

Worked example

Default inputs: base income $75,000, 1 exemption, 26 paychecks.

  1. Exemption allowance = $2,925.
  2. Net income = 75,000 − 2,925 = $72,075.
  3. Tax = 4.95% × 72,075 ≈ $3,568.
  4. Effective rate = 3,568 ÷ 75,000 ≈ 4.76%.
  5. Per paycheck = 3,567.71 ÷ 26 ≈ $137.22; income after Illinois tax ≈ $71,432.

How to read the result

Because the rate is flat, each extra $1,000 of income adds $49.50 of Illinois tax. Compare the tax per paycheck with your pay stub to check withholding. Federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare are separate and not included.

Common mistakes

  • Using gross wages instead of Illinois base income.
  • Forgetting extra exemptions for a spouse or dependents.
  • Using the 2025 allowance of $2,850 for 2026.
  • Expecting withholding to match exactly; it depends on your IL-W-4.
Keep in mind: Some exemptions and credits have income limits and special rules. Check the Illinois Department of Revenue instructions or a tax professional. This is not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Illinois income tax rate for 2026?

A flat 4.95% on net income.

Does Illinois have income tax brackets?

No. Illinois has one flat rate, 4.95%, for every income level; only the $2,925 exemption per person makes the effective rate lower on small incomes.

How much is the Illinois exemption for 2026?

$2,925 per exemption (you, your spouse on a joint return, and each dependent), up from $2,850 in 2025.

How much Illinois tax is taken from a biweekly paycheck?

On $60,000 a year with one exemption, about $108.66 per biweekly paycheck: ($60,000 − $2,925) × 4.95% ÷ 26.

What is the Illinois income tax rate?

A flat 4.95% of net income.

How much is the Illinois exemption for 2026?

$2,925 per exemption.

Does Illinois have tax brackets?

No. It uses one flat rate for all individual income.
