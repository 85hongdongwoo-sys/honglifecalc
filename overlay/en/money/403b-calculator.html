2027 403(b) limits in one minute

In 2027 a 403(b) takes up to $25,500 of elective deferrals. On top you may add the age catch-up ($8,500 at 50+, or $11,750 at ages 60–63) and, with 15+ years at a qualifying employer, the 15-year catch-up of up to $3,000. The most one person can defer is therefore $37,000 (50+) or $40,250 (ages 60–63).

  1. Start with the base limit: $25,500.
  2. 15-year catch-up = the smallest of $3,000; $15,000 minus 15-year catch-ups used before; $5,000 × years of service minus earlier deferrals to this employer.
  3. Add the age catch-up for your age on 31 December 2027.
  4. Your deferrals plus employer money must stay within $75,000 or 100% of includible pay (catch-ups are outside this cap).

Understanding the 403(b) calculator — concept and principle

This calculator checks how much you can defer into a 403(b) plan in 2026, including the age catch-up and the special 15-year catch-up, adds your employer's contribution and projects the balance at retirement. Use it if you work for a public school, hospital, church or other eligible employer.

How it is calculated

  • Base elective deferral limit: $25,500 in 2027 ($24,500 in 2026).
  • Age catch-up: $8,500 in 2027 ($8,000 in 2026) at age 50 or over at year-end, or $11,750 in 2027 ($11,250 in 2026) at ages 60–63.
  • 15-year catch-up (15+ years with a qualifying employer, if the plan allows): the lesser of $3,000; $15,000 minus earlier 15-year catch-ups; or $5,000 × years of service minus earlier elective deferrals to that employer.
  • Maximum deferral = $24,500 + 15-year catch-up + age catch-up, but not more than your pay.
  • Annual additions (your deferrals plus employer money) are limited to the lesser of $75,000 in 2027 ($72,000 in 2026) or your includible pay.
  • Projection: balance × (1 + r)^t + yearly contributions × ((1 + r)^t − 1) ÷ r.

The principle

A 403(b) lets employees of certain tax-exempt organisations save for retirement with tax advantages. The IRS sets yearly limits; catch-ups let older or long-serving employees save more. Deferrals above the base limit count first toward the 15-year catch-up and then toward the age catch-up. The projection is a future value: the balance compounds and each year's contributions form a growing annuity.

Worked example

Default inputs: pay $85,000, age 52, planned deferral $30,000, 15-year catch-up yes, 16 years of service, earlier deferrals $70,000, earlier 15-year catch-ups $0, employer 4%, balance $150,000, return 6%, 13 years.

  1. 15-year catch-up = min(3,000; 15,000 − 0; 5,000 × 16 − 70,000 = 10,000) = $3,000.
  2. Age catch-up (50+) = $8,000. Maximum = 24,500 + 3,000 + 8,000 = $35,500.
  3. Your $30,000 is within the limit: allowed deferral $30,000, nothing above the limit.
  4. Employer = 4% × 85,000 = $3,400; total in this year $33,400, under the $72,000 additions limit.
  5. Projection: 150,000 × 1.06¹³ + 33,400 × (1.06¹³ − 1) ÷ 0.06 ≈ $950,603.

How to read the result

"Above the limit" should be 0; any excess deferral must be corrected with the plan. The projection is a planning figure in future dollars, not adjusted for inflation, and assumes the same contribution and a steady return every year. Small changes in the return assumption change the result a lot over long periods.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming every 403(b) offers the 15-year catch-up; the plan must allow it.
  • Using years of service with all employers instead of the current qualifying employer.
  • Forgetting that the employer's contributions count toward the $72,000 additions limit.
Keep in mind: Limits change every year and depend on plan rules; higher earners may face extra rules for catch-up contributions. Check with your plan administrator or a qualified tax adviser. This is not financial or tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 403(b) limit for 2027?

The 2027 elective deferral limit is $25,500. Add $8,500 at age 50+ (or $11,750 at ages 60–63), plus up to $3,000 more under the 15-year rule if you qualify.

What is the maximum 403(b) contribution with both catch-ups in 2027?

$37,000 if you are 50–59 or 64+ ($25,500 + $3,000 + $8,500), or $40,250 at ages 60–63 ($25,500 + $3,000 + $11,750).

Is the 2027 annual additions limit for a 403(b) still $72,000?

No. For 2027 the limit on your deferrals plus employer contributions rises to $75,000 (from $72,000), or 100% of includible compensation if lower.

What is the 403(b) limit for 2026?

$24,500 in elective deferrals, plus catch-ups if you qualify.

Can I use both the 15-year and the age catch-up?

Yes, if eligible; excess deferrals count toward the 15-year catch-up first.
