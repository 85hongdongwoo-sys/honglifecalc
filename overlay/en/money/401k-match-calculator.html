2027 401(k) limits at a glance

For 2027 you can put up to $25,500 of your own pay into a 401(k) — $1,000 more than in 2026. At age 50+ the catch-up is $8,500 (total $34,000), and at ages 60–63 it is $11,750 instead (total $37,250). Employer match does not count toward $25,500; it counts toward the overall $75,000 cap.

Understanding the 401(k) employer match calculator — concept and principle

Many US employers add money to your 401(k) when you contribute, for example "50% of what you put in, up to 6% of salary". That match is part of your pay, and contributing too little leaves it unclaimed. This calculator shows your contribution, the employer match, the yearly total, the 2027 (or 2026) employee deferral limit for your age, a simple projection of the balance at retirement, and any match you are missing.

How it is calculated

  • Your contribution = salary × your % (capped at the age-based limit)
  • Employer match = min(your %, match cap %) × match rate × salary
  • Total per year = your contribution + match
  • Limit: $25,500 in 2027 ($24,500 in 2026), plus an $8,500 catch-up at 50+ ($8,000 in 2026), or $11,750 instead at ages 60–63 ($11,250 in 2026)
  • Future value = yearly total × ((1 + r)^years − 1) ÷ r (an ordinary annuity: equal deposits at each year end, no raises)
  • Match left on the table = max(0, cap % − your %) × match rate × salary

The principle

The match formula rewards contributions only up to the cap, so the first few percent you save earn an instant return equal to the match rate (50% here) before any investment growth. The projection is the standard future value of a series of equal yearly payments growing at a constant rate. It deliberately ignores salary raises, fees, taxes, vesting schedules and market ups and downs, so treat it as a rough picture of how steady saving compounds.

Worked example

Default: $80,000 salary, 6% contribution, 50% match up to 6%, age 35, 7% return, 30 years.

  1. Your contribution: 80,000 × 6% = $4,800 (under the $24,500 limit).
  2. Match: min(6%, 6%) × 50% × 80,000 = $2,400 a year.
  3. Total: 4,800 + 2,400 = $7,200 per year.
  4. Growth factor: (1.07³⁰ − 1) ÷ 0.07 ≈ 94.461.
  5. Value after 30 years: 7,200 × 94.461 ≈ $680,118. Match left on the table: $0.

How to read the result

If "match left on the table" is above zero, raising your contribution to the cap is often the first step people consider, because no other investment returns 50% or 100% immediately. Check your plan's vesting schedule too: employer money may only become fully yours after a few years of service. Inflation means the projected balance buys less in today's dollars.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the match is "up to 6%" of your contribution rather than of salary.
  • Hitting the annual limit early in the year; some plans then stop matching for the remaining pay periods (ask about a "true-up").
  • Treating a 7% return as guaranteed.
Keep in mind: This is an educational estimate, not financial or tax advice. Plan rules, vesting, limits for future years and investment results vary; check your plan documents.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 401(k) limit for 2027?

The employee deferral limit for 2027 is $25,500, up from $24,500 in 2026. Workers aged 50 and over can add $8,500 (total $34,000), and those aged 60–63 can add $11,750 instead (total $37,250).

What is the total 401(k) limit including the employer match in 2027?

Your contributions plus employer contributions (match and profit sharing) are capped at $75,000 in 2027, or 100% of pay if lower. Catch-up contributions come on top of that cap.

How much do I need to contribute to max out my 401(k) on an $80,000 salary?

About 31.9% of pay ($25,500 ÷ $80,000). At 50+, $34,000 is 42.5% of an $80,000 salary.

What is the 2026 401(k) contribution limit?

$24,500 for employee deferrals, with an $8,000 catch-up at 50+ and $11,250 for ages 60–63.

Does the employer match count toward my $24,500 limit?

No. The employee deferral limit covers only your own contributions; a higher overall limit applies to combined contributions.

What does "50% up to 6%" mean?

The employer adds 50 cents per dollar you contribute, on contributions up to 6% of salary, so the most it adds is 3% of salary.
