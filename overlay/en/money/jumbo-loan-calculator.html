Is it a jumbo loan in 2027?

From 1 January 2027 a one-unit mortgage above $855,400 is a jumbo loan in most US counties (2026: $832,750, +2.72%). In high-cost areas the limit can go up to $1,283,100; in Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and the US Virgin Islands the baseline is $1,283,100 and the ceiling $1,924,650.

Understanding the jumbo loan calculator — concept and principle

A jumbo loan is a US mortgage larger than the conforming loan limit, the maximum that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can buy. This calculator tells you whether your loan would be jumbo, estimates the monthly principal-and-interest payment and shows how big a down payment would keep the loan conforming.

How it is calculated

  • Loan amount L = home price × (1 − down payment %).
  • Loan type: jumbo if L is above the conforming limit you enter, otherwise conforming.
  • Monthly payment = L × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n), with i = annual rate ÷ 12 and n = years × 12.
  • Down payment to stay conforming = price − limit (and as a share of the price).
  • Total interest = payment × n − L.

The principle

The Federal Housing Finance Agency sets the conforming limit each year. For 2027 the one-unit baseline is 855,400 USD, and in high-cost areas it can reach 1,283,100 USD (150% of the baseline); for 2026 they were 832,750 and 1,249,125 USD. Loans above the limit cannot be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so lenders keep them or sell them privately and set their own rules for credit score, cash reserves and down payment. The payment formula is the standard level-payment amortization: each payment covers that month's interest and repays a little principal, so the balance reaches zero at the end of the term.

Worked example

Example with the 2026 limit: price 1,100,000 USD, 20% down, limit 832,750 USD, 6.75% a year, 30 years.

  1. Down payment = 1,100,000 × 20% = 220,000 USD, loan = 880,000 USD.
  2. 880,000 is above 832,750, so it is a jumbo loan, 47,250 USD over the limit.
  3. i = 6.75% ÷ 12 = 0.5625%, n = 360. Payment = 880,000 × 0.005625 ÷ (1 − 1.005625^−360) ≈ 5,708 USD a month.
  4. Total interest = 5,707.66 × 360 − 880,000 ≈ 1,174,759 USD.
  5. To stay conforming you would need 1,100,000 − 832,750 = 267,250 USD down, about 24.3%.

How to read the result

If you are only slightly over the limit, a larger down payment or a lower price may let you use a conforming loan with easier rules. Jumbo rates can be similar to or different from conforming rates depending on the market, so get quotes for both. Remember that property tax, homeowners insurance and any HOA fees are added on top of this payment.

Common mistakes

  • Using the national baseline when your county has a higher high-cost limit.
  • Treating principal and interest as the full housing payment.
  • Forgetting that jumbo lenders often require larger cash reserves.
  • Using last year's conforming limit.
Keep in mind: Limits change every year and vary by county; check the FHFA table for your area. This is an estimate, not financial advice or a loan offer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the conforming loan limit for 2027?

FHFA set the 2027 baseline one-unit conforming loan limit at $855,400, up from $832,750 in 2026. The high-cost ceiling is $1,283,100.

What home price makes a loan jumbo with 20% down in 2027?

Above $1,069,250 in a baseline county, because 80% of $1,069,250 equals the $855,400 limit.

When do the 2027 loan limits take effect?

For loans delivered to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac from 1 January 2027 (FHFA announcement of 24 November 2026).

What is the conforming loan limit for 2026?

The FHFA baseline for a one-unit home is 832,750 USD, with higher limits in designated high-cost areas up to 1,249,125 USD.

Do jumbo loans need 20% down?

Not always. Each lender sets its own minimum, and requirements vary with credit score and loan size.

Is a jumbo loan rate always higher?

No. Jumbo rates move with the market and lender appetite and can be close to conforming rates.
