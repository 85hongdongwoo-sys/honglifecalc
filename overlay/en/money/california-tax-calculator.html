Understanding the California income tax calculator — concept and principle

This calculator estimates California state income tax from your California adjusted gross income, filing status, itemized deductions and exemption credits. It gives taxable income, the deduction used, tax from the rate schedule, the Behavioral Health Services Tax on very high incomes, the effective and marginal rates and the monthly amount. Figures use the 2026 FTB schedules by default; switch to 2025 for returns filed in 2026.

How it is calculated

  • Taxable income = California AGI − the larger of the standard deduction ($5,706 single or married filing separately; $11,412 joint, head of household or surviving spouse) and itemized deductions.
  • Tax from the Franchise Tax Board rate schedules: nine rates from 1% to 12.3%; Schedule X (single), Y (joint, brackets about twice as wide) and Z (head of household).
  • Minus exemption credits.
  • Plus the 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax on taxable income above $1,000,000.
  • Effective rate = tax ÷ AGI; marginal rate = rate on the next dollar.

The principle

California uses a progressive income tax: each slice of taxable income is taxed at the rate of its own bracket, so moving into a higher bracket only raises the rate on the income inside that bracket. That is why the effective rate is always lower than the marginal rate. Brackets are adjusted for inflation each year. Unlike federal tax, California taxes capital gains as ordinary income and uses exemption credits instead of personal exemptions.

Worked example

Default inputs: single, California AGI $95,000, standard deduction, no exemption credits.

  1. Deduction used = $5,706 (standard, larger than the $0 itemized).
  2. Taxable income = 95,000 − 5,706 = $89,294.
  3. Tax from Schedule X ≈ $4,743, with the top slice taxed at 9.3%.
  4. No Behavioral Health Services Tax (taxable income below $1,000,000).
  5. Effective rate = 4,743 ÷ 95,000 ≈ 4.99%; about $395 a month.

How to read the result

The effective rate is the share of income that goes to state tax; the marginal rate is what an extra dollar of income would cost. Exemption credits for yourself and dependents reduce the result further, and other credits may apply. For budgeting, add federal tax, Social Security, Medicare and State Disability Insurance, which are not included here.

Common mistakes

  • Entering federal AGI when California adjustments differ.
  • Forgetting to enter exemption credits, which overstates the tax.
  • Assuming the marginal rate applies to all income.
  • Using 2025 brackets for 2026 income without checking the FTB's updated schedules.
Keep in mind: This is a simplified estimate based on the published schedules. Check the FTB instructions or a qualified tax professional before filing. This is not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

What are the California tax brackets for 2026?

For single filers: 1% up to $11,456, 2% to $27,157, 4% to $42,861, 6% to $59,498, 8% to $75,197, 9.3% to $384,109, 10.3% to $460,927, 11.3% to $768,213 and 12.3% above. Joint brackets are twice as wide.

What is the California standard deduction for 2026?

$5,900 for single or married filing separately and $11,800 for married filing jointly, surviving spouses and heads of household.

What are the California tax brackets for 2026 married filing jointly?

1% up to $22,912, 2% to $54,314, 4% to $85,722, 6% to $118,996, 8% to $150,394, 9.3% to $768,218, 10.3% to $921,854, 11.3% to $1,536,426 and 12.3% above, plus 1% on taxable income over $1 million.

How much California tax on $100,000?

About $4,911 for a single filer in 2026 using the standard deduction and the $158 personal exemption credit.

What is the top California income tax rate?

12.3% in the schedules, plus 1% on taxable income above $1,000,000.

Why is my effective rate lower than my bracket?

Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate.

Does California tax capital gains differently?

No. They are taxed as ordinary income at the same rates.
