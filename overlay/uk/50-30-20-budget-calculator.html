How the 50/30/20 split works

Example first. Your take-home pay is £3,200 a month.

  • Needs: £3,200 × 50% = £1,600. Rent, bills, food, travel to work, minimum debt payments.
  • Wants: £3,200 × 30% = £960. Eating out, hobbies, subscriptions, holidays.
  • Savings and debt: £3,200 × 20% = £640. That is £7,680 in a year.

Take-home pay means the money that arrives in your account after tax, National Insurance and pension. Always start from that number, not your salary.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Using your salary instead of take-home pay. The split only works on money you actually receive.
  • Hiding wants inside needs. A basic phone plan is a need; the newest phone on contract is partly a want.
  • Forgetting yearly bills. Car insurance, TV licence and birthdays: divide by 12 and add them to your monthly budget.
  • Giving up when needs are over 50%. Change the split instead – 60/30/10 still gets you saving.
  • Saving what's left at the end of the month. Move the 20% on payday with a standing order.

50/30/20 budget FAQ

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

It splits your take-home pay into three parts: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and paying off debt. On £3,200 a month that is £1,600 for needs, £960 for wants and £640 for savings.

How do I use 50/30/20 on a £30,000 salary?

In 2026/27 a £30,000 salary in England, Wales or Northern Ireland gives about £2,093 a month after income tax and National Insurance (no pension or student loan). That splits into £1,047 for needs, £628 for wants and £419 for savings – about £5,024 a year.

What counts as a need and what is a want?

A need is something you must pay even in a bad month: rent or mortgage, council tax, energy, water, basic food, getting to work, insurance and minimum debt payments. A want is something you could cut: takeaways, holidays, streaming, new clothes you don't need, gym upgrades.

Is 50/30/20 realistic with UK rents?

Often not. The ONS says average private rent was £1,411 a month in the UK and £2,350 in London in October 2026. On £2,093 take-home, average UK rent alone is 67% of pay. Many people use 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 instead – the key is to save something every month.

Does the 20% include pension?

Workplace pension is usually taken off before your take-home pay, so it's already counted. The 20% is extra: emergency fund, savings goals, and overpaying debt like credit cards.

Should I save or pay off debt first?

Pay at least the minimum on every debt (that's a need). Then many people build a small emergency fund first, then put the 20% towards the most expensive debt, such as credit cards, before saving more.
