Your numbers

Quick answer (2026/27): Child Benefit is £27.05 a week for the eldest child and £17.90 for each other child – £1,407 a year for one child, £2,337 for two. If the higher earner's adjusted net income is over £60,000, 1% is paid back for every £200 above it, so all of it goes at £80,000.

How it works, in plain words

Example first. You have two children and earn £68,000. Child Benefit is £44.95 a week, or £2,337 a year. You are £8,000 over £60,000. £8,000 ÷ £200 = 40, so the charge is 40%: £934. You keep about £1,403.

  • Adjusted net income is worked out before any Personal Allowance and after certain tax reliefs, such as pension contributions and Gift Aid (use the gross amounts).
  • High Income Child Benefit Charge is an extra tax the higher earner pays to give back some or all of the benefit.
  • Charge % = (income − £60,000) ÷ £200, rounded down, up to 100%.
  • Charge = yearly Child Benefit × charge %.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Always claim for a new baby. Even if you won't keep the money, the claim protects the stay-at-home parent's State Pension record.
  • Tell HMRC if you go over £60,000. The higher earner pays the charge through their tax code (PAYE) or through Self Assessment. Self Assessment is needed if you already file a return, or if you pay later than 31 January after the tax year.
  • Use pension contributions. Between £60,000 and £80,000, every £200 into a pension saves 1% of the benefit on top of normal tax relief.
  • Count all income. Bonuses, rental profit and savings interest all count towards adjusted net income.
  • Earning over £80,000? Don't just stop the claim. Stay registered and opt out of the payments instead, so you keep the National Insurance credits.

Child Benefit FAQ

How much is Child Benefit in 2026/27?

£27.05 a week for the eldest or only child and £17.90 a week for each other child, from 6 April 2026. For one child that is £1,407 a year; for two children £2,337 a year.

How much Child Benefit do you get for 2 children?

£44.95 a week (£27.05 + £17.90). Over a year that is £2,337, or about £195 a month.

At what income do you lose Child Benefit?

You start paying it back when the higher earner's adjusted net income is over £60,000. The charge is 1% for every £200 above that, so at £80,000 or more all of it is paid back.

How much High Income Child Benefit Charge will I pay on £70,000?

At £70,000 the charge is 50%. With two children you get £2,337 and pay back £1,168, so you keep £1,169. With one child you keep £704.

Can paying into a pension stop the Child Benefit charge?

Yes. Pension contributions and Gift Aid lower your adjusted net income. Someone on £70,000 with two children who adds £10,000 (gross) to a pension drops to £60,000 and keeps all £2,337.

Should I still claim Child Benefit if I earn over £80,000?

Usually yes. You can stay registered but choose not to receive the payments. Then you pay no charge, and you still get the National Insurance credits that count towards your State Pension, plus a National Insurance number for your child.

Is Child Benefit going up in 2027?

Yes. Budget 2026 set the 2027/28 rates at £28.00 a week for the eldest child and £18.55 for each other child, from April 2027. For two children that is £2,420 a year.

Does the charge use household income?

No. It looks at each person's own adjusted net income. Only the partner with the higher income pays the charge, even if the other partner claims the benefit.
