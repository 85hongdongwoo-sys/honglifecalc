Fuel Cost Calculator

Enter the distance, your car’s fuel consumption and the fuel price.

Your trip

Quick answer (2026): Fuel cost = miles ÷ mpg × 4.546 litres × pump price. A 300-mile trip at 45 mpg uses 30.3 litres, which is £52.73 at £1.74 a litre (17.6p a mile; HMRC's December 2026 basis is petrol 174.1p and diesel 199.2p). Fuel duty is 52.95p a litre until at least 31 December 2026.

How the fuel cost is calculated

UK drivers usually think in miles and miles per gallon, while pumps charge per litre. The calculator converts everything to litres:

  • Gallons used = miles ÷ mpg
  • Litres used = gallons × 4.54609 (one UK imperial gallon)
  • Fuel cost = litres × price per litre
  • Cost per mile = price per litre × 4.54609 ÷ mpg

If you use metric units, litres = km × L/100 km ÷ 100. One mile is exactly 1.609344 km, so L/100 km = 282.48 ÷ mpg.

Ways to cut the fuel bill

  • Slow down on motorways: fuel use rises quickly above about 60 mph because air resistance grows with the square of speed.
  • Check tyre pressures against the figure on the door pillar or fuel flap, especially before a long, loaded trip.
  • Remove roof bars and boxes when not in use and clear heavy items from the boot.
  • Drive smoothly: anticipate traffic, change up early and avoid hard acceleration.
  • Compare forecourts: motorway services are usually the most expensive place to fill up; supermarket sites often charge less.

Common mistakes

  • Using a US mpg figure from an American website – it understates UK mpg by about 17%.
  • Typing the price in pence (150) instead of pounds (1.50).
  • Forgetting the return leg – set Return journey to double the distance.
  • Using the brochure (WLTP) economy instead of your real-world average.

Business mileage

If you drive your own car for work, your employer can repay you tax-free up to HMRC's approved mileage allowance payments – check the current rates on GOV.UK. For company cars, HMRC's advisory fuel rates above are updated quarterly (1 March, 1 June, 1 September and 1 December). Your real cost per mile from this calculator may be higher or lower than the advisory rate.

FAQ

How do I work out the fuel cost of a trip in the UK?

Divide the miles by your mpg to get UK gallons, multiply by 4.546 to get litres, then multiply by the price per litre. 300 miles at 45 mpg uses 6.67 gallons = 30.3 litres; at £1.74 a litre that costs £52.73.

How much does it cost to drive 100 miles?

At 45 mpg and £1.74 a litre, 100 miles uses 10.1 litres and costs about £17.58 (17.6p a mile). At 30 mpg it is £26.37, at 60 mpg £13.18. Change the price box to today's pump price for your own figure.

How do I convert mpg to litres per 100 km?

Divide 282.48 by the mpg figure (UK gallons). 45 mpg = 6.28 L/100 km, 50 mpg = 5.65 L/100 km. The same formula works the other way: 282.48 ÷ 7 L/100 km = 40.4 mpg.

Why is my mpg different from the figure on the car's brochure?

Official figures come from the WLTP laboratory test. Real driving with motorway speeds above 70 mph, cold starts, short trips, roof boxes, under-inflated tyres and air-con usually gives a lower mpg, so use your trip computer or a brim-to-brim measurement.

Is a US mpg the same as a UK mpg?

No. A UK (imperial) gallon is 4.54609 litres and a US gallon is 3.785 litres, so a car doing 40 US mpg does about 48 UK mpg. Check which one a review or website quotes before using it here.

How do I split fuel costs fairly for a lift share?

Work out the fuel cost of the whole journey and divide by the number of people in the car, including the driver. A 300-mile trip costing £52.73 split four ways is £13.18 each. Set the 'People sharing the cost' box to do this automatically.
