Your numbers

Quick answer (2026/27): Pension contributions get relief at your top income tax rate: 20% basic (added automatically), 40% higher and 45% additional (extra claimed via Self Assessment or HMRC). Between £100,000 and £125,140 the effective relief is 60% because you win back your personal allowance.

How pension tax relief works (2026/27)

The government tops up pension contributions with tax relief at your highest rate of income tax. How you get it depends on the type of scheme:

MethodHow relief is givenTypical schemes
Relief at sourceYou pay from taxed pay; the provider claims 20% from HMRC (£80 becomes £100). Higher and additional-rate relief must be claimed.Personal pensions, SIPPs, many workplace schemes
Net pay arrangementContribution taken before income tax, so full relief is automatic. Non-taxpayers get no relief.Many occupational schemes
Salary sacrificeYou give up salary; employer pays it in. Saves income tax and National Insurance.Workplace schemes that offer it

Limits to know

  • Annual allowance: £60,000 gross a year across all your pensions, including employer contributions. Unused allowance from the previous three tax years can usually be carried forward.
  • Earnings cap on relief: relief only on personal contributions up to 100% of your UK relevant earnings, or £3,600 gross if higher.
  • Tapered allowance: reduces by £1 for every £2 of adjusted income above £260,000, to a minimum of £10,000 (applies only if threshold income is also over £200,000).
  • Money purchase annual allowance: £10,000 once you have flexibly taken taxable income from a defined contribution pension.

Salary sacrifice change from April 2029

The Autumn Budget 2025 announced that from 6 April 2029 National Insurance relief on salary-sacrificed pension contributions will be capped at £2,000 a year per employee; amounts above that will pay employee and employer NI like normal salary. Income tax relief is not affected.

How to claim the extra relief

  • If you file Self Assessment, enter your gross personal pension contributions (net payment × 1.25) in the pensions section.
  • If you don't, contact HMRC with your contribution details; they will usually change your tax code or send a repayment.
  • You can backdate claims for up to four previous tax years.

FAQ

How much does a £4,000 pension contribution cost a higher-rate taxpayer?

£4,000 paid in becomes £5,000 in the pension once the provider adds £1,000 of basic-rate relief. A higher-rate taxpayer with at least £5,000 of income taxed at 40% claims another £1,000 through Self Assessment, so the real cost is £3,000.

How do I claim higher-rate pension tax relief?

Through your Self Assessment tax return, or by writing to or calling HMRC if you don't complete a return — HMRC usually adjusts your tax code. Relief at source only adds the basic 20% automatically. You can claim for the current year and the previous four tax years.

What is the 60% tax trap?

Between £100,000 and £125,140 of adjusted net income you lose £1 of personal allowance for every £2 of income, so the effective tax rate is 60%. Pension contributions reduce adjusted net income and win the allowance back. In the calculator, paying in £8,000 net on £110,000 of income puts £10,000 in the pension for a real cost of £4,000 — 60% relief.

Is there a limit on pension tax relief?

Yes. You get relief on contributions up to 100% of your UK relevant earnings (or £3,600 gross if you earn less than that), within the £60,000 annual allowance. The allowance tapers down to £10,000 for adjusted income above £260,000, and is £10,000 (the money purchase annual allowance) if you have flexibly accessed a pension.

Is salary sacrifice better than relief at source?

Usually, because salary sacrifice also saves employee National Insurance (8% or 2%) and often employer NI. From April 2029 NI savings will only apply to the first £2,000 a year of sacrificed salary; income tax relief is unchanged.
