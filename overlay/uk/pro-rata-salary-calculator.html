Your numbers

Quick answer: Pro rata salary = full-time salary × your hours ÷ full-time hours. £36,000 pro rata for 22.5 of 37.5 hours is £21,600 a year (£1,800 a month), at the same hourly rate. Holiday is 5.6 weeks pro rata — 16.8 days for 3 days a week.

How pro rata salary works

"Pro rata" means in proportion. A job advertised at "£36,000 pro rata" pays £36,000 for full-time hours; you receive the share that matches your hours.

Pro rata salary = full-time salary × your weekly hours ÷ full-time weekly hours × weeks employed ÷ 52. The hourly rate stays the same as the full-time job: full-time salary ÷ (full-time hours × 52).

Worked examples

  • Part-time hours: £36,000 full time, 37.5-hour week, you work 22.5 hours → £36,000 × 22.5 ÷ 37.5 = £21,600 a year (£1,800 a month).
  • Days instead of hours: 3 days of a 5-day week → £36,000 × 3 ÷ 5 = £21,600.
  • Part of the year: a 37.5-hour fixed-term contract for 26 weeks at £36,000 pro rata → £36,000 × 26 ÷ 52 = £18,000 for the contract.
  • Term-time only: often paid as weeks worked plus holiday entitlement, spread over 12 months — check how your employer calculates it.

Things to check in a part-time offer

  • What full-time hours the employer uses (35, 37.5 or 40) — it changes your pay.
  • Whether bank holidays are pro-rated or given on the days they fall.
  • Pension: auto-enrolment applies once you earn over £10,000 a year with one employer.
  • Tax: your personal allowance (£12,570) isn't pro-rated, so a lower salary means a smaller share goes in tax.

FAQ

What is £36,000 pro rata for 22.5 hours?

£21,600 a year if full time is 37.5 hours: £36,000 × 22.5 ÷ 37.5. That is £1,800 a month before tax and the same £18.46 hourly rate as the full-time job.

How do I calculate pro rata salary?

Divide your contracted hours by the full-time hours for the job and multiply by the full-time salary. For a role that only runs part of the year, also multiply by the weeks you are employed ÷ 52.

What does 0.6 FTE mean?

FTE (full-time equivalent) is your hours as a share of full time. 0.6 FTE on a 37.5-hour week is 22.5 hours, and you are paid 60% of the full-time salary.

How is holiday worked out for part-time workers?

Everyone is entitled to 5.6 weeks of paid holiday. For a regular pattern, multiply 5.6 by the days you work a week: 3 days a week gives 16.8 days (including bank holidays, pro rata).

Do part-time workers have to be paid the same rate?

Yes. Under the Part-time Workers Regulations 2000 part-timers must not be treated less favourably than comparable full-timers on pay rates, holiday, pension and training, unless the employer can objectively justify it.
