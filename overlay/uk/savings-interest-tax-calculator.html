Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): In 2026/27 basic-rate taxpayers can earn £1,000 of savings interest tax-free, higher-rate taxpayers £500 and additional-rate taxpayers £0, plus up to £5,000 at 0% if other taxable income is under £17,570. Interest above that is taxed at 20%, 40% or 45% – rising to 24%, 44% and 49% from 6 April 2027. Example: £2,400 of interest on a £35,000 salary costs £280 now and £336 from April 2027.

How tax on savings works

Think of it as three free layers, used in this order. Whatever is left is taxed.

  • 1. Unused Personal Allowance. If your wages or pension are under £12,570, interest first fills the gap at 0%.
  • 2. Starting rate for savings. Up to £5,000 of interest at 0%. Every £1 of other income over £12,570 cuts it by £1. A £15,000 pension leaves £2,570.
  • 3. Personal Savings Allowance (PSA). £1,000 if all your taxable income stays in the basic rate band, £500 in the higher rate band, £0 in the additional rate band.
  • The rest is taxed at your rate: 20%, 40% or 45% in 2026/27. Interest sits on top of your other income, so part of it can fall into a higher band.

Interest that is tax-free under the PSA still counts as income. It can still move you into the higher rate band.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Count interest when it is paid. For a fixed-rate bond that pays all its interest at the end, the whole amount counts in the tax year it is paid.
  • Joint accounts: each of you usually counts half the interest.
  • Fill your ISA first if your interest is near your allowance. The rate rise in April 2027 makes this more valuable.
  • Check your tax code. HMRC estimates interest from last year. If your interest has gone down, ask HMRC to update it.
  • Near £100,000? Interest counts towards it too. Above £100,000 you start to lose your Personal Allowance.

Savings tax FAQ

How much interest can I earn before paying tax?

It depends on your other income. A basic-rate taxpayer can earn £1,000 of interest tax-free, a higher-rate taxpayer £500 and an additional-rate taxpayer £0. If your other income is under £17,570 you can also get up to £5,000 extra at 0% (the starting rate for savings). So someone on £15,000 can earn up to £3,570 of interest tax-free.

Do I pay tax on £2,400 of interest on a £35,000 salary?

Yes, £280 in 2026/27. The first £1,000 is covered by the Personal Savings Allowance. The other £1,400 is taxed at 20%. With the same interest from April 2027 it would be £336 (24%).

How much tax will I pay on savings from April 2027?

Interest above your tax-free amounts will be taxed at 24% (basic), 44% (higher) or 49% (additional), up from 20%, 40% and 45%. The £1,000 and £500 allowances and the £5,000 starting rate stay. A higher-rate taxpayer with £1,500 of interest pays £400 now and £440 from April 2027.

Does savings interest push me into the higher rate?

It can. Interest counts as income. If your salary plus interest takes your taxable income over £37,700 (about £50,270 in total), you become a higher-rate taxpayer and your Personal Savings Allowance drops from £1,000 to £500.

How does HMRC collect tax on savings interest?

Banks tell HMRC how much interest you earned. If you are employed or get a pension, HMRC usually changes your tax code to collect it. If you already fill in a Self Assessment return, you add it there.

Is ISA interest counted towards the Personal Savings Allowance?

No. Interest in a cash ISA is tax-free and is ignored. You can put up to £20,000 a year into ISAs in 2026/27. Moving savings into an ISA is the simplest way to stop paying tax on interest.
