Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): monthly saving = what you still need ÷ months, a little less with interest. £10,000 in 2 years takes about £417 a month (£401 at 4% interest); a £30,000 house deposit in 5 years about £500 a month (£452 at 4%). Most savers pay no tax on interest, and savings are FSCS-protected up to £120,000.

How the calculator works

Example first. You want £16,000 in 3 years. You already have £1,600 and your account pays 4% a year. The £1,600 grows on its own to about £1,804. You still need about £14,196. Saving £372 a month gets you there, because interest on your deposits adds about £1,015 along the way.

  • Monthly rate = yearly rate ÷ 12. So 4% a year is about 0.33% a month.
  • Compound interest means you also earn interest on your interest. The calculator adds interest every month.
  • Gap = goal − what your current savings will grow to.
  • Monthly saving = gap × i ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1). Here i is the monthly rate and n is the number of months.

With no interest it is simply (goal − already saved) ÷ months. If your savings already grow past the goal, you need £0 a month.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Pay yourself first. Set up a standing order on payday so the money moves before you can spend it.
  • Check the real rate. Some accounts pay a bonus rate only for 12 months, or only if you make no withdrawals. Enter the rate you will actually get.
  • Use your ISA allowance. If your interest could go over £1,000 (or £500 as a higher-rate taxpayer), a cash ISA keeps it tax-free.
  • Saving for a first home? Look at a Lifetime ISA. The 25% bonus beats most interest rates, but other withdrawals lose 25%.
  • Keep an emergency buffer too. Then a surprise bill doesn't empty your goal pot.

Savings goal FAQ

How much do I need to save each month to reach my goal?

Take what you still need and divide it by the number of months. Interest then lowers the amount a little. For £10,000 in 2 years from zero: £10,000 ÷ 24 = £417 a month with no interest, or £401 a month at 4% a year.

How long does it take to save £10,000?

At £500 a month it takes 20 months (with no interest you reach exactly £10,000). At £250 a month it takes 40 months, or about 3 years and 4 months. Interest makes it a little quicker.

How much should I save a month for a house deposit?

For a £30,000 deposit in 5 years from zero you need £500 a month with no interest, or £452 a month at 4% a year. A Lifetime ISA can help: the government adds 25% on up to £4,000 a year, so £4,000 becomes £5,000.

Do I pay tax on the interest from my savings?

Most people don't. Basic-rate taxpayers can earn £1,000 of interest a year tax-free and higher-rate taxpayers £500. Interest in a cash ISA is always tax-free. From April 2027 interest above the allowances is taxed at 24%, 44% or 49%.

Is my money safe in a UK savings account?

Yes, up to £120,000 per person at each authorised bank or building society, through the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS). Some banks share one licence, so check before you split money between brands.

Why does the Bank of England rate matter for my savings?

Banks often change their savings rates after Bank Rate moves. Bank Rate went up to 4% on 5 November 2026. If it changes again, re-run the calculator with your new rate.
