How PCP finance works

Personal Contract Purchase (PCP) splits the car's cost into a deposit, monthly payments and an optional final payment — the Guaranteed Minimum Future Value (GMFV), often called the balloon. Your monthly payments cover the car's expected loss in value plus interest on the whole amount borrowed. At the end you choose to pay the balloon and keep the car, hand it back, or use any equity (car value above the balloon) as a deposit on the next one.

This calculator converts the APR to a monthly rate, discounts the final payment back to today, and spreads the rest over the term. It also shows the hire purchase (HP) payment for the same car, where the whole amount is repaid monthly.

Voluntary termination (the "half-way" rule)

The Consumer Credit Act 1974, section 99, lets you end a hire-purchase or PCP agreement early once you have paid half of the total amount payable — deposit, monthly payments, final payment and fees. In the first example the total is about £34,346, so you need to have paid about £17,173: the £3,000 deposit plus about 34 monthly payments. You return the car and pay nothing more unless it has excess damage.

Mileage and the balloon

The GMFV is set from your agreed annual mileage. A higher mileage allowance means a lower GMFV and so higher monthly payments; going over the allowance leads to a per-mile charge if you hand the car back. Set the allowance honestly — it is usually cheaper to pay for miles up front than in excess charges.

Car finance commission redress

If you had car finance between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024 where the broker was paid commission you weren't told about (discretionary commission, very high commission or tied lender arrangements), you may be owed compensation under the FCA's motor finance redress scheme. The FCA estimates an average of about £829 per agreement. Some parts of the scheme were suspended by the Upper Tribunal on 2 July 2026, so check the FCA's latest update. You can complain to your lender directly, free of charge.

Before you sign

  • Compare the APR and total amount payable, not just the monthly figure.
  • Ask for the HP price for the same car to see the true cost of the lower PCP payment.
  • Check the mileage limit, excess-mileage rate and the end-of-term condition guide.
  • Gap insurance and service plans are optional — don't add them to the finance without comparing.

FAQ

How is a PCP monthly payment calculated?

The lender finances the car price minus your deposit, but you only repay the part that is not deferred to the end: monthly payment = (amount financed − present value of the final payment) × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)−n), where i is the monthly rate from the APR and n the number of months. Interest is still charged on the whole balance, including the deferred final payment.

PCP or hire purchase — which is cheaper?

Hire purchase is usually cheaper if you want to keep the car, because you pay interest on a balance that falls faster. In our £28,000 example over 4 years, buying the car through PCP costs about £34,346 in total against £32,610 on HP. PCP is cheaper month to month and suits drivers who will hand the car back or change it every few years.

What is the GMFV or balloon payment?

The guaranteed minimum future value is the lender's estimate of what the car will be worth at the end, based on age and your agreed mileage. You can pay it and keep the car, hand the car back and owe nothing more (if within mileage and in fair condition), or part-exchange it.

Can I hand a PCP car back early?

Yes. Under section 99 of the Consumer Credit Act you can end the agreement once you have paid half of the total amount payable (including the final payment and fees). With a large final payment that point comes late — about month 34 of 48 in our example. Excess wear and mileage charges can still apply.

Am I owed money for car finance commission?

Possibly, if you took motor finance between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024 and weren't told about certain commission arrangements. The FCA has set up a redress scheme (average payout estimated at £829 per agreement). Parts of it were suspended by the Upper Tribunal in July 2026, so check the FCA's motor finance page for the current position — you don't need a claims company to complain.
