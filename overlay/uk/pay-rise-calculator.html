Your numbers

Short answer: In 2026/27 a basic-rate taxpayer in England, Wales or Northern Ireland keeps 72% of a pay rise (63% with a Plan 2 student loan), so a 5% rise on £45,000 (£2,250) adds about £1,620 a year, or £1,418 with Plan 2. Higher-rate earners keep 58%, and between £100,000 and £125,140 only 38%. CPI inflation was 3.6% in the 12 months to October 2026, so a 3% rise is a real-terms cut.

Pay rise vs inflation

“Real terms” means after taking rising prices into account. If your take-home pay rises by less than prices, you can buy less than before. Examples for England with no student loan, using CPI of 3.6%:

How the calculator works

It works out your take-home pay twice – on your old salary and on your new salary – and shows the difference.

  • Take-home pay = salary − income tax − National Insurance − student loan.
  • Income tax: tax on salary above your personal allowance, band by band.
  • National Insurance (NI): a separate deduction that pays for the State Pension and some benefits.
  • Student loan: 9% of everything above your plan's threshold.
  • Real change = (1 + take-home rise) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.

The share of each extra £1 you lose is called your marginal rate. This decides how much of a rise you keep:

Tips and common mistakes

  • Ask for the rise as a percentage and a figure. 4% of £35,000 is £1,400 before tax, about £1,008 after tax and NI.
  • Check your tax code on your next payslip. 1257L is the usual code. A wrong code can make a rise look smaller or bigger for a few months.
  • Near £50,270 or £100,000? Paying some of the rise into your pension can save 40% or 60% tax.
  • A one-off bonus is taxed the same way. It goes through PAYE like normal pay. See the bonus tax calculator.
  • Scotland is different. If your main home is in Scotland, choose Scotland in the calculator. The 42% band starts at £43,663, so mid-salary rises keep less.

Pay rise FAQ

How much is a 5% pay rise after tax on £45,000?

A 5% rise is £2,250 a year before tax. In England with no student loan you keep £1,620 a year (£135 a month), which is 72%. With a Plan 2 student loan you keep £1,418 (63%). In Scotland with no loan you keep £1,125 (50%), because the 42% rate starts at £43,663.

How much is a 3% pay rise after tax?

On £30,000 a 3% rise is £900 a year. You keep £648 (£54 a month) in England, Wales or Northern Ireland with no student loan. On £60,000 a 3% rise is £1,800 and you keep £1,044, because higher-rate tax is 40%.

Is my pay rise above inflation?

Compare the rise in your take-home pay with CPI inflation, which was 3.6% for the 12 months to October 2026. Example: £30,000 with a 3% rise gives take-home pay about 2.6% higher, so in real terms you are about 1.0% worse off. A 5% rise gives about 4.3% more take-home pay, about 0.7% ahead of prices.

Why do I keep less of a rise than my tax band suggests?

Because NI and student loan come out too. On £30,000 with a Plan 2 loan, each extra £100 loses £20 tax, £8 NI and £9 loan, so you keep £63. Your tax band only tells you the income tax part.

What is the 60% tax trap?

Between £100,000 and £125,140 you lose £1 of personal allowance for every £2 you earn. So each extra £100 costs £40 tax plus £20 tax on the lost allowance, plus £2 NI. You keep about £38. Paying more into a pension can bring your income back under £100,000.

Will a pay rise move me into a higher tax band?

Only the part of your salary above the line is taxed at the higher rate. If a rise takes you from £49,000 to £52,000, only the £1,730 above £50,270 is taxed at 40%. You never take home less in total because of a rise, except when you lose a benefit such as Child Benefit or free childcare.
