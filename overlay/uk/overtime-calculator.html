Quick answer (2026/27): UK law sets no overtime rate — time and a half or double time applies only if your contract says so, but your average pay for all hours worked must stay at or above the minimum wage (£12.71 for 21+ from 1 April 2026, £13.21 from April 2027). Example: £14/hour × 37.5 hours + 6 hours at time and a half = £651 a week before tax.

How overtime pay works in the UK

Overtime is any time you work beyond your normal contracted hours. There is no statutory overtime rate: your contract (or a collective agreement) decides whether overtime is paid at your normal rate, time and a half, double time, or given back as time off in lieu (TOIL). The calculator works it out as:

Weekly gross = rate × basic hours + rate × 1.5 × time-and-a-half hours + rate × 2 × double-time hours

Your rights on overtime

  • Pay: only what your contract says, but average pay for all hours worked must not fall below the minimum wage.
  • Compulsory overtime: allowed if your contract says so; refusing it may be a disciplinary matter.
  • Guaranteed vs non-guaranteed: if overtime is guaranteed, your employer must offer it; non-guaranteed overtime is offered as needed.
  • 48-hour limit: you can't be made to work more than 48 hours a week on average unless you opt out in writing; under-18s are limited to 8 hours a day and 40 a week.
  • Part-time workers: are usually only paid an overtime rate once they work beyond the full-time hours, if full-timers would be.
  • Holiday pay: regular overtime counts towards holiday pay for the first 4 weeks of statutory leave.

Tips

  • Record overtime hours yourself and compare them with your payslip — overtime is often paid a month in arrears.
  • If you're offered TOIL instead of pay, check how long you have to take it.
  • Overtime counts as income for student loan, pension contributions (if your scheme uses qualifying earnings) and Universal Credit.

FAQ

Is overtime paid at time and a half by law in the UK?

No. UK law does not set an overtime rate, and employers don't have to pay extra for overtime at all. Time and a half (1.5×) or double time (2×) only applies if your contract, a collective agreement or a workplace policy says so. The legal floor is that your average pay for all the hours you work must not fall below the National Minimum Wage.

How do I calculate time and a half?

Multiply your hourly rate by 1.5, then by the overtime hours. At £14 an hour, time and a half is £21 an hour, so 6 hours of overtime pays £126. Added to 37.5 basic hours (£525) that is £651 for the week before tax.

How much is double time on £12.71 an hour?

£25.42 an hour. Time and a half on the April 2026 National Living Wage of £12.71 is £19.07 an hour (£19.065 rounded).

Does overtime count towards holiday pay?

Regular overtime must be included in holiday pay for the first 4 weeks of statutory leave, along with other pay you normally receive. Genuinely occasional overtime does not have to be included. Your contract may give more.

Can my employer make me work overtime?

Only if your contract says overtime is compulsory. Even then, you cannot be made to work more than an average of 48 hours a week (usually averaged over 17 weeks) unless you have signed an opt-out, which you can cancel. Workers under 18 cannot work more than 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week.

Is overtime taxed more?

No. Overtime is added to your pay for the pay period and taxed through PAYE at your normal rates. A large overtime month can push some pay into a higher band for that month, but with a cumulative tax code it evens out over the tax year.
