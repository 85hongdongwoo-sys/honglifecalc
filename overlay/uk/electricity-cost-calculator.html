How to work out the running cost

Your bill charges for kilowatt-hours (kWh) – one kWh is 1,000 watts used for one hour.

  • kWh = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours of use
  • Cost = kWh × unit rate (£ per kWh)
  • Cost per hour = kW × unit rate – a 2 kW heater at 26.32p/kWh costs 52.6p an hour

Enter your own unit rate from your bill: the price varies by supplier, tariff, region and payment method, and on standard variable tariffs it changes every quarter with Ofgem's energy price cap (which rose by 4% from 1 October 2026).

Tips to cut appliance running costs

  • Target the high-wattage, long-use items first: electric heaters, tumble dryers and electric showers dominate most bills; LED bulbs and phone chargers barely register.
  • Shorten heating times rather than lowering power settings – a 2 kW heater for one hour less a day saves 2 kWh a day.
  • Use the energy label (annual kWh) for fridges, freezers and washing machines instead of the wattage.
  • Time-of-use tariffs such as Economy 7 make night-time use cheaper – enter the night rate to see the saving.
  • Measure it with a plug-in energy monitor if you are unsure how much an appliance really uses.

Common mistakes

  • Typing the unit rate in pence (26.32) instead of pounds (0.2632).
  • Using watts as kWh – a 1,000 W appliance only uses 1 kWh if it runs for a full hour.
  • Assuming a thermostat-controlled appliance runs at full power the whole time.
  • Adding the standing charge to each appliance – it is a fixed daily charge for the whole home.

FAQ

How do I calculate the cost of running an appliance?

Multiply the power in kilowatts by the hours used to get kWh, then multiply by your unit rate. A 2,000 W heater for 3 hours uses 2 × 3 = 6 kWh; at the October–December 2026 price cap rate of 26.32p per kWh that is £1.58.

How much does it cost to run a 1,000 W appliance for an hour?

1,000 W for one hour uses exactly 1 kWh, so it costs your unit rate – 26.32p at the Oct–Dec 2026 price cap average. Three hours a day for 30 days is 90 kWh, or £23.69.

How much does it cost to boil a kettle?

A 3,000 W kettle that takes about 2 minutes to boil uses 0.1 kWh, so about 2.6p at 26.32p per kWh. Boiling only the water you need cuts the time and the cost.

Does the calculator include the standing charge?

No. The standing charge is a fixed amount per day on your bill that you pay regardless of use, so it does not change when you switch an appliance on or off. Under the October–December 2026 price cap the average electricity standing charge is 54.83p a day – about £200 a year. This calculator shows the extra cost of using the appliance.

How do I find my electricity unit rate?

It is printed on your bill or in your supplier's app as pence per kWh, often with a separate day and night rate on Economy 7. If you are on a standard variable tariff, Ofgem's price cap sets the maximum average unit rate each quarter: 26.32p per kWh (GB average, Direct Debit) from 1 October to 31 December 2026.

Why does my fridge cost less than its wattage suggests?

Fridges and freezers cycle on and off, so they rarely draw full power for 24 hours. Use the annual kWh figure on the energy label instead: 250 kWh a year at 26.32p per kWh is £65.80 a year.
