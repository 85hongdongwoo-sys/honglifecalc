Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): You buy a share of the home (usually 25%–75%, sometimes 10%) and pay rent on the rest – no more than 3% a year on new homes, typically 2.75%. On a £300,000 home with a 40% share, rent is £412.50 a month; with a 5% example mortgage and a £120 service charge the total is about £1,164 a month. Household income must be £80,000 or less (£90,000 in London).

How shared ownership works

You buy part of a home – your share – usually with a mortgage. A housing association or other landlord owns the rest. You pay rent on the part they own.

Take a £300,000 home. You buy 40%, which is £120,000. The landlord owns 60%, worth £180,000. At 2.75% a year, the rent is £4,950 a year, or £412.50 a month. Then add your mortgage and the service charge.

The calculator does this for you:

  • Your share = full value × share %
  • Mortgage = share − deposit, paid back monthly over the term you choose
  • Rent a month = (full value − your share) × rent % ÷ 12
  • Total = mortgage + rent + service charge

Staircasing: buying more of your home

Staircasing means buying extra shares. Each time, you own more and pay less rent. The price depends on what your home is worth when you buy, not what you first paid. If a £300,000 home is now worth £330,000, another 10% costs £33,000, not £30,000.

For shares of 5% or more, a surveyor registered with RICS (the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors) values the home. You must buy within 3 months of that valuation. For 1% shares, the landlord uses the House Price Index (an official measure of how house prices change) instead.

Stamp duty on shared ownership

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is a tax on buying property in England and Northern Ireland. With shared ownership you choose one of two ways:

  • Market value election: pay once on the full value of the home. GOV.UK’s example: a 50% share of a £280,000 home costs £4,000 in SDLT (0% on £125,000, 2% on the next £125,000, 5% on £30,000). You pay nothing more when you staircase. First-time buyers can use first-time buyer rates: 0% on a home worth up to £300,000.
  • Paying in stages: pay only if your share price is above the threshold. You may also owe 1% on the rent if its value over the lease is above the threshold. You then pay nothing more until you own more than 80%.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Rent goes up every year. It is usually reviewed once a year and can rise but not fall. On a newer lease with CPI + 1%, CPI of 2.1% means a 3.1% rise.
  • Don’t forget repairs. After any initial repair period ends, all repairs are your job, even if you own only 25%.
  • Check the service charge. Flats often have high and rising service charges. Ask for a breakdown.
  • Staircasing gets dearer as prices rise. Use the calculator’s “buy another 10%” line as a starting point only.
  • 1% shares can’t be saved up. If you skip a year, you can’t buy 2% the next year.
  • A smaller share isn’t always cheaper each month. Less mortgage means more rent. Try 25%, 40% and 50% in the calculator.

Shared ownership questions

How much is rent on shared ownership?

Rent is a percentage of the value of the share you don’t own. On new homes it can be no more than 3% a year, and 2.75% is typical. On a £300,000 home where you own 40%, you rent the other £180,000: 2.75% of that is £4,950 a year, or £412.50 a month.

What is the monthly cost of shared ownership on a £300,000 home?

With a 40% share, a 10% deposit, a 5% mortgage over 25 years, 2.75% rent and a £120 service charge, it comes to about £1,164 a month: £631 mortgage, £413 rent and £120 service charge. With a 35-year mortgage the total drops to about £1,078, but you pay more interest overall.

How does staircasing work?

Staircasing means buying more of your home. You can usually buy 10% or more at any time, at the home’s value on the day you buy. On a home now worth £300,000, another 10% costs £30,000 and cuts rent at 2.75% by £68.75 a month. Homes bought from April 2021 may also let you buy 1% a year for the first 15 years, priced using the House Price Index, with no admin fee.

Who can buy a shared ownership home?

Your household income must be £80,000 a year or less (£90,000 in London), and you must be unable to afford a suitable home on the open market. You also need to be a first-time buyer, a former owner who can’t afford to buy now, a new household, or an existing shared owner moving home.

Do I pay stamp duty on shared ownership?

You can choose. With a market value election you pay once, on the full market value, and never again when you staircase. If you don’t elect, you pay only if your share price is above the threshold, and nothing more until you own over 80%. First-time buyers who elect pay 0% on a home worth up to £300,000.

Is shared ownership worth it?

It lowers the deposit: 10% of a 40% share of £300,000 is £12,000, against £30,000 for 10% of the whole home. But you pay rent that usually rises every year, a service charge, and all repairs after any initial repair period. Buying more shares later costs more if prices rise. Compare the total monthly cost with renting and with buying outright before you decide.
