How this calculator works

Britain’s electricity comes from a changing mix of wind, nuclear, gas, solar, biomass, hydro and imports, so the CO₂ released for each unit you use – the carbon intensity – rises and falls through the day. This page reads the live half-hourly forecast for your region and finds the start time that gives your charge the lowest average carbon intensity before you need the car.

  • Electricity drawn = battery size × (charge to − charge from) ÷ (1 − charging losses)
  • Charging time = electricity drawn ÷ charger power
  • CO₂ of the charge = electricity drawn × average gCO₂/kWh over the charging time ÷ 1,000 (kg)

Every possible half-hour start in your window is tested and the lowest-average block is shown in blue on the chart. The orange bar is the current half-hour, so you can compare “plug in now” with “wait”.

Worked example

A 60 kWh battery charged from 20% to 80% takes 36 kWh. With 10% charging losses the car draws 40 kWh from the grid – about 5.7 hours on a 7 kW wallbox.

Regions covered

The forecast is available for Great Britain as a whole, for England, Scotland and Wales, and for 14 regional distribution areas: North Scotland, South Scotland, North West England, North East England, Yorkshire, North Wales & Merseyside, South Wales, West Midlands, East Midlands, East England, South West England, South England, London and South East England. Northern Ireland is a separate grid and is not covered.

Tips for cleaner charging

  • Set a schedule, not a reminder. Enter the start time in your charger or car app so it starts even if you are asleep.
  • Only charge what you need. Stopping at 80% for daily driving shortens the charge and lets it fit into the cleanest hours.
  • Check again before you plug in. The forecast is refreshed every half hour, and windy spells can arrive earlier or later than expected.
  • Lower power can be fine. A slower charge spread over a long clean night can beat a fast charge at a dirtier time.

Common mistakes

  • Using the national figure when your own region is very different.
  • Forgetting charging losses – the grid supplies more than the battery stores.
  • Treating the forecast as exact. It is a forecast and can change.

FAQ

When is the best time to charge an electric car in the UK?

The cleanest time is whenever the grid’s carbon intensity is lowest, which changes every day with the wind, sunshine and demand. Overnight and windy periods are often low, and weekday early evenings (around 4pm–8pm) are often high, but not always. The calculator reads the live 48-hour forecast for your region and picks the start time with the lowest average for your charge.

What does gCO₂/kWh mean?

Grams of carbon dioxide emitted for each kilowatt-hour of electricity used. Multiply it by the kWh your car draws to get the CO₂ of a charge: 40 kWh at 200 gCO₂/kWh is 8 kg of CO₂; the same charge at 50 gCO₂/kWh is 2 kg.

Where does the forecast come from?

From the Carbon Intensity API run by the National Energy System Operator (NESO). It gives a half-hourly forecast up to 48 hours ahead for Great Britain and for 14 regional distribution areas, together with the expected fuel mix. The data is published under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence.

Why is my region different from the national figure?

Each region’s figure reflects the generation feeding its local network. Scotland often has much lower intensity because of wind and hydro, while regions that rely more on gas power stations and imports are usually higher. Choose your own region for the most relevant answer.

Does charging at the cleanest time also save money?

Often, but not always. Clean periods tend to be times of low demand or high wind, which are also when time-of-use tariffs are cheapest. Your bill depends on your own tariff, though, so check its off-peak hours as well. Use our electricity cost calculator to work out the price of a charge.

Will my home charger let me choose the time?

Smart charge points sold in Great Britain since 30 June 2022 must have smart functionality and come with default charging hours outside peak times (8am–11am and 4pm–10pm on weekdays). You can change or remove these hours and set a schedule in the charger’s app – set it to the start time shown here.
