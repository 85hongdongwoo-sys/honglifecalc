Your numbers

Quick answer: MoneyHelper's guide is to spend up to 30% of your income before tax on rent: on £40,000 that is £1,000 a month. Many agents also check that income is at least 30× the monthly rent. In England deposits are capped at 5 weeks' rent and, since 1 May 2026, rent in advance at 1 month.

How the calculator works

Example first. You earn £40,000 a year.

  • 30% guide: £40,000 × 30% ÷ 12 = £1,000 a month.
  • Agent 30× check: £40,000 ÷ 30 = £1,333 a month at most.
  • 35% of take-home: you take home about £2,693 a month, and 35% of that is £943.

Gross income means pay before tax. Take-home pay is what reaches your bank after income tax and National Insurance. The 30× check is what many agents use to pass you; the 30% guide and the take-home figure are better for checking you can really live on what's left.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Count bills too. Council tax, energy, water and broadband can add hundreds a month on top of rent.
  • Use everyone's income. On a joint tenancy, add up all tenants' incomes in the calculator.
  • Don't pay before you sign. In England a landlord can't take rent before the tenancy agreement is signed.
  • In Scotland or Wales the renting rules and tax bands are different.

Rent affordability FAQ

How much rent can I afford on £30,000?

MoneyHelper's 30% guide gives £750 a month (30% of £30,000 ÷ 12). A letting agent's 30× check allows up to £1,000. After 2026/27 tax and National Insurance you take home about £2,093 a month, and 35% of that is £733.

What is the 30% rule for rent?

MoneyHelper, the government-backed money guidance service, describes the 30% rule: spend up to 30% of your income before tax on rent. It says the rule is just a guide and keeping to 30% isn't always possible.

What does '30 times the rent' mean?

Many letting agents check that your yearly income is at least 30 times the monthly rent. For £1,000 a month you would need £30,000 a year. This is an agent practice, not a law, and agents can use different multiples or ask for a guarantor.

How much deposit can a landlord ask for in England?

Up to 5 weeks' rent if the yearly rent is under £50,000, or 6 weeks' rent if it is between £50,000 and £100,000. For £1,000 a month that is £1,153.85. A holding deposit can be up to 1 week's rent (£230.77).

Can a landlord ask for 6 months' rent in advance?

Not in England since 1 May 2026. Under the Renters' Rights Act, a landlord can't ask for or accept rent before you sign the tenancy agreement, and after signing can ask for at most 1 month's rent in advance.

How often can my rent go up?

In England, from 1 May 2026, only once a year and not in the first 12 months of a new tenancy. The landlord must use Form 4A and give at least 2 months' notice. You can challenge an increase above the market rent.
