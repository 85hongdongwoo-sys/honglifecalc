401(k) Contribution Limit 2027: $25,500

How much you can put in for 2027 — and how close your paycheck percentage gets you.

For 2027 you can defer $25,500 into a 401(k), 403(b), most 457 plans or the federal TSP — $1,000 more than in 2026. Workers 50 and older can add a $8,500 catch-up (total $34,000), and those aged 60 to 63 can add $11,750 instead. The IRA limit stays $7,500 (+$1,100 catch-up) and the HSA limit for self-only coverage is $4,500.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 401(k) limit for 2027?

$25,500 for employee elective deferrals to 401(k), 403(b), most 457 plans and the Thrift Savings Plan (2026: $24,500).

What is the 401(k) catch-up for 2027?

$8,500 for ages 50 and over, or $11,750 for ages 60 to 63 under the SECURE 2.0 "super catch-up".

What is the IRA limit for 2027?

$7,500, unchanged from 2026, plus a $1,100 catch-up for ages 50 and over.

What are the Roth IRA income limits for 2027?

The phase-out range is $158,000–$173,000 for singles and heads of household and $250,000–$260,000 for married couples filing jointly.

What is the HSA limit for 2027?

$4,500 for self-only coverage (2026: $4,400).

Who is a highly compensated employee in 2027?

Someone who earned more than $170,000 (up from $160,000).
