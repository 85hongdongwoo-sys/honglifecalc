Check your numbers

Short answer: with a Section 8 voucher you usually pay about 30% of your adjusted monthly income for rent and utilities (or 10% of gross monthly income if that is higher), and the voucher pays the rest up to the local payment standard. In 2026 adjusted income takes off $500 per dependent and $550 for an elderly or disabled family ($525 and $575 from 1 January 2027).

How the voucher works (in plain words)

A Housing Choice Voucher (often called Section 8) helps you rent a home from a private landlord. You pay part of the rent. The local public housing agency (PHA) pays the rest straight to the landlord.

  • Step 1 – adjusted income. Start with yearly income before tax. Take off the deductions above. Divide by 12.
  • Step 2 – your share. Take 30% of adjusted monthly income. Compare it with 10% of gross monthly income. Your share is the higher one.
  • Step 3 – the voucher. The voucher pays the rent (or the payment standard, if that is lower) minus your share.
  • Step 4 – anything above the standard is yours to pay too.

The payment standard is the amount the agency uses for each unit size in your area. It is usually close to the local fair market rent.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Ask for the payment standard first. Then look for homes at or under it.
  • Count utilities. A cheap rent with high bills can push you over the standard.
  • Report income changes. Your share is recalculated when your income changes.
  • Get on several lists. You can apply to more than one agency.
  • Never pay to apply. Applying for a voucher is free.

Section 8 FAQ

How much rent do you pay with Section 8?

Usually about 30% of your adjusted monthly income. For example, a family of 4 earning $24,000 with 2 children has adjusted income of $23,000 in 2026 ($24,000 − 2 × $500), so their share is $575 a month. The voucher pays the rest, up to the local payment standard.

What counts as adjusted income for Section 8?

Your yearly income before tax, minus deductions. In 2026 these are $500 for each dependent and $550 if the head or spouse is 62 or older or disabled (from 1 January 2027: $525 and $575). Child care costs for children under 13 also come off, and medical or disability costs above 10% of your yearly income for elderly or disabled families. Agencies not yet on the new HUD rules still use $480 and $525.

What is the income limit for a Section 8 voucher?

Most families must be under 50% of the area median income (AMI) for their county and family size. Each housing agency must give at least 75% of new vouchers each year to families under 30% of AMI. The limits differ by area, so check with your local agency or HUD.

Can I rent a place that costs more than the payment standard?

Yes, but you pay the extra. When you first rent the unit, your share of rent and utilities cannot be more than 40% of your adjusted monthly income. This 40% limit is checked at move-in (initial occupancy).

Why do I pay more than 30%?

Because the rent is above the payment standard, or because 10% of your gross monthly income is higher than 30% of your adjusted income. The higher of the two is used. Your agency can also set a minimum rent of up to $50.

How long is the Section 8 waiting list?

It depends on the area. Many agencies have long waiting lists and open them only for a short time. Ask your local public housing agency (PHA) when its list opens.

Does the voucher pay utilities?

The voucher is based on rent plus utilities. If you pay utilities yourself, the agency gives a utility allowance, which lowers the rent you pay to the landlord.
