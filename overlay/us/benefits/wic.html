Check your numbers

Short answer: from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, WIC income limits are 185% of the poverty guidelines: $29,526 a year for 1 person, $50,542 for 3 and $61,050 for 4 (48 states and DC), plus $10,508 for each extra person. If you get SNAP, Medicaid or TANF, you automatically meet the income rule.

How WIC works (in plain words)

WIC stands for the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children. It is run by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) through state and local clinics. It is free.

  • You need three things: you are in a WIC group, your income is under the limit (or you get SNAP, Medicaid or TANF), and a clinic finds a nutrition need. A "nutrition need" can be things like low iron or poor weight gain. Many people meet it.
  • What you get: a monthly benefit card for healthy foods such as milk, eggs, fruit and vegetables, whole grains and infant formula, plus breastfeeding help and nutrition advice.
  • The limit is 185% of the poverty guideline. So for 4 people: $33,000 × 1.85 = $61,050.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Count the unborn baby. Pregnant women often forget, and use too small a household.
  • Apply even if you are close. Clinics check income carefully, and recent pay cuts may count.
  • Working families qualify too. Many families with jobs are under 185% of the poverty line.
  • Bring proof. ID, proof of address and proof of income or of SNAP, Medicaid or TANF.

WIC FAQ

What is the WIC income limit for a family of 4?

$61,050 a year from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027. That is $5,088 a month or $1,175 a week, before tax. Alaska and Hawaii have higher limits.

What is the WIC income limit for a family of 3?

$50,542 a year, $4,212 a month or $972 a week (48 states and DC). For 2 people it is $40,034 a year.

Does a pregnant woman count as two people for WIC?

Yes. A pregnant woman's household is counted with the unborn baby, so a pregnant woman living alone uses the limit for 2 people ($40,034). With twins, count 3.

If I get Medicaid or SNAP, do I qualify for WIC?

You meet the income test automatically if you get SNAP, Medicaid or TANF. You still need to be in a WIC group (pregnant, a new mother, a baby or a child under 5) and have a nutrition check at the clinic.

Who can get WIC?

Pregnant women, women up to 6 months after the baby is born, breastfeeding women up to the baby's first birthday, babies, and children up to their 5th birthday. Moms, dads, foster parents, grandparents and anyone else raising a child under 5 can apply for the child in their care. A foster child counts as a separate household of one.

Is WIC income before or after tax?

Before tax (gross income). Count income from everyone in the household.
