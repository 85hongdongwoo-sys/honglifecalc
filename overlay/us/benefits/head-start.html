Head Start eligibility

Head Start serves children from birth until they are old enough for school.

Check your numbers

Quick answer (2026): a child qualifies for free Head Start if family income is at or below the 2026 poverty line — $27,320 a year for a family of 3, $33,000 for 4 ($15,960 + $5,680 per extra person, 48 states and DC; Alaska and Hawaii are higher) — or if the family is on public assistance, is homeless, or the child is in foster care. Families up to 130% of the line ($35,516 for 3) may get a place if the program has room.

Hong Life is an independent guide, not a government website. Apply and confirm with your local Head Start program.

Who qualifies (in plain words)

A child can get a Head Start place in one of these ways:

  • Low income. Family income is at or below the poverty line (the "100% line" in the table).
  • Public assistance. The family gets it, or would be able to get it if it were not for child care. This includes TANF child-only payments. TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families) is cash help paid by your state.
  • Homeless – for example, staying with others because you lost your home, or living in a shelter, motel or car.
  • Foster care. Children in foster care are eligible.

Programs can also fill places in two extra ways. A child who meets none of the rules above can still be enrolled if they would benefit from the program, as long as such children stay within 10% of the program's enrollment. On top of that, a program can enroll an extra 35% of children from families under 130% of the poverty line, but only after it has set up its outreach rules and served the families who qualify first.

There is a separate rule about disability. At least 10% of the places a program actually fills must go to children who qualify for help under IDEA – the federal law that pays for special education and early intervention for children with disabilities.

How to apply

  • Find a program near you with the Head Start Center Locator, then apply directly to that program.
  • Bring proof of income (pay stubs or a tax return), proof of public assistance if you get it, and the child's birth certificate or other proof of age.
  • Places are limited. If the program is full, ask to be put on the waiting list. Programs pick children by need, not first come first served.
  • Once a child is taking part, they stay eligible through the end of the next program year, even if your income goes up.

Head Start FAQ

What is the Head Start income limit for a family of 3 in 2026?

$27,320 a year, which is 100% of the 2026 federal poverty guideline (48 states and DC). That is about $2,277 a month before tax. Families earning up to $35,516 (130%) may still get a place if the program has room.

What is the income limit for a family of 4?

$33,000 a year at 100% of the poverty guideline, or $42,900 at 130%. For each extra person, add $5,680 to the 100% limit. Alaska and Hawaii use higher figures.

Does my child qualify for Head Start if we get public assistance?

Yes. A child is eligible if the family gets public assistance, including TANF child-only payments, or would be able to get it if it were not for child care. Income does not matter in that case. The same is true if the child is homeless or in foster care.

Does SNAP (food stamps) make my child eligible for Head Start?

The rules name public assistance, homelessness and foster care, but they do not list each program, so ask your local program whether your benefit counts. Families on SNAP often have income under the limit anyway, so apply either way.

What age does a child have to be for Head Start?

Early Head Start is for babies and toddlers under 3 years old. Head Start Preschool is for children who are at least 3, or who turn 3 by the date your public school uses, and who are not yet old enough to have to be in school.

Which income counts for Head Start?

Gross income (money before tax) over the time period set in the rules. The program will tell you which months it needs and ask for proof, such as pay stubs or a tax return. If you cannot get proof, the program can use a written statement from your employer and work out the yearly amount from it.

How much does Head Start cost?

Nothing. Head Start and Early Head Start are free for eligible families.

Can I apply if my income is over the limit?

Yes. Children who meet none of the rules can fill up to 10% of a program's enrollment, and a program can take an extra 35% of families under 130% of the poverty line once it has served the families who qualify. Ask the local program – it decides who gets the places it has.

Does my child have to re-qualify every year?

No. A child who is taking part stays eligible through the end of the next program year, even if your income goes up. A program can still decide not to enroll a child again if it has a compelling reason.
