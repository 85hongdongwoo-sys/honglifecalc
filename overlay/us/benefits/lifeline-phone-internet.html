Check your numbers

Short answer: in 2026 you qualify for Lifeline if your household income is at or below 135% of the federal poverty guidelines – $21,546 a year for 1 person, $36,882 for 3 and $44,550 for 4 (48 states and DC) – or if someone in your home gets SNAP, Medicaid, SSI, Federal Public Housing Assistance or the Veterans Pension. The discount is up to $9.25 a month on internet or a bundle, up to $5.25 on phone-only service, and up to $34.25 on Tribal lands.

Hong Life is an independent guide, not a government website. Apply and confirm through the official Lifeline National Verifier.

How Lifeline works (in plain words)

Lifeline is a federal program run by the FCC (the Federal Communications Commission, which regulates phone and internet). It makes phone or internet service cheaper for people with low income. The money comes from a fund that phone companies pay into.

  • Step 1 – check you qualify. Either your household income is at or below 135% of the poverty guideline, or someone in your home gets a qualifying benefit.
  • Step 2 – apply. The National Verifier checks your proof. It is the official system that decides who qualifies.
  • Step 3 – choose a company. Pick a phone or internet company that offers Lifeline. It takes the discount off your bill.

"Household" means the people you live with and share money with. Two families at one address who keep their money apart can each count as a household.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use your gross income. Count income before tax, from everyone in the household.
  • Benefits are the fast route. If you get SNAP or Medicaid, show that letter. You don't need to prove your income then.
  • Only one per home. If someone else in your household already has Lifeline, you can't get a second one.
  • Don't miss recertification. Watch for the yearly notice. If you don't answer, the discount ends.
  • Never pay to apply. Applying for Lifeline is free.

Lifeline FAQ

What is the Lifeline income limit for 2026?

135% of the federal poverty guideline. For 1 person that is $21,546 a year, for 2 it is $29,214, for 3 it is $36,882 and for 4 it is $44,550 (48 states and DC). Add $7,668 for each extra person. Alaska and Hawaii have higher limits.

How much is the Lifeline discount?

Up to $9.25 a month off internet service or a phone + internet bundle, about $111 a year. Phone-only (voice) service gets up to $5.25 a month, about $63 a year. On Tribal lands the discount can be up to $34.25 a month, about $411 a year.

Which programs qualify me for Lifeline?

SNAP (food stamps), Medicaid, Supplemental Security Income (SSI), Federal Public Housing Assistance (such as Section 8) and the Veterans Pension and Survivors Benefit. If anyone in your household gets one of these, you qualify whatever your income. On Tribal lands some Tribal programs also count.

Can I get Lifeline for both my phone and my internet?

No. You get one Lifeline discount per household, on either a phone service or an internet service (or a bundle of both from one company).

Did the Affordable Connectivity Program come back?

No. The Affordable Connectivity Program (ACP) ended on 1 June 2024 because its funding ran out (full discounts stopped after April 2024). Lifeline is the federal program that is still running.

How do I apply for Lifeline?

Apply online through the National Verifier at lifelinesupport.org, by mail, or through a phone or internet company that offers Lifeline. You need proof of income or proof that you get a qualifying benefit. Then pick a participating company to get the discount. In Oregon and Texas, the state runs its own Lifeline process.

Do I need to renew Lifeline every year?

Yes. You must recertify every year to show you still qualify. If you don't, your discount stops.
