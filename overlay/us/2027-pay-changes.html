2027 Pay Changes: Minimum Wage & Federal Raise

What changes on January 1, 2027 — and what is still undecided.

On January 1, 2027 the minimum wage rises in states such as Connecticut ($17.48), Rhode Island ($17.00), Colorado ($15.71), Michigan ($15.00) and Virginia ($13.75). The federal minimum wage stays $7.25. For federal employees, the President's August 2026 plan proposes a 0% raise (pay freeze) for most GS employees in 2027; many law enforcement positions are slated for 3.8%. Congress has not made a final decision.

Frequently asked questions

What is the federal minimum wage in 2027?

Still $7.25 an hour. Congress has not changed it since 2009, so states with no own minimum (or a lower one) keep $7.25.

Which states raise the minimum wage on January 1, 2027?

Among them: Connecticut $17.48, Rhode Island $17.00, Colorado $15.71, Michigan $15.00 and Virginia $13.75. Other states (e.g. California, Arizona, Maine, New Jersey, Ohio, Minnesota) also adjust for 2027 — check your state labor department for the exact date.

Will federal employees get a raise in 2027?

Under the President's alternative pay plan sent in August 2026, most civilian GS employees would get 0% in 2027. Many law enforcement personnel are slated for 3.8%, and OPM is extending a 6.7% raise (3.8% plus a 2026 catch-up) to more positions. Nothing is final until the executive order, usually signed in December.

What are the 2027 tax brackets?

The IRS released them on November 5, 2026: 10% up to $12,800 (single), standard deduction $16,600 single / $33,200 married filing jointly. See the 2027 tax brackets calculator.

How much is $15.00 an hour per year?

$15.00 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $31,200 gross per year.
