How a bonus is taxed in 2026

A bonus is supplemental wages. It is taxed at the same brackets as your salary, but your employer withholds it differently. These amounts are usually taken out of the bonus check:

Flat 22% method vs. aggregate method

Flat (percentage) method. When the bonus is paid separately from your salary — or listed separately on the same pay stub — and income tax was withheld from your regular wages this year or last year, the employer may withhold a flat 22%, with no other percentage allowed. This is what most payroll systems do, and what this calculator assumes.

Aggregate method. If the bonus is lumped in with a regular paycheck without being identified, or the employer chooses this method, it adds the bonus to the regular pay for the period, works out withholding on the total as if it were one normal paycheck using your W-4, and subtracts what was already withheld from the regular pay. Because a large one-off paycheck looks like a very high annual salary, this method often withholds more than 22%.

Either way, withholding is only a prepayment. Your actual tax is settled on your return.

Over $1 million of supplemental pay

Example: you have already received $900,000 of bonuses this year and get another $200,000. The first $100,000 stays at 22% ($22,000) and the $100,000 above $1 million is withheld at 37% ($37,000) — $59,000 in federal withholding, whatever your W-4 says.

Ways people lower the tax on a bonus

  • Put part of it into a 401(k) or 403(b) if your plan allows deferrals from bonuses. The 2026 elective deferral limit is $24,500 (plus catch-up if you are 50 or older). The deferral is not subject to federal income tax now, but Social Security and Medicare still apply.
  • Fund an HSA through payroll if you have a high-deductible health plan.
  • Check your W-4 if you regularly owe money at tax time because of bonuses.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the bonus "pushes all your income into a higher bracket" — only the dollars above a bracket threshold are taxed at the higher rate.
  • Treating 22% as the final tax — it is only withholding.
  • Forgetting that state withholding can be a separate flat bonus rate set by the state.
  • Counting salary toward the $1 million test — the 37% rule looks only at supplemental pay.

FAQ

How much tax is taken out of a $10,000 bonus?

With the 22% flat federal rate, 6.2% Social Security, 1.45% Medicare and no state tax, about $2,965 is withheld and you receive about $7,035. In a 5% state another $500 is withheld, leaving about $6,535.

What is the federal bonus tax rate for 2026?

22% for supplemental wages up to $1 million in the calendar year and 37% on the part above $1 million (IRS Publication 15, 2026). The 22% rate has applied since 2018; in 2017 and earlier it was 25% (39.6% above $1 million).

Are bonuses taxed more than regular pay?

No. A bonus is ordinary income taxed at the same brackets as your salary. Only the withholding method differs, so the amount taken out of the bonus check can be higher or lower than the tax you finally owe on it.

Will I get bonus tax back?

If your top bracket is 10% or 12%, the 22% withholding is more than the tax on the bonus, so the difference usually comes back as part of your refund. At 22% it roughly matches; at 24% and above you may owe more when you file.

Does Social Security tax apply to my bonus?

Yes, 6.2% until your total wages for the year reach the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Medicare (1.45%) has no cap, and an extra 0.9% is withheld on wages above $200,000.
