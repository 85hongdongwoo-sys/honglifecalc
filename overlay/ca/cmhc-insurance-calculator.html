Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): CMHC insurance costs 2.80%–4.00% of the mortgage when you put down less than 20% (4.00% with 5–9.99% down, 4.50% with a non-traditional down payment), plus 0.20% for a 30-year amortization. On a $500,000 home with 10% down the premium is $13,950, added to the loan; Ontario charges 8% retail sales tax on it at closing, and Quebec and Saskatchewan also tax it.

How CMHC mortgage insurance is calculated

If your down payment is under 20%, Canadian lenders require mortgage default insurance from CMHC (or a private insurer such as Sagen or Canada Guaranty). The premium is a one-time percentage of the loan amount, set by your loan-to-value ratio (LTV), and is usually added to the mortgage.

Since December 15, 2024, insured mortgages are available on homes priced below $1.5 million, and all first-time buyers and all buyers of new builds can choose a 30-year amortization.

Formula: premium = (price − down payment) × premium rate; LTV = loan ÷ price. Add 0.20% to the rate for a 30-year amortization.

Sales tax on the premium

Premiums in Ontario, Quebec and Saskatchewan are subject to provincial sales tax, which is payable at closing and cannot be added to the loan amount. In Ontario it is the 8% retail sales tax on insurance premiums; check the current rate with Revenu Québec or Saskatchewan Finance if you buy there. The calculator shows the Ontario amount only.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Watch the tier edges: going from 9.9% to 10% down moves you from 4.00% to 3.10% — on a $500,000 home the premium falls from about $18,000 to $13,950.
  • Premium is on the loan, not the price, and it accrues interest because it is part of the mortgage.
  • Bring cash for the tax in Ontario (8% of the premium), Quebec and Saskatchewan, on top of the down payment and closing costs.
  • 30 years costs more: the 0.20% surcharge plus more interest over the longer term; it lowers the monthly payment, not the total cost.
  • Price of $1.5 million or more cannot be insured, so 20% down is mandatory.

FAQ

How much is CMHC insurance on a $500,000 house?

With the minimum 5% down ($25,000) the loan is $475,000 and the premium is 4.00%: $19,000 added to the mortgage. With 10% down ($50,000) it is 3.10% of $450,000 = $13,950.

How much is CMHC insurance on a $650,000 home with $50,000 down?

$24,000 (4.00% of the $600,000 loan, loan-to-value 92.3%), plus $1,920 Ontario retail sales tax (8%) paid in cash at closing. With $65,000 down (10%) the premium drops to $18,135 at 3.10%.

Can I avoid CMHC insurance?

Yes — put down at least 20% of the purchase price. Then the mortgage is conventional and no default insurance premium is required (lenders may still buy portfolio insurance at their own cost).

Is CMHC insurance paid upfront?

The premium is normally added to the mortgage balance and paid off with it. Only the provincial sales tax on the premium — charged in Ontario (8% retail sales tax), Quebec and Saskatchewan — must be paid at closing; it cannot be added to the loan.

What is the minimum down payment in Canada?

5% of the first $500,000 and 10% of the part between $500,000 and $1.5 million: $15,000 on $300,000, $40,000 on $650,000 and $75,000 on $1,000,000. The purchase price must be below $1.5 million for insurance; at $1.5 million or more you need 20%.

Who can get a 30-year insured mortgage?

Since December 15, 2024, all first-time homebuyers and all buyers of newly built homes can get insured mortgages with 30-year amortizations. CMHC adds a premium surcharge of 20 basis points (0.20%) for a 30-year amortization.
