How the GIC calculator works

Example first. You put $20,000 in a 3-year GIC at 3.5%, compounded once a year. After year 1 you have $20,700. After year 2, $21,424.50. After year 3, $22,174.36. Each year you earn 3.5% on a bigger amount.

  • Compound interest: value = deposit × (1 + rate ÷ n)n × years. Here n is how many times a year interest is added (1, 2 or 12).
  • Simple interest: value = deposit × (1 + rate × years). Interest is paid out, so it doesn't grow.
  • Tax: for a regular account, the calculator works out each year's interest and adds it to your income. The extra tax is your tax on the interest.
  • Marginal rate means the tax rate on your last dollar of income. GIC interest is taxed at this rate.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use your TFSA first if you have room. The same GIC keeps all its interest there.
  • Stay under $100,000 per category per bank to keep all of it CDIC-insured.
  • Check if it's cashable. Most GICs lock your money until the end. Cashable ones pay less.
  • Ladder your GICs. Split money across 1- to 5-year terms so some comes due every year.
  • Don't forget the tax each year. A 5-year GIC that pays at the end is still taxed every year.

GIC FAQ

How much interest will $10,000 earn in a GIC?

At 3% for 1 year it earns $300. At 3.5% for 5 years with yearly compounding it earns about $1,877. In a regular account you pay tax on that interest; in a TFSA you don't.

How much does $20,000 earn in a 3-year GIC at 3.5%?

$2,174.36 with yearly compounding, so you get back $22,174.36. In Ontario at $70,000 income, about $645 of that goes to tax, leaving about $1,530.

Is GIC interest taxable in Canada?

Yes, in a regular (non-registered) account. It is taxed as income at your full marginal rate, every year as it builds up, even if the GIC pays only at maturity. In a TFSA it is tax-free. In an RRSP it is taxed only when you withdraw.

Are GICs insured by CDIC?

GICs and other term deposits at CDIC member banks are eligible, covered up to $100,000 of principal and interest per insured category at each bank, with no limit on the term. Deposits in one name, joint deposits, and TFSA, RRSP, RRIF, FHSA, RDSP, RESP and trust deposits are each a separate category, so you can be covered for more than $100,000 at one bank. Credit union GICs are covered by provincial insurers instead.

What is the difference between annual and monthly compounding?

Compounding means you earn interest on your interest. Monthly adds interest 12 times a year, so it earns a little more. $25,000 at 3% for 2 years earns $1,543.93 monthly, versus $1,500 with simple interest paid out each year.

Why did GIC rates change in 2026?

GIC rates follow the Bank of Canada. On 28 October 2026 it raised its policy rate by 25 basis points (a quarter of a percentage point) to 2.5%, from 2.25%. The next decision is on 9 December 2026. Banks set their own GIC rates, so compare offers.
