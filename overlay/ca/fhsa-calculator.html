Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): you can put $8,000 a year into an FHSA, up to $40,000 for life, and up to $8,000 of unused room carries forward. An $8,000 contribution at $70,000 income saves about $2,372 in tax in Ontario ($2,440 in Alberta, $2,889 in Quebec), and money taken out for a qualifying first home is tax-free.

FHSA rules and 2026 figures

An FHSA (First Home Savings Account) is a registered account for buying your first home in Canada. Money going in lowers your tax. Money coming out for a first home is tax-free.

How the FHSA calculator works

Example first. You earn $70,000 in Ontario and put in $8,000. Your taxable income drops to $62,000. All of that $8,000 was taxed at 20.5% federal (the rate above $58,523) and 9.15% Ontario (the rate above $53,891). So you save $8,000 × 20.5% = $1,640 plus $8,000 × 9.15% = $732. Altogether about $2,372.

  • Balance: each year the calculator adds your contribution, then grows the whole balance by your return. It stops adding once you hit $40,000.
  • Tax refund: tax on your income − tax on (income − contribution), each year.
  • Real cost: what you put in − all the refunds.
  • Marginal rate means the tax rate on your last dollar of income. It is the rate your deduction saves.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Open the account early. Room only starts the year you open your first FHSA. Opening one with $0 still starts the clock on your $8,000.
  • Don't over-contribute. Going over your room costs you an extra tax for every month the extra money sits in the account. Check your room in CRA My Account first.
  • Save the deduction for a higher-income year if you expect a big raise soon.
  • Match the investment to your timeline. If you buy in 1–2 years, a GIC or savings account is safer than stocks.
  • Get a written agreement first. A tax-free withdrawal needs a written agreement to buy or build the home, and you have to intend to live in it. Ask your bank for the withdrawal form and the exact dates that apply to you.

FHSA FAQ

How much tax refund do I get for an FHSA contribution?

It depends on your income and province. In 2026, $8,000 at $70,000 of income saves about $2,372 in Ontario, $2,440 in Alberta, $2,256 in B.C. and $2,889 in Quebec. At $45,000 in Ontario it is about $1,614.

How much can I put in an FHSA in 2026?

$8,000 a year, up to $40,000 for life. If you opened your account last year and didn't use all your room, up to $8,000 carries forward. So the most you can add in one year is $16,000.

What happens to my FHSA if I don't buy a house?

You can move the money to your RRSP or RRIF with no tax, and it doesn't use your RRSP room. You must do this before the account has to close: 31 December of the 15th year after you opened it, or of the year you turn 71, whichever comes first. If you just withdraw it as cash, it is taxed as income.

Can I use the FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan together?

Yes. For the same qualifying home you can take your FHSA money out tax-free and also borrow from your RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan. The HBP money must be paid back to your RRSP; the FHSA money does not.

Who counts as a first-time home buyer for the FHSA?

You count if, this calendar year or in the previous 4 calendar years, you did not live as your main home in a home that you, or your spouse or common-law partner, owned or jointly owned. You also need to be a Canadian resident, 18 or older (19 in provinces where that is the age of majority), and 71 or younger on 31 December of the year you open it.

Should I use an FHSA or a TFSA for a down payment?

If you qualify, the FHSA is usually better. Money going in cuts your tax, like an RRSP, and money coming out for a home is tax-free, like a TFSA. A TFSA gives no deduction, but you can take money out for anything.

Do I have to claim my FHSA deduction in the same year?

No. You can carry the deduction forward and claim it in a later year, for example when your income, and so your tax rate, is higher.
