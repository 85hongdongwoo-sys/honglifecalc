How capital gains are taxed in Canada (2026)

A capital gain is the amount you sell an investment or property for, minus its adjusted cost base (what you paid plus purchase costs) and selling expenses. Only 50% of the gain — the taxable capital gain — is added to your income for the year and taxed at your marginal rate. This calculator adds that half to your other income, applies the 2026 federal and Ontario brackets, the Ontario surtax and Ontario Health Premium, and shows how much of the gain goes in tax.

Capital gains tax = (your tax on other income + 50% of the gain) − (your tax on other income alone).

Ways to reduce capital gains tax

  • Use registered accounts (TFSA, RRSP, FHSA) for investments you expect to grow.
  • Tax-loss selling: sell losing positions before year-end to offset gains, watching the superficial loss rule (no buying back the same property within 30 days before or after).
  • Spread large gains over more than one tax year where possible, or claim a capital gains reserve when you receive sale proceeds over several years (generally up to 5 years).
  • Track your adjusted cost base including reinvested distributions and commissions — an understated ACB means paying tax twice.
  • Donating publicly listed securities in kind to a registered charity eliminates the tax on the gain.

Common mistakes

  • Applying your marginal rate to the whole gain instead of half of it.
  • Forgetting that RRSP withdrawals are fully taxable, even if the growth came from gains.
  • Not reporting the sale of a principal residence (it's required even when exempt).

FAQ

What is the capital gains inclusion rate in 2026?

50%. Only half of a capital gain is added to your taxable income. The increase to two-thirds announced in 2024 was cancelled in March 2025, so it does not apply in 2026.

How much tax on a $20,000 capital gain in Ontario?

With $80,000 of other employment income, $10,000 (half the gain) is taxed at a combined federal and Ontario rate of 29.65%, which is $2,965 — an effective 14.8% of the gain. You keep about $17,035.

What is the highest capital gains tax rate in Ontario?

About 26.76% of the gain: the top combined marginal rate of 53.53% applied to the 50% taxable portion. It applies to income above $258,482 in 2026.

Do I pay capital gains tax on my house?

Usually not. A gain on a property that was your principal residence for every year you owned it is generally exempt under the principal residence exemption, but you must still report the sale on Schedule 3 of your return.

Can capital losses reduce my tax?

Yes. Capital losses offset capital gains in the same year. Unused net capital losses can be carried back three years or forward indefinitely, but only against capital gains.
