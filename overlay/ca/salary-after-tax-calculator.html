How your Canadian take-home pay is calculated (2026)

Your employer deducts four things from each paycheque: federal income tax, provincial or territorial income tax, Canada Pension Plan contributions (Quebec Pension Plan in Quebec) and Employment Insurance premiums (plus QPIP in Quebec). This calculator applies the 2026 brackets, basic personal amounts and contribution rates to your salary, then divides the net pay by your pay frequency.

Worked example: $75,000 in Ontario

  • Federal tax ≈ $8,259 (14% and 20.5% brackets, less the basic personal amount and CPP/EI credits).
  • Ontario tax ≈ $4,446 (5.05% and 9.15% brackets, less Ontario credits, plus the Ontario Health Premium).
  • CPP: (74,600 − 3,500) × 5.95% = $4,230.45, plus CPP2 of 4% on $400 = $16.
  • EI: $68,900 × 1.63% = $1,123.07 (the maximum).
  • Net ≈ $56,926 a year, $4,744 a month, $2,189 biweekly.

Why your paycheque can differ

  • CPP and EI stop for the year once you reach the maximums, so December cheques for higher earners are often bigger.
  • Your TD1 forms (federal and provincial) change the credits used for withholding.
  • Taxable benefits, bonuses and pre-tax deductions (RRSP through payroll, pension plans) change the tax.
  • The final tax is settled on your T1 return; a refund or balance owing makes up the difference.

FAQ

How much is $75,000 after tax in Ontario in 2026?

About $56,926 a year: $8,259 federal tax, $4,446 Ontario tax, CPP and CPP2 contributions and $1,123 EI come off. That is about $4,744 a month or $2,189 every two weeks.

How much is $100,000 after tax in Canada?

In 2026 about $74,206 in Ontario, $75,373 in British Columbia, $74,459 in Alberta and $69,585 in Quebec — roughly $5,800 to $6,280 a month.

What are the 2026 federal tax brackets?

14% on the first $58,523 of taxable income, 20.5% up to $117,045, 26% up to $181,440, 29% up to $258,482 and 33% above. The 14% lowest rate replaced 15% from July 2025.

How much CPP and EI will I pay in 2026?

CPP: 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 (up to $4,230.45), plus CPP2 of 4% on earnings from $74,600 to $85,000 (up to $416). EI: 1.63% of insurable earnings up to $68,900 (up to $1,123.07). In Quebec QPP is 6.30% (up to $4,479.30), EI 1.30% and QPIP 0.43%.

Why is take-home pay lower in Quebec?

Quebec residents file a separate provincial return with higher provincial rates, and also pay QPP and QPIP. They receive a 16.5% abatement of federal tax, which is why federal tax is lower in the table, but the provincial tax more than offsets it.
