Your numbers

Short answer: in 2026 a self-employed Canadian pays income tax plus both halves of CPP: 11.9% on net earnings between $3,500 and $74,600 (up to $8,460.90) and 8% CPP2 up to $85,000 (up to $832). On $80,000 of profit in Ontario that is about $22,126 in total, or roughly 28% to set aside.

How self-employed tax works (in plain words)

Example first. You earn $80,000 profit in Ontario. CPP is 11.9% of your earnings between $3,500 and $74,600: $71,100 × 11.9% = $8,461. Then 8% CPP2 on $5,400 = $432. Part of that CPP is taken off your income before tax, and the rest gives you a tax credit. Income tax comes to $13,234.

  • Net income means what you earned minus business expenses. Tax and CPP are worked out on this, not on your sales.
  • CPP (Canada Pension Plan) builds your future pension. An employee pays half and the employer pays half. When you work for yourself, you pay both halves.
  • Part of your CPP is deducted from your income (line 22200): the employer half, plus the enhanced parts and CPP2. For the employee base part (4.95%) you get a tax credit instead (line 31000). Schedule 8 works this out.
  • Instalments are tax payments in advance, four times a year, so you don't owe a big amount in spring.

Quebec is different: you pay QPP and QPIP and file a separate Quebec return, so this calculator leaves Quebec out.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Set money aside every month. Put the "set aside each month" amount into a separate savings account.
  • Keep receipts. Business expenses lower your net income, and so lower both tax and CPP.
  • Don't forget CPP. At lower incomes it can be bigger than the income tax.
  • Pay by April 30. The June 15 filing date does not delay the payment date.
  • Watch the $30,000 GST/HST line. Once you pass it, you must register and charge GST/HST.

Self-employed tax FAQ

How much tax does a self-employed person pay on $80,000 in Ontario?

About $22,126 in 2026: $8,893 of CPP (both halves plus CPP2) and $13,234 of income tax. That is 27.7% of the $80,000, so you keep about $57,874. Setting aside about $1,844 a month covers it.

What percentage should I set aside for taxes when self-employed in Canada?

Many people need 20–30% of their profit. In our examples it is 19.7% on $30,000 in Nova Scotia, 22.4% on $50,000 in B.C. and 28.5% on $120,000 in Alberta. Use the calculator with your own province.

How much CPP does a self-employed person pay in 2026?

11.9% of net self-employment income between $3,500 and $74,600 – up to $8,460.90. Above that you pay 8% CPP2 on income up to $85,000, up to $832 more. You pay both the employee and the employer part.

Do self-employed people have to pay tax quarterly in Canada?

Only if the CRA asks. You must pay instalments when your net tax owing is more than $3,000 in 2026 and in either 2025 or 2024. The due dates are March 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15.

When is the tax deadline for self-employed people?

For 2026 income you have until June 15, 2027 to file your return. But any balance owing must still be paid by April 30, 2027, or interest starts.

Do self-employed people pay EI?

Not by default. You can sign up to pay EI premiums to get special benefits: maternity, parental, sickness, compassionate care and family caregiver benefits. In 2026 the premium is $1.63 for every $100 you earn, up to $1,123.07 a year. The program is for these special benefits, not job-loss (regular) EI.

When do I need to register for GST/HST?

When your taxable sales go over $30,000 in a single calendar quarter or over the last four calendar quarters in a row. Sales of associated businesses count too. Below that you are a small supplier and registering is optional.
