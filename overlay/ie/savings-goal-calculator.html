Your numbers

Quick answer (2026): monthly saving = what you still need ÷ months, a little less with interest. €10,000 in 2 years takes about €417 a month (€410 at 2.5% after 33% DIRT). Bank interest loses 33% DIRT; State Savings are tax-free, and deposits are protected up to €100,000 per bank.

How the calculator works

Example first. You want €18,000 in 3 years. You already have €1,800, and your bank pays 2.5% a year. After 33% DIRT that is about 1.68%. Saving €437 a month gets you there. If the interest were tax-free, you would need about €430 a month.

  • DIRT (Deposit Interest Retention Tax) is the tax on bank interest. The calculator takes 33% off the rate if you choose "Yes".
  • Monthly rate = yearly rate ÷ 12.
  • Compound interest means you also earn interest on your interest. The calculator adds it every month.
  • Monthly saving = (goal − what your savings grow to) × i ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1). Here i is the monthly rate and n is the number of months.

With no interest it is simply (goal − already saved) ÷ months.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Pay yourself first. Set up a standing order for payday.
  • Compare after-tax rates. 2.5% from a bank is about 1.68% after DIRT. A tax-free State Savings product with a lower headline rate can be better.
  • Check lock-in rules. Fixed-term accounts and State Savings pay less if you cash in early.
  • Don't put over €100,000 in one bank if you want it all covered by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme.
  • Re-check your rate after ECB rate changes. Banks often follow.

Savings goal FAQ

How much do I need to save each month in Ireland?

Divide what you still need by the number of months, then take off a little for interest. For €10,000 in 2 years from zero: €10,000 ÷ 24 = €417 a month with no interest, or €410 a month at 2.5% after 33% DIRT.

How much is DIRT on savings in Ireland?

DIRT is 33% of the interest. The bank takes it off before paying you. So 2.5% interest is about 1.68% after tax, and €300 of interest leaves you €201.

Are State Savings tax-free?

Yes. State Savings bonds and certificates from An Post and the NTMA are tax-free and 100% State-protected. For new issues from 30 August 2026, the 3-year bond pays 6% in total (1.96% AER) and the 5-year certificate 12% (2.29% AER). You must keep the money in for the full term to get the full return.

Is my money safe in an Irish bank?

Deposits are protected up to €100,000 per person, per bank, by the Deposit Guarantee Scheme run by the Central Bank of Ireland. A joint account counts €100,000 for each holder.

How much should I keep in an emergency fund?

The Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC) says to aim for at least three to six months' income, and that three months' income is a good starting point. Build that before saving for other goals.

Does inflation matter for my savings goal?

Yes. Prices rose 4.3% in the year to October 2026 (CSO). If your goal is a price that rises, such as a car or a house deposit, aim a little higher than today's price.
